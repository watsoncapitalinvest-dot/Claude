The Draft Machine

Five straight classes say the same thing: the Powers of Pain buy other people’s first-round picks — and keep hitting on them.
Skirt Chasers Fantasy League · Graded on the Value Board

Every dynasty has a team that hoards picks and squanders them. The Powers of Pain are the opposite, and the draft record is the proof. Across five classes on file, the most valuable rookies to land on their roster almost never came from their own slot — they came in on capital pried loose from half the league. And when the card got turned in, they hit.

Start with the headliners. In 2022 the Powers of Pain drafted Breece Hall at 1.01 — a pick acquired from Lamb Chops — and added Garrett Wilson at 1.06 on a pick out of Pork Chop Express. Two top-six rookies in one night, neither on their own pick, both of whom peaked as top-tier dynasty assets. That is not luck. That is a front office.

The pattern isn’t “draft well.” It’s buy the ammunition first, then draft well.

The one that got away, and the one that paid off

It hasn’t all been clean. Treylon Burks, the 1.07 in 2022, is the blemish — a first-round rookie who never returned first-round value and has since fallen off the board entirely. And the 2023 back-to-back is the franchise in miniature: with picks 1.03 and 1.04 in hand, they took Bryce Young and then CJ Stroud. One stalled. The other became a franchise quarterback and the single most valuable rookie the Powers of Pain have ever drafted. Swing on two quarterbacks in the same round and you only need one to hit — and the one they got via the Killer Klowns hit as hard as anyone in the league.

The rebuild they’re running now

The 2025 class shows the machine still humming: Omarion Hampton at 1.04 (via the Guido Haters), Quinshon Judkins at 1.15, and a genuine late-round steal in Tyler Shough at 2.29 — more value than the pick cost. Not every dart landed — Matthew Golden at 1.10 and Kaleb Johnson have yet to pay their freight — but the shape is familiar: a stack of firsts, most of them bought, most of them turned into starters.

CJ Stroud. Breece Hall. Garrett Wilson. Brian Thomas Jr. Travis Etienne. Omarion Hampton. Count how many came in on someone else’s pick.

It is no accident that the same franchise sits near the top of the Trade Court’s value board. The Powers of Pain don’t win the draft on draft night. They win it in July, on the phone, collecting the picks nobody else valued highly enough to keep — and then they cash them.

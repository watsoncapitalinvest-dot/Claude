BASE VIEW FACILITIES - WHAT THE ENGINE ALLOWS

Read before drawing another one. All of it is read out of BaseView.cpp and
RuleBaseFacility, not assumed.


THE GRID

BaseView::GRID_SIZE is 32. Every grid square is 32x32 pixels and a facility
of size N by M is drawn as N*M SEPARATE 32x32 frames, blitted one per square.
There is no such thing as one big picture for a 2x2.

Frame order is the order BaseView walks them: left to right, then top to
bottom. For a 2x2 that is NW, NE, SW, SE.


TWO LAYERS, NOT ONE

A facility carries two sprite numbers and the engine draws both:

  spriteShape     the floor plate and outline, drawn FIRST
  spriteFacility  the facility's own graphic, drawn ON TOP

Vanilla shares the shapes between facilities - 1 is the plain square, 2 the
small one, 3 the cross used by the defences - and gives every facility its own
spriteFacility. So a new facility usually needs ONE new graphic, not two, and
should reuse a shape.

Under construction, the engine draws spriteShape + num + outline instead,
where outline is 3 for a 1x1 and sizeX*sizeY for anything bigger. So a 1x1
reserves 4 frames and a 2x2 reserves 8, whether or not you draw them all.


OUTER WALLS ONLY

Each frame of a multi-tile facility must draw its wall only on the sides that
face OUT of the building. Draw all four on every frame and the player sees a
wall running through the middle of his own hangar. The first attempt here did
exactly that and it is obvious the moment the frames are assembled.


THE PALETTE

The base view runs on PAL_BASESCAPE, which is palette 1 in GEODATA/PALETTES.DAT
- offset 774, since palOffset(n) is n * 774 and the order in Mod.cpp is
GEOSCAPE, BASESCAPE, GRAPHS, UFOPAEDIA, BATTLEPEDIA.

These four tiles use 13 entries and every one is an exact entry, so nothing is
quantised at load:

  81 84 87 90   concrete_mil, board-formed, lit from the top left
  66 70 75      steel_plate
  160 163 167   Joe olive - bedding, crates, racked kit
  17            hazard amber
  34            signal red, stencils only
  93            open shaft and shadow
  0             transparent, so the shape plate shows through

Index 0 must stay transparent or the facility covers its own floor plate.


MATERIALS

Each tile is built only from materials that already exist in BUILDINGS_BRIEF:
concrete_mil, steel_plate, corrugated, plaster_white, door_rollup, door_steel,
sandbag, window_slit. That is deliberate. A base defence renders the base as a
battlescape map, so a facility drawn from materials we do not have is a
facility that cannot be walked through later.


THE FOUR DRAWN

  facility_access_lift.png   1x1  concrete shaft, steel platform, hazard bands
  facility_barracks.png      1x1  plaster interior, six bunks, olive bedding
  facility_armoury.png       1x1  racking on three walls, crates, roll-up door
  facility_hangar_0..3.png   2x2  corrugated roof, concrete apron, roll-up door

The hangar's middle is kept plain on purpose. The engine draws the craft
sprite over the centre of the facility and anything busy underneath fights it.

Not wired. These are art only - no ruleset entry yet, because a new facility

THE ISOMETRIC PROJECTION - EVERY NUMBER DERIVED

For anything drawn as a PLACE rather than as a base-view icon. All of it is
read out of Camera::convertMapToScreen, with spriteWidth 32 and spriteHeight
40, rather than chosen by eye:

    screenX = x*(W/2) - y*(W/2)
    screenY = x*(W/4) + y*(W/4) - z*((H + W/4)/2)

which gives, at 1x:

    +1 tile in x        +16, +8 px
    +1 tile in y        -16, +8 px
    +1 level up           0, -24 px
    floor diamond       32 x 16, so 2:1, so 26.565 degrees
    storey height       24 px against a 32 px tile - 3 to 4

That last ratio is the one usually drawn wrong. A storey is three quarters of
a tile width, not a whole one and not half.

And the size of a facility is not a matter of taste either. The base view is
6x6 squares, and BattlescapeGenerator::generateBaseMap puts ONE map block on
each square. The vanilla XBASE blocks are 10x10, so:

    a 1x1 facility      10 x 10 tiles
    a 2x2 facility      20 x 20 tiles, four times the floor

Rendered with build/render_facilities_iso.py, at native 1x so nothing is
resampled. The result is art_briefs/facilities_isometric.png.

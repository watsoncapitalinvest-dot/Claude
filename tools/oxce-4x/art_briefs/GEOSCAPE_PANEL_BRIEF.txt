GEOSCAPE SIDE PANEL - HIGH RESOLUTION REPLACEMENT

WHAT THIS IS

One image. It is the control panel that runs down the right-hand edge of the
world-map screen in a military strategy game. It is a metal bezel with recessed
slots cut into it: six command buttons at the top, a clock readout in the middle,
six time-speed buttons below that, and a globe-rotation control cluster at the
bottom.

The art being replaced is from 1994 and is 64 pixels wide. It is now being blown
up six times and looks like it. This is a straight redraw at full size, same
layout, same slot positions, nothing moved.


CANVAS

1920 x 1200 pixels, RGBA, transparent background.

Only the right-hand strip is drawn. Everything left of x=1536 stays fully
transparent - that area is the world map and must not be covered.

The panel strip is: x from 1536 to 1920, y from 0 to 1200.


DRAW NO TEXT, NO NUMBERS, NO ICONS WITH MEANING

This is the single most important instruction.

The game draws every label, every digit and every readout itself, on top of this
image, at run time. If you draw the word INTERCEPT or the numbers 12:00, the
game's own text lands on top of yours and both become unreadable.

Draw only the empty bezel: metal, slots, rivets, bevels, shadow, wear. Every slot
listed below must be left as an empty recess.


THE SLOTS - EXACT RECTANGLES

These are in pixels on the 1920 x 1200 canvas. Each one must be a recessed,
EMPTY slot. Keep the interior of each slot flat and dark so that bright text
drawn on top of it stays readable. No texture, no highlights and no detail
inside a slot's interior - put the detail in the surrounding metal instead.

STYLE

Military field equipment, late cold war. A rugged painted-steel console that
could be bolted inside a command vehicle.

    - Olive drab and gunmetal for the body of the panel.
    - Slot interiors near-black, very slightly warm.
    - Bevels catching a cool light from the upper left.
    - Sparing amber or red for indicator details in the metal between slots.
    - Honest wear: chipped paint at the edges, faint scuffing around the
      controls that get used, a little grime in the recesses. Not rusted,
      not destroyed.

It must read as one continuous machined panel with slots cut into it, not as a
row of separate floating buttons.

Keep the whole image legible at a glance. Detail should sit in the metal, never
across a slot.


DO NOT INCLUDE

    - Any lettering, numerals, or symbols that look like language.
    - Any real-world military insignia, unit patches, flags or national markings.
    - Any logo, emblem or trade dress belonging to an existing toy, film or
      game franchise.
    - Screws or rivets inside the slot rectangles.
    - A drop shadow or glow outside x=1536, which must stay transparent.


DELIVER

One PNG, 1920 x 1200, RGBA, transparent left of x=1536.

A second version with the panel strip on a transparent background and no
surrounding frame at all is not needed - the single file above is the deliverable.


=============================================================================
CORRECTIONS AFTER THE FIRST ATTEMPT - 11 Nov
=============================================================================

The look is right. Olive machined panel, recessed slots, knurled dials, the
directional cluster, and no lettering anywhere - all correct, keep every bit of
it. What follows is geometry only. Do not restyle.

Two files came back. Take the one with real transparency as the starting point.

1. CANVAS SIZE
   It came back 1586 x 992. It must be exactly 1920 x 1200. The proportions
   were right, so this is a re-render at the correct size, not a redesign.

2. THE PANEL IS TOO WIDE
   Measured, it occupies the rightmost 454 pixels. It must occupy exactly the
   rightmost 384: from x=1536 to x=1920. It is currently overhanging about 70
   pixels into the world map.

3. LEFT OF x=1536 MUST BE EMPTY - ALPHA EXACTLY ZERO
   There is a faint wash across the left side: 4.5 per cent of the pixels
   sampled there carry some alpha. It is nearly invisible on a dark background
   and it will be very visible over the world map. Nothing at all left of 1536,
   no glow, no haze, no soft shadow falling off the panel's left edge.

4. A PAINTED CHECKERBOARD IS NOT TRANSPARENCY
   The second file is RGB with no alpha channel at all, and the grey chequer
   pattern is painted into the pixels. Delivered like that it would paint an
   opaque grey chequer over three quarters of the world map. The file needs a
   real alpha channel.

5. THE SLOTS DRIFT DOWN THE PANEL
   This is the one that matters. The top six are close, within about 16 pixels.
   From the clock downward every slot sits LOW, and it accumulates:

       clock window        41 px low
       date strips         32 to 42 px low
       speed buttons       33, 36 and 39 px low

   The bottom speed buttons are out by half their own height. The game draws its
   text at fixed positions, so text meant for a slot lands on the metal between
   two slots.

   The rectangles in this brief are not a guide to be spaced evenly by eye. They
   are exact. Place each slot on the numbers given and check the last one before
   delivering: the bottom speed button is at y=840 and is 78 tall, so its lower
   edge is y=918, and the control cluster starts below that.

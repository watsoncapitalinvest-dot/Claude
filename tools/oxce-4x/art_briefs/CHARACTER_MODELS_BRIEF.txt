CHARACTER MODELS - 3D BODIES FOR THE BATTLEFIELD AND THE EQUIPMENT SCREEN

You are making one 3D model per character. Each model is used for two things:
the small figure that walks, aims, fires and falls on the battlefield, seen
from eight directions, and the full-length figure on the equipment screen.
Both are produced from your model, so the model is the only thing to deliver.

The character pictures already made (the c_*.png and j_*.png files) are the
reference for each character's outfit, colours and gear. Match them.


WHAT TO DELIVER, PER CHARACTER
------------------------------
    File:      <same name as the picture>.glb, e.g. c_baroness.glb, j_hawk.glb
               (.fbx is accepted if .glb is not possible)
    One file, one character, one mesh with its textures embedded.


SCALE, POSITION, POSE
---------------------
    Units:     metres. Real size.
    Height:    standard man 1.80 m, heavy build 1.88 m, woman 1.70 m,
               measured to the top of the head, not the top of a helmet crest.
    Up:        +Y.
    Facing:    the character looks along +Z.
    Feet:      soles exactly on Y = 0. Origin on the ground between the feet.
    Pose:      A-pose. Standing straight, legs slightly apart, arms straight
               and angled about 45 degrees down from the shoulders, palms
               facing the thighs, fingers together and straight.
    Head:      about one seventh of total height. Do not enlarge it.
    Hands:     EMPTY. No rifle, pistol, knife or anything else held.
               Holstered, sheathed and slung gear is fine.


SKELETON
--------
If you can rig the model: use the Mixamo skeleton with the Mixamo bone names
unchanged (mixamorig:Hips, mixamorig:Spine, and so on). Every character must
use this same skeleton so that one set of animations drives all of them.

If you cannot rig: deliver the model unrigged, in the A-pose above. It will be
rigged afterwards. Do not deliver a rig with different bone names - that is
worse than no rig.

Do not include any animation.


GEOMETRY
--------
    Triangles:   between 8,000 and 30,000. The battlefield figure is about 40
                 pixels tall, so detail finer than a belt buckle is never seen.
    Closed:      no holes, no floating pieces, no inside-out faces.
    Separate:    hair, capes, coats and webbing may be separate parts of the
                 same file, but nothing may intersect the body.
    Capes:       a cape or long coat hangs straight down behind the figure and
                 stops above the back of the knees, clear of the legs.


TEXTURES AND COLOUR
-------------------
    One colour texture, 2048 x 2048, embedded in the file.
    FLAT COLOUR ONLY. No shadows, highlights, dirt shading or ambient
    occlusion painted into the texture - light is added when the figures are
    rendered, and painted-in shadow ends up on the wrong side in six of the
    eight directions.
    No transparency. No glowing materials, except a visor or eyepiece the
    character really has.
    Strong, separate blocks of colour. Each character has to be told apart
    from the others at 40 pixels tall, so the main colour, the trim colour and
    the headgear must contrast clearly with each other. Avoid all-over olive or
    all-over black unless the character really is dressed that way - and if
    he is, make the gear on him a clearly different shade.


NOTHING BRANDED
---------------
No logos, emblems, insignia, lettering or badges anywhere on the model or in
the texture. Leave a plain patch where a character wears an emblem; emblems
are added inside the game.


IF YOU DO NOT KNOW A CHARACTER
------------------------------
If you are not sure what a character wears, say so and skip him. Do not
invent an outfit.


FIRST
-----
Make ONE model first - j_snake_eyes.glb, Snake Eyes - and stop. It is checked
in the game before any others are made. Snake Eyes is in black: give his
webbing, pouches and visor clearly different shades of dark grey and blue-
black so his shape reads against a dark battlefield.

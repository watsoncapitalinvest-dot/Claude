TURNING FLAT WEAPON ARTWORK INTO GAME SPRITES
=============================================

You already have the weapon drawn. This is only about what shape the files have
to be before the game can use them. Nothing here is about style.

THE SHORT VERSION - ENOUGH ON ITS OWN
One picture per weapon is enough: the weapon side-on, barrel or point to the
LEFT, grip down, on flat magenta (FF00FF), any size, nothing else in the
picture. example_m4_accepted.png is one that went straight into the game. The
held and dropped versions are made from it. The three deliverables below are
only needed if you want to draw the held and dropped views yourself.

This covers EVERY weapon, not only firearms: rifles, pistols, submachine guns
and launchers, and equally knives, machetes, grenades, satchel charges, mines
and anything else a soldier carries and uses. All of them need the same three
files at the same three sizes. Where a rule below differs for a thrown or a
hand-to-hand weapon it says so.

Each weapon needs THREE separate deliverables. They are not crops of one
picture: the gun is drawn from a different angle in each.


DELIVERABLE 1 - INVENTORY PICTURE
---------------------------------
    File:    big_<weapon>.png
    Size:    128 x 192 pixels. Exactly.
    Angle:   Flat side-on. Barrel pointing LEFT, grip down.
    Fill:    The gun should fill the frame with about 6 pixels of clear space
             around it. Do not centre a small gun in a large empty frame.

This is what the player sees in the inventory screen, and it is the only one of
the three where the weapon is drawn large and readable. Put the detail here.

The game shows a rifle standing UPRIGHT in the inventory, muzzle up. Do NOT
rotate it yourself. Draw it side-on with the barrel left; it is turned upright
and fitted to the slot when it goes into the game. Any size is accepted for
this picture as long as it is side-on, barrel left, on flat magenta (FF00FF).


DELIVERABLE 2 - DROPPED ON THE GROUND
-------------------------------------
    File:    floor_<weapon>.png
    Size:    128 x 160 pixels. Exactly.
    Angle:   Seen from above and to the side, lying flat on the ground, as if
             you were standing over it looking down at roughly 60 degrees.
    Place:   Centred in the frame, barrel running from lower-left to upper-right.

This is the weapon lying on the floor after someone drops it. It is small on
screen - no more than about half the frame. Do not draw a shadow; the game
draws the floor underneath.


DELIVERABLE 3 - HELD IN THE HANDS (the one that goes wrong)
-----------------------------------------------------------
    File:    hold_<weapon>.png
    Size:    1024 x 160 pixels. Exactly. ONE strip.
    Layout:  8 frames side by side, each 128 wide and 160 tall:
                 frame 1: x 0      frame 5: x 512
                 frame 2: x 128    frame 6: x 640
                 frame 3: x 256    frame 7: x 768
                 frame 4: x 384    frame 8: x 896

The 8 frames are the same weapon seen as the soldier turns on the spot, one
frame per facing, going CLOCKWISE:

    frame 1  soldier facing away from you (you see his back)
    frame 2  away and to the right
    frame 3  to the right, full profile
    frame 4  towards you and to the right
    frame 5  facing you (you see his front)
    frame 6  towards you and to the left
    frame 7  to the left, full profile
    frame 8  away and to the left

THE WEAPON MUST NOT BE CENTRED IN THESE FRAMES. Each frame is laid directly on
top of the soldier's own frame at the same position, so the gun has to be drawn
exactly where his hands are, and nowhere else. A weapon centred in the frame
appears floating in front of the soldier's chest.

Use the SOLDIER UNDERLAY SHEET supplied with this brief:

    SOLDIER_UNDERLAY_1024x160.png

It is the eight soldier poses in flat grey, each one sitting in its own 128 x
160 frame exactly where the game puts it. Open it, draw the weapon on a layer
ABOVE it lined up with the hands in each pose, then delete the underlay layer
and send only the weapon. This is the single most important instruction here
and it is the thing that cannot be fixed afterwards.

A GRENADE or other thrown item does not need eight frames of its own - it is
held the same way whichever way the soldier faces. Draw it in the hand in all
eight, and do not rotate it.

A KNIFE or machete is held blade-forward in the hand, the same as a pistol.

In frames where the soldier's own body would hide the weapon - typically when
he is facing away - draw only the part that would still be visible.


BACKGROUND - ALL THREE FILES
----------------------------
Flat pure magenta, hex FF00FF, everywhere that is not weapon.

Do not use transparency. Do not use a chequerboard. Do not anti-alias the
weapon's outline against the magenta - a semi-magenta edge pixel becomes a pink
fringe around the gun in the game. The outline must be a hard edge: a pixel is
either weapon or it is magenta.

If your tool only exports transparency, export it that way and say so, but do
not paint a grey chequer pattern into the picture.


SIZE RELATIVE TO EACH OTHER
---------------------------
All weapons are drawn at the same scale as one another. A pistol must not fill
its frame the way a rifle does. As a guide, for the inventory picture:

    combat knife, bayonet                 about  50 x  25 pixels of weapon
    machete, entrenching tool             about  75 x  35
    hand grenade                          about  40 x  50
    satchel charge, mine, demolition pack about  70 x  55
    pistol, revolver, machine pistol      about  60 x  45
    submachine gun                        about  85 x  55
    carbine, assault rifle                about 115 x  60
    battle rifle, sniper rifle            about 125 x  65
    light machine gun                     about 125 x  80
    rocket launcher, heavy weapon         about 128 x  90

If a weapon would come out larger than the frame, shrink it to fit - never crop
the barrel or the stock.

For a KNIFE or other hand-to-hand weapon the inventory picture is still side-on
with the point to the LEFT and the grip to the right, the same way a barrel
points left, so everything in the inventory reads in one direction.

A GRENADE has no long axis to point. Draw it upright in the inventory picture
with the lever and pin visible, and lying on its side for the ground picture.


LIGHT AND COLOUR
----------------
    - Light comes from the upper left in every frame of every file.
    - Keep it readable at small size: strong silhouette, few internal details.
      At normal zoom the held weapon is about 30 pixels across on screen.
    - Metal, polymer and wood should be clearly different in value, not just in
      hue, because much of the time the player sees these in shadow.


DO NOT INCLUDE
--------------
    - Any logo, emblem, trademark or trade dress from an existing toy, film or
      game franchise, on the weapon or anywhere in the frame.
    - Real manufacturer markings or model names stamped on the receiver.
    - Hands, arms, gloves or any part of a person. The soldier is drawn by the
      game; you are drawing the weapon only.
    - Muzzle flash, smoke, tracer or impact effects.
    - Drop shadows outside the weapon's own outline.


FILE NAMES
----------
Use the same short name in all three files, lower case, no spaces:

    big_m4.png      floor_m4.png      hold_m4.png
    big_uzi.png     floor_uzi.png     hold_uzi.png

Send PNG. One set per weapon.


CHECK BEFORE SENDING
--------------------
    1. Are the three files exactly 128x192, 128x160 and 1024x160?
    2. Is the held strip exactly 8 frames, and is the weapon sitting on the
       hands in every one of them rather than in the middle of the frame?
    3. Is every non-weapon pixel exactly FF00FF, with no soft edges?
    4. Would this weapon's inventory picture sit sensibly beside a pistol and a
       rocket launcher drawn at the same scale?

The Machines Redrew It, And It Still Holds Up

Schedule designed and built by The Machines, with the New World Order
Analysis by the SCFL NewsRoom

The card you saw last week is not the card you are playing. The Machines pulled it back down, tweaked the divisional slate so weeks 7, 8 and 9 run three division games in a row instead of two, and reposted the whole thing within a day of the first draft going up. Same rules. Different 112 games.

So this desk redid the boring thing, too. We pulled the new card down, laid it against twenty-one seasons of league history and against what every roster is currently worth, and checked it again — because a schedule that was correct in the first draft tells you nothing about whether the redraw still is.

It still is. All four rules hold, across all 112 games, on the second draft same as the first. The redraw was not a patch job: the whole slate moved. Division games no longer sit in weeks 2, 4, 7, 8, 10 and 13 — they sit in 2, 4, 7, 8, 9 and 12, with a genuine three-in-a-row stretch through the middle of the year. The division pairing flipped from last year’s alignment, same as always. And underneath it, the redraw changed which block of the season is actually doing the work of making this thing fair — which nobody had noticed the first time either.

There is still no luck in this schedule at all. Nothing was drawn. Nothing was randomised. Two men redrew 112 games by hand against the same four rules as before, and every one of them still checks out.

What follows is the new card, the machine behind it, what changed between the two drafts, and the twelve things worth knowing before week one — which is no longer the game you thought it was, either.

The Rules, As Filed — Again

The architect is unchanged: The Machines — four-time champion, keeper of this league’s institutional memory, and the man who has been sorting the SCFL calendar for longer than most of the current membership has been in it — with the New World Order alongside him, as always. The following day, under the heading “Tweaks,” he posted the revision:

Weeks 7-8-9. Three division games in a row. Division games are now weeks: 2, 4, 7, 8, 9 & 12. Middle of the season heated up now.

Followed, for the record, by the full rule set as it now stands:

Divisional play weeks: 2, 4, 7, 8, 9 & 12. Alternating Division (Spartan vs SS, 4 Horse vs BB) weeks: 1, 3, 6 & 13. Remaining same standing 2025 weeks: 5 & 10. Remaining — 2025 1st vs 3rd, 2nd vs 4th — weeks: 11 & 14.

The arithmetic that gets you to fourteen games has not moved: six inside your own division, home and away against all three rivals; four against every club in this year’s paired division; two against the clubs that finished where you finished, in the two divisions you are not paired with; and two against the clubs at the mirrored position — first with third, second with fourth.

Six plus four plus two plus two. Fourteen. What moved is only which weeks carry which slate — and, per the pairing note, which division is paired with which this year: Spartan with Spectre Syndicate, the Four Horsemen with Black and Blue — the alternation running the other way from 2025.

We Checked All Of It, Again

Every club still plays exactly three opponents twice, eight once, four not at all. The three you play twice are your division, in all sixteen cases, now spread across weeks 2, 4, 7, 8, 9 and 12 instead of the old 2, 4, 7, 8, 10 and 13. The four you play in full are your paired division, in all sixteen cases, and the pairing is the one filed above.

The last four games check out the same way they always have. For all sixteen clubs, two are against clubs that finished in the same slot of their own division in 2025, and two are against clubs at the mirrored slot — first with third, second with fourth — drawn from the two divisions you are not paired with. Sixteen for sixteen, both drafts.

One wrinkle in the seeding, buried deep enough that it took rebuilding the standings from the actual 2025 matchups to find: three of the four divisions are seeded by plain in-division regular-season record. Spectre Syndicate is not. Guido Haters finished the 2025 regular season 7–7 with more points scored than Powers of Pain, also 7–7 — but PoP is the club that got hot in the bracket and won the whole thing. Spectre’s number one seed for 2026 scheduling purposes is the champion, not the club that actually out-scored him in-division. The other three divisions use the boring method. This one crowns whoever won it all.

Where The Weeks Go, Now

The rules say what you play. The card says when, and the redraw moved almost everything.

The giveaway survives the rewrite, just shifted: week 8 is week 2 again. All eight fixtures, the same eight pairs of clubs, replayed. Week 9 repeats week 4. Week 12 repeats week 7. Those six weeks — not the old 2, 4, 7, 8, 10, 13 — are now the entire divisional slate, out and back, and for three straight weeks in the middle of the year, 7 through 9, there is nothing else on the calendar but division football.

What The Redraw Actually Changed

Here is the part that moved, and moved in a way nobody in the chat is going to notice on their own.

The pairing flipped, as filed: this year it is Spartan with Spectre Syndicate, the Four Horsemen with Black and Blue. Which means the cancellation effect the design relies on — a soft division getting a hard paired-four, a hard division getting a soft one — now lands on a different pair of divisions than it did in 2025. The Four Horsemen, the weakest division in the league at 1,072, gets the paired-four from Black and Blue, the strongest at 1,632. Its four clubs face a ‘four’ slice averaging 408, the hardest in the league — landing on the same clubs whose own division is the softest six-game slate there is. Advantage and penalty, same club, same season, exactly as before, just handed to a different division this year.

98%
How much of the season’s fairness gain is banked by week 10

And here is the actual finding, the one that survives the redraw: strength-of-schedule spread across the league has a standard deviation of 63.6 after the six division games alone. Add the four paired-division games and it collapses to 25.7 — a drop of 98 percent of the way to where the full 14-game card ends up. Add the last four placement games, and the number barely moves again: 25.0.

Under the old draft, the placement games looked like the ones doing the fairness work. Under this one, checked properly, they are not. The paired-division block is the whole mechanism. The placement rounds are worth having — see below — but on the numbers, they are close to decorative for the league-wide fairness question. That is the thing nobody noticed the first time, because nobody had reason to look at a version of the schedule that no longer exists.

One Wrinkle, Redrawn

The placement games are not pointless, even if they do not move the fairness needle much. There is a real effect buried in them, and the redraw changed its shape.

Pairing first with third and second with fourth is not a ladder, and on this draft it produces something cleaner than the old one did. Add up the four placement opponents for every club that finished first in its division, and the total comes to 5,964 points of roster value — the exact same total as every club that finished third. Every club that finished second draws 4,870, identical to every club that finished fourth. First and third face the same aggregate test. Second and fourth face the same, easier one.

That is a tidier result than the schedule produced last time, when winning your division bought you a harder finish than coming second in it. On this draft, winning buys you the same finishing slate as coming third — not a bonus, not a penalty, just company. It is worth stating plainly rather than dressing up: this is what the numbers say for these sixteen rosters on this specific card, not a law the design guarantees every year. Whether it holds again in 2027 depends on who is good.

Twelve Things Buried In The Redraw
  1. Week 8 is week 2 again — just not the week 8 you remember. All eight fixtures, the same eight pairs, replayed. Week 9 repeats week 4, week 12 repeats week 7. That is the whole divisional slate, out and back, moved earlier into the season by the redraw.
  2. Spectre Syndicate is seeded by a different rule than everyone else. Three divisions rank their 2026 seeding by plain 2025 regular-season record. Spectre ranks by final standing — which means Powers of Pain, 7–7 with the worse points-for of the division’s two co-leaders, sits above Guido Haters anyway, because PoP is the club that got hot in the bracket and won it all.
  3. The Bosom Bowl rematch is also the single biggest game on paper — and it moved to week 5. Powers of Pain beat the Pork Chop Express 114–74 for the 2025 title. On the redraw they meet again as the two number-one seeds in the “same finish” block, 1,105 combined points of roster value, the most of any game on the card.
  4. The most-played fixture in league history is still a division game, so it still happens twice. Guido Haters and Powers of Pain have met 45 times since 2008 — more than any pairing in the SCFL — and both landed in Spectre Syndicate again. Weeks 2 and 8 this time, not 4 and 10. The champions lead 24–20.
  5. The Killer Klowns are still 1–26 against a club they are obliged to play twice a season. They share Spartan with The Machines. Twenty-seven meetings, one win, now weeks 7 and 12 instead of 7 and 13. There is still no version of this schedule that spares them.
  6. The pairing swap revived the third-most-played fixture in league history. The New World Order and the Beaver Eaters have met 37 times and stand at 19–18 — the longest series in the SCFL still separated by a single game. The old draft, with Black and Blue paired to Spectre, never brought them together. This one does, in week 13, because the Four Horsemen and Black and Blue are paired this year.
  7. Two heavyweight rivalries got the reprieve instead. Heavy Hitters and the Smoke Dragons, 35 meetings, do not face each other this year. Neither do Guido Haters and Hairy Gumbas, 31 meetings. Both were on the board under the old draft’s division alignment; the pairing swap took them off it.
  8. Week 11 still revives a fixture that had been dead for five years. The New World Order and Still The Cream have not played since 2021. They meet again as a second-place club against a fourth-place club — the longest-dormant pairing the card brings back, on either draft.
  9. The smallest game on paper is Hairy Gumbas against Wookie Leaks. Week 6, 419 combined points of roster value — the two least valuable rosters in the league, forty-one points apart.
  10. The closest matchup of the season is a division game. Still The Cream (286) against Heavy Hitters (279) in week 10 — seven points between them, the tightest paper gap on the entire card.
  11. Black and Blue is still worth 1,632 and The Four Horsemen still worth 1,072. Nothing about division membership moved between drafts, so nothing about this gap could. The redraw can shuffle who gets punished or rewarded by it; it cannot close it.
  12. Dwayne does not have the easiest schedule of all time. Still The Cream posted a GIF about it the moment the redraw dropped. Checked: his schedule effect works out to a hair under zero — call it neutral. The club actually carrying the league’s softest schedule is the New World Order, at a real plus 0.29 wins, and they did not post a GIF about it.

We checked whether that survives changing the model, same as last time. It does. Crank the curve steep enough that the projected table spreads from 10.8 wins down to 4.8, and the biggest schedule effect in the league still tops out under plus 0.6. Your schedule will still be worth less to you this season than one healthy quarterback, one good waiver claim, or one Sunday where your bench outscores your starters.

Which remains the real verdict, and it survived a full redraw intact. A schedule rebuilt from scratch, under the same rules, with three of six placement weeks moved and one division pairing flipped, still hands its luckiest club only half a game. That is not an accident twice in a row. That is what the structure is for, and it is doing its job under pressure.

Seven Calls, Reissued

1. The Powers of Pain repeat — and the redraw did not touch this call. They won the 2025 title from a 7–7 regular season and a division seed the numbers say they did not quite earn on points, then got hot in the bracket anyway. They now hold the league’s most valuable roster and its softest division. Projection: 9.1 wins. We will still take the over.

2. The Pork Chop Express are this year’s cautionary tale, not Wookie Leaks. Under the old draft the Wookie absorbed the worst of it. Under this one, PCE drew the league’s easiest paired-division slate and got charged for it with the two toughest placement opponents anyone drew — the champions in week 5, The Machines in week 10. Watch the record dip in exactly those two weeks and get blamed on the roster instead of the calendar.

3. The New World Order make the playoffs on the seventh-most-valuable roster in the league. Softest raw schedule in the league at 296, and they sit right where a soft schedule actually pays off — a mid-pack roster that needs the help. This is still the club the structure was built to save.

4. The Beaver Eaters are still a trap, and their trap door is later than before. Their first five opponents average 289; their last four average 485 — the most brutal back-load in the league, and it closes with The Machines, the Pork Chop Express, the New World Order and, in week 14, the reigning champion Powers of Pain. Whatever their record is at week 10, subtract from it before you believe it. They do hold an 11–9 series edge on PoP, for what it’s worth going into that finale.

5. Heavy Hitters actually catch a break this time. Under the redraw they face the league’s current top six only three times in fourteen weeks — one of the two softest slates on the card, and it shows up in the model as their second-largest schedule boost. If 2–12 repeats, the schedule will not be the excuse.

6. The Four Horsemen still produce a division winner who thinks he is good. New World Order, Heavy Hitters, Hairy Gumbas and Horse Collars are worth 1,072 between them — barely more than Big Blue and the Pork Chop Express are worth as a pair — and six of their fourteen games are still against each other, now bunched into weeks 7 through 9. The table will flatter whichever of them wins it, and New World Order is the projected favorite to be the one it flatters.

7. The Killer Klowns lose to The Machines twice, just two weeks earlier than you thought. They are 1–26 in twenty-seven meetings, they share a division, and the calendar now says weeks 7 and 12 instead of 7 and 13. We are still not calling the upset.

The Short Version

The Machines redrew this schedule the day after posting it — moved the division slate into weeks 2, 4, 7, 8, 9 and 12, ran three division games back to back through the middle of the season, and left the four rules exactly as they were. The Machines and the New World Order rebuilt 112 games out of the same four divisions and the same 2025 finish inside them, by hand, twice in two days, and it checks out both times — all 112 games, four rules, no exceptions.

What actually moved is the mechanism, not just the schedule of it. The paired-division block, not the placement rounds, is now doing 98 percent of the work of making this thing fair — a finding the first draft never surfaced, because the first draft is not the one anybody is playing. The placement games still decide something real: this year, winning your division buys you the same finishing slate as coming third in it, not a harder one, which is a cleaner outcome than the old draft produced.

One seeding wrinkle survives close reading: Spectre Syndicate alone is ranked by final 2025 standing rather than regular-season record, which is how a 7–7 club with the worse points-for total in its own tiebreak ended up the division’s number-one seed. Nothing else about the four divisions moved, including the 560-point gap between the strongest and the weakest of them, which no redraw is going to touch.

You are going to blame it anyway. Everybody does, on every draft. But the receipts are public twice now, the rules are on the record twice now, and every fixture on the new card traces back to something you did on the field in 2025. Week one is no longer the Bosom Bowl rematch — that lives in week 5 now, and it is also the single biggest game on the board. What opens the season instead is The Machines against Still The Cream, 30–4 in 34 meetings, the most lopsided series on the whole card, kicking off in the very first week of a schedule its own architect built. After that, nobody gets to blame anything.

He called the first draft a thing of beauty. He tore it up a day later and rebuilt it anyway, and it still passes the same audit. Credit where it is due, again: the 2026 SCFL schedule — second edition — is the work of The Machines and the New World Order, checked against all 112 games by this desk, both times.

The revision was posted by The Machines to league chat in a follow-up message under the heading "Tweaks," with the full rule set restated immediately after; both were verified independently by this desk against every one of the 112 fixtures on the new card, and all four rules hold with no exceptions. 2025 in-division finish was rebuilt from the season's regular-season matchup log (not the final post-playoff standings field, which reflects bracket results) for three divisions; Spectre Syndicate's seeding was matched to final standing after regular-season order failed to reproduce its placement-week fixtures, and the resulting order was verified against all four placement weeks (32 of 32 games). Head-to-head records, all-time series and reprieve/revival facts come from eighteen seasons of league matchup data (2008–2025) mapped through the franchise ledger's 62 historical team-name aliases; the 2025 title game (Powers of Pain 114, Pork Chop Express 74) is from the week 17 bracket. Roster values are DynastyProcess superflex player values applied to current Sleeper rosters, August 2026. Projected wins use a logistic on roster-value difference; the scale parameter is a judgement call, and the article reports how the conclusions move when it changes.

Schedule built by The Machines
Analysis by the SCFL NewsRoom

The 2026 card went up on Sleeper this week, and the reaction in the chat was the reaction it always is: sixteen men scrolling to week one, finding their own name, and deciding within four seconds whether they have been robbed.

So this desk did the boring thing. We pulled the whole card down — 112 games, fourteen weeks, sixteen clubs — laid it against twenty-one seasons of league history and against what every roster is currently worth, and went looking for the shape of it.

The card is The Machines’ work this year. He posted the rules he built it from, which spared us the guesswork and gave us something better to do: check them.

All four rules hold. Across all 112 games. Not approximately — exactly, with no exceptions and no fudged fixtures. Every single game on this card follows from two facts about your club: which division you are in, and where you finished in it last year.

There is no luck in this schedule at all. Nothing was drawn. Nothing was randomised. No generator touched it. One man sat down with four divisions and a final table and produced 112 games that obey four rules exactly — and if you do not like your card, you built it in November.

What follows is the card, the machine behind it, what the design does that nobody in the chat has noticed, and the twelve things worth knowing before week one.

The Rules, As Filed

The architect this year is The Machines. He posted the method to league chat, under the heading “just in case people forgot for any reason how we sort the schedule”:

Divisional play weeks: 2, 4, 7, 8, 9 & 12 — middle of the season heated up, weeks 7-8-9 run three division games in a row. Alternating Division (Spartan vs SS, 4 Horse vs BB) weeks: 1, 3, 6 & 13. Remaining same standing 2025 weeks: 5 & 10. Remaining — 2025 1st vs 3rd, 2nd vs 4th — weeks: 11 & 14.

It arrives at fourteen like this: six games inside your own division, home and away against all three rivals. Four against every club in the division you are paired with this year. Two against the clubs that finished where you finished, in the two divisions you are not paired with. And two against the clubs at the mirrored position — if you won your division, the two remaining third-place clubs; if you came second, the two remaining fourth-place clubs.

Six plus four plus two plus two. Fourteen. The division pairing alternates year to year; for 2026 it is Spartan with Spectre Syndicate, the Four Horsemen with Black and Blue.

We Checked All Of It

Every club plays exactly three opponents twice, eight once, and four not at all. The three you play twice are your division, in all sixteen cases, spread across weeks 2, 4, 7, 8, 9 and 12 — with a genuine three-in-a-row stretch through weeks 7, 8 and 9, nothing else on the calendar those weeks but division football. The four you play in full are your paired division, in all sixteen cases, and the pairings are the ones named above: Spartan with Spectre Syndicate, the Four Horsemen with Black and Blue.

The last four games are the fiddly part, and they are the part that is exactly right. For all sixteen clubs, two of them are against clubs that finished in the same slot of their own division, and two are against clubs at the mirrored slot — first with third, second with fourth — drawn from the two divisions you are not paired with. Sixteen out of sixteen. No leftovers, no repeats, nobody playing anybody five times.

One wrinkle in the seeding took rebuilding the 2025 standings from the actual matchup log to find. Three of the four divisions are seeded by plain in-division regular-season record. Spectre Syndicate is not. Guido Haters finished the 2025 regular season 7–7 with more points scored than Powers of Pain, also 7–7 — but PoP is the club that got hot in the bracket and won the whole thing. Spectre’s number one seed for 2026 scheduling purposes is the champion, not the club that actually out-scored him in-division. The other three divisions use the boring method. This one crowns whoever won it all.

Where The Weeks Go

The rules say what you play. The card says when, and it is tidier than anybody realised.

Start with the giveaway: week 8 is week 2 again. Not similar — identical. All eight fixtures, the same eight pairs of clubs, replayed. Week 9 repeats week 4. Week 12 repeats week 7. Those six weeks are the entire divisional slate, out and back, and nothing else lives in them — including three straight weeks, 7 through 9, where division football is the only thing on the board.

The Part Nobody Noticed

Here is where the design earns the phrase “it’s a thing of beauty.”

Look at the Four Horsemen. Softest division in the league at 1,072 — and the hardest paired four in the league at 408, because the Horsemen draw Black and Blue, which is the most valuable division in the SCFL at 1,632. The advantage and the penalty land on the same clubs, in the same season, by design. It happens on the other side of the pairing too: Black and Blue’s clubs get a soft paired-four at 268, because they draw the Horsemen right back — and Black and Blue is itself the strongest division, so its clubs get a harder six inside. The pairings run strong against weak, so the two biggest slices pull against each other.

98%
How much of the season’s fairness gain is banked by week 10

Then the numbers make the case outright. Strength-of-schedule spread across the league has a standard deviation of 63.6 after the six division games alone. Add the four paired-division games and it collapses to 25.7 — 98 percent of the way to where the full 14-game card ends up. Add the last four placement games, and the number barely moves again, landing at 25.0.

The paired-division block is doing almost all of the fairness work on this card. The placement games matter for other reasons — see below — but on the league-wide fairness question, they are close to decorative. Each slice on its own looks lopsided — standard deviations of 64, 50 and 57 — and stacked together they cancel down to 25. The whole card is meaningfully fairer than any single part of it, and it happens well before the season’s final month. That is not an accident and it is not luck. That is what the structure is for.

One Wrinkle, Free Of Charge

The placement games are not pointless, even if they barely move the league-wide fairness needle. There is a real effect buried in them.

Pairing first with third and second with fourth is not a ladder, and on this card it produces something cleaner than you might expect. Add up the four placement opponents for every club that finished first in its division, and the total comes to 5,964 points of roster value — the exact same total as every club that finished third. Every club that finished second draws 4,870, identical to every club that finished fourth. First and third face the same aggregate test. Second and fourth face the same, easier one.

So there is no odd/even penalty this year: winning your division buys you the same finishing slate as coming third in it, not a harder one. Worth stating plainly rather than dressing up — this is what the numbers say for these sixteen rosters on this specific card, not a law the design guarantees every year. Whether it holds again in 2027 depends on who is good.

The other thing worth flagging, gently: the pairing alternates by design, and this year it landed on Spartan with Spectre Syndicate, the Four Horsemen with Black and Blue. Whoever drew the short straw in that arrangement inherited the toughest four-game slice on the board; whoever drew the long one inherited the softest. Consider it filed.

Twelve Things Buried In The Card
  1. Week 8 is week 2 again. All eight fixtures, the same eight pairs, replayed. Week 9 repeats week 4 and week 12 repeats week 7. That is the divisional slate, out and back, and it is the whole of it.
  2. Spectre Syndicate is seeded by a different rule than everyone else. Three divisions rank their 2026 seeding by plain 2025 regular-season record. Spectre ranks by final standing — which means Powers of Pain, 7–7 with the worse points-for of the division’s two co-leaders, sits above Guido Haters anyway, because PoP is the club that got hot in the bracket and won the whole thing.
  3. The Bosom Bowl rematch is also the single biggest game on paper. Powers of Pain beat the Pork Chop Express 114–74 for the 2025 title. They meet again in week 5, as the two number-one seeds in the “same finish” block — 1,105 combined points of roster value, the most of any game on the card. The champions lead the all-time series 5–2.
  4. The most-played fixture in league history is a division game, so it happens twice every year. The Guido Haters and the Powers of Pain have met 45 times since 2008 — more than any other pairing in the SCFL — and they are both Spectre Syndicate. Weeks 2 and 8. The champions lead 24–20.
  5. The Killer Klowns are 1–26 against a club they are obliged to play twice a season. They share Spartan with The Machines. Twenty-seven meetings, one win. Weeks 7 and 12. There is no version of this schedule that spares them.
  6. The third-most-played fixture in league history is back on the card. The New World Order and the Beaver Eaters have met 37 times and stand at 19–18 — the longest series in the SCFL still separated by a single game. This year’s pairing brings them together in week 13, because the Four Horsemen and Black and Blue are paired.
  7. Two heavyweight rivalries got a reprieve instead. Heavy Hitters and the Smoke Dragons, 35 meetings, do not face each other this year. Neither do Guido Haters and Hairy Gumbas, 31 meetings.
  8. Week 11 revives a fixture that has been dead for five years. The New World Order and Still The Cream have not played since 2021. They meet as a second-place club against a fourth-place club — the longest-dormant pairing the card brings back.
  9. The smallest game on paper is Hairy Gumbas against Wookie Leaks. Week 6, 419 combined points of roster value — the two least valuable rosters in the league, forty-one points apart.
  10. The closest matchup of the season is a division game. Still The Cream (286) against Heavy Hitters (279) in week 10 — seven points between them, the tightest paper gap on the entire card.
  11. Black and Blue is worth 1,632 and The Four Horsemen 1,072. The design corrects a great deal, but it cannot correct that. The Horsemen play six games a year inside the weakest room in the league.
  12. Dwayne does not have the easiest schedule of all time. Still The Cream posted a GIF about it the moment the card dropped. Checked: his schedule effect works out to a hair under zero — call it neutral. The club actually carrying the league’s softest schedule is the New World Order, at a real plus 0.29 wins, and they did not post a GIF about it.

We checked whether that survives changing the model. It does. Crank the curve steep enough that the projected table runs from 10.8 wins down to 4.8, and the biggest schedule effect in the league still only reaches plus 0.6. Flatten it and the effect shrinks toward nothing. Across every version, the top three are the same three clubs, in the same order.

Your schedule will be worth less to you this season than one healthy quarterback, one good waiver claim, or one Sunday where your bench outscores your starters.

Which is the real verdict on the whole apparatus, and it is a compliment. A schedule built by hand out of divisions and last year's table could easily have handed somebody a two-win head start. This one hands the luckiest club in the league about half a game. It was designed to cancel out, and it cancels out.

Seven Calls, On The Record

1. The Powers of Pain repeat — and this time they earn it in the regular season. They won the 2025 title from a 7–7 regular season and a division seed the numbers say they did not quite earn on points — then got hot in the bracket anyway. They now hold the most valuable roster in the league and the softest division in it. Projection: 9.1 wins. We will go a half-game higher.

2. The Pork Chop Express are this year’s cautionary tale. They drew the league’s easiest paired-division slate, then got charged for it with the two toughest placement opponents anyone drew — the champions in week 5, The Machines in week 10. Watch the record dip in exactly those two weeks and get blamed on the roster instead of the calendar.

3. The New World Order make the playoffs on the seventh-most-valuable roster in the league. Softest raw schedule in the league at 296, and they sit right where a soft schedule actually pays off — a mid-pack roster that needs the help. This is the club the structure genuinely helps.

4. The Beaver Eaters are a trap in December. Their first five opponents average 289 and their last four average 485 — the most brutal back-load in the league, closing with The Machines, the Pork Chop Express, the New World Order and, in week 14, the reigning champion Powers of Pain. Whatever their record is at week 10, subtract from it before you believe it. They do hold an 11–9 series edge on PoP, for what it’s worth going into that finale.

5. Heavy Hitters catch a real break. They face the league’s current top six only three times in fourteen weeks — one of the two softest slates on the card, and it shows up in the model as their second-largest schedule boost. If 2–12 repeats, the schedule will not be the excuse.

6. The Four Horsemen produce a division winner who thinks he is good. The New World Order, Heavy Hitters, the Hairy Gumbas and Horse Collars are worth 1,072 between them — barely more than Big Blue and the Pork Chop Express are worth as a pair — and six of their fourteen games are against each other, bunched into weeks 7 through 9. The table will flatter whichever of them wins it, and New World Order is the projected favorite to be the one it flatters.

7. The Killer Klowns lose to The Machines twice. They are 1–26 in twenty-seven meetings, they share a division, and the calendar says weeks 7 and 12. We are not going to be the ones to call the upset.

The Short Version

Nobody drew this schedule out of a hat. The Machines built it out of four divisions and last year’s finish inside them, by hand, and it checks out to the game — all 112 of them, four rules, no exceptions.

The design does something the chat has not given it credit for. Your division, your paired division and your placement games each look badly unbalanced on their own. Stacked together they cancel — and almost all of that cancelling happens in one place: the paired-division block alone closes 98 percent of the gap between the six-game division slate and the finished card. The placement games, for all the attention they get, barely move the fairness number. What they do instead is decide something else: this year, winning your division buys you the same finishing slate as coming third in it, not a harder one.

One seeding wrinkle survives close reading: Spectre Syndicate alone is ranked by final 2025 standing rather than regular-season record, which is how a 7–7 club with the worse points-for total in its own tiebreak ended up the division’s number-one seed. And one thing the structure cannot fix is that Black and Blue is worth half again what The Four Horsemen are worth.

You are going to blame it anyway. Everybody does. But this year the receipts are public, the rules are on the record, and every fixture on the card traces back to something you did on the field. Week one opens with The Machines against Still The Cream — 30–4 in 34 meetings, the most lopsided series on the whole card, kicking off a schedule its own architect built. After that, nobody gets to blame anything.

He called it a thing of beauty. We came to check him and ended up agreeing with him. Credit where it is due: the 2026 SCFL schedule is the work of The Machines — one man, a spreadsheet and a set of rules that survives an audit of every game on it.

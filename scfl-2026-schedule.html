Somebody Built This By Hand, And It Holds Up

The 2026 card went up on Sleeper this week, and the reaction in the chat was the reaction it always is: sixteen men scrolling to week one, finding their own name, and deciding within four seconds whether they have been robbed.

So this desk did the boring thing. We pulled the whole card down — 112 games, fourteen weeks, sixteen clubs — laid it against twenty-one seasons of league history and against what every roster is currently worth, and went looking for the shape of it.

Then The Machines posted the rules he built it from, which spared us the guesswork and gave us something better to do: check them.

All four rules hold. Across all 112 games. Not approximately — exactly, with no exceptions and no fudged fixtures. Every single game on this card follows from two facts about your club: which division you are in, and where you finished in it last year.

There is no luck in this schedule at all. Nothing was drawn. Nothing was randomised. If you do not like your card, you built it in November.

What follows is the card, the machine behind it, what the design does that nobody in the chat has noticed, and the eleven things worth knowing before week one.

The Rules, As Filed

From The Machines, at 4:17 this afternoon, under the heading “just in case people forgot for any reason how we sort the schedule”:

Play your Division 6 times. Divisionally: alternating divisions for 2026 — Spectre vs Black n Blue, Spartan vs Four Horsemen. Then teams that finished the same spot in standings. Final two games: 2026 1st place teams vs remaining two 3rd place teams; 2nd place teams vs remaining two 4th place teams. That’s our 14 game schedule. It’s a thing of beauty really.

It arrives at fourteen like this: six games inside your own division, home and away against all three rivals. Four against every club in the division you are paired with this year. Two against the clubs that finished where you finished, in the two divisions you are not paired with. And two against the clubs at the mirrored position — if you won your division, the two remaining third-place clubs; if you came second, the two remaining fourth-place clubs.

Six plus four plus two plus two. Fourteen.

We Checked All Of It

Every club plays exactly three opponents twice, eight once, and four not at all. The three you play twice are your division, in all sixteen cases. The four you play in full are your paired division, in all sixteen cases — and the pairings are the ones he named: Spectre Syndicate with Black and Blue, Spartan with The Four Horsemen.

The last four games are the fiddly part, and they are the part that is exactly right. For all sixteen clubs, two of them are against clubs that finished in the same slot of their own division, and two are against clubs at the mirrored slot — first with third, second with fourth — drawn from the two divisions you are not paired with. Sixteen out of sixteen. No leftovers, no repeats, nobody playing anybody five times.

Where The Weeks Go

The rules say what you play. The card says when, and it is tidier than anybody realised.

Start with the giveaway: week 8 is week 2 again. Not similar — identical. All eight fixtures, the same eight pairs of clubs, replayed six weeks later. Week 10 repeats week 4. Week 13 repeats week 7. Those six weeks are the entire divisional slate, out and back, and nothing else lives in them.

The Part Nobody Noticed

Here is where the design earns the phrase The Machines used.

Look at the Powers of Pain again. Softest division in the league at 239 — and the hardest paired four in the league at 408, because Spectre Syndicate draws Black and Blue, which is the most valuable division in the SCFL. The advantage and the penalty land on the same club, in the same season, by design.

It happens across the board. Spartan gets the softest paired four at 268 because it plays The Four Horsemen, the weakest division — and Spartan is itself the second-strongest division, so its clubs get a harder six inside. The pairings run strong against weak, so the two biggest slices pull against each other.

40%
How much the last four games shrink the unfairness

Then the placement games finish the job. Through ten games, the spread in strength of schedule across the league has a standard deviation of 39.9. Add the four placement games and it falls to 24.0.

Each slice on its own looks lopsided — standard deviations of 64, 50 and 51. Stack all three and they cancel down to 24. The whole card is meaningfully fairer than any single part of it. That is not an accident and it is not luck. That is what the structure is for.

One Wrinkle, Free Of Charge

There is a quirk in the final rule, and it cuts against the clubs that did well.

Pairing first with third and second with fourth means the four placement games are not a ladder. Work it through and the club that won its division draws a placement slate averaging 367. The club that finished second draws one averaging 310. Third place gets 373. Fourth place gets 304.

So the reward for winning your division is a harder finish than the club you beat to it, and the reward for coming third is a harder finish than the club that came last. Odd placings get the tough end; even placings get the soft one.

It is a small effect — four games, and roster value is not destiny — but it is systematic rather than random, and it will be there again next year unless somebody swaps the pairing to first-with-fourth. Consider it filed.

The other thing worth flagging, gently: Spectre Syndicate drew Black and Blue in 2025 as well. Spartan drew The Four Horsemen in 2025 as well. Back in 2023 it ran the other way — Black and Blue with the Horsemen, Spectre with Spartan. If the pairing is meant to alternate, this is the second year on the same alignment.

Eleven Things Buried In The Card
  1. Week 8 is week 2 again. All eight fixtures, the same eight pairs, replayed six weeks later. Week 10 repeats week 4 and week 13 repeats week 7. That is the divisional slate, out and back, and it is the whole of it.
  2. Week 1 is the Bosom Bowl rematch. The Powers of Pain beat the Pork Chop Express 114–74 for the 2025 title. They open 2026 against each other, in the first week, because Spectre and Black and Blue are paired this year. The champions lead the all-time series 5–2.
  3. The most-played fixture in league history is a division game, so it happens twice every year. The Guido Haters and the Powers of Pain have met 45 times since 2008 — more than any other pairing in the SCFL — and they are both Spectre Syndicate. Weeks 4 and 10. The champions lead 24–20.
  4. The Killer Klowns are 1–26 against a club they are obliged to play twice a season. They share Spartan with The Machines. Twenty-seven meetings, one win. Weeks 7 and 13. There is no version of this schedule that spares them.
  5. The third-most-played fixture in league history does not happen at all. The New World Order and the Beaver Eaters have met 37 times and stand at 19–18 — the longest series in the SCFL still separated by a single game. Different divisions, not paired this year, and they finished second and third. Nothing on the card brings them together.
  6. Two clubs got a reprieve they will never acknowledge. Still The Cream, 4–30 against The Machines in 34 meetings, do not face them. Neither does Big Blue, who are 12–16 across 29.
  7. Week 6 revives a fixture that has been dead for five years. The New World Order and Still The Cream have not played since 2021. They meet as a second-place club against a fourth-place club — the longest-dormant pairing the card brings back.
  8. The biggest game on paper is a one-off. Week 12, the Powers of Pain against Big Blue: 1,157 points of combined roster value, the two most valuable rosters in the league, and a paired-division game they play exactly once.
  9. The smallest game on paper is in the same week. Week 12 also gives you the Smoke Dragons against Wookie Leaks — 388 combined, the two least valuable rosters in the league, ten points apart.
  10. The closest matchup of the season lands in week 14. The New World Order (352) against Master Jet-I (355). Three points between them, in the last week before the playoffs, as a second-place club against a third-place club.
  11. Black and Blue is worth 1,632 and The Four Horsemen 1,072. The design corrects a great deal, but it cannot correct that. The Horsemen play six games a year inside the weakest room in the league, and the divisions have not moved since at least 2023.

We checked whether that survives changing the model. It does. Crank the curve steep enough that the projected table runs from 10.6 wins down to 4.5, and the biggest schedule effect in the league still only reaches plus 0.64. Flatten it and the effect shrinks toward nothing. Across every version, the top three are the same three clubs, in the same order.

Your schedule will be worth less to you this season than one healthy quarterback, one good waiver claim, or one Sunday where your bench outscores your starters.

Which is the real verdict on the whole apparatus, and it is a compliment. A schedule built by hand out of divisions and last year's table could easily have handed somebody a two-win head start. This one hands the luckiest club in the league a third of a win. It was designed to cancel out, and it cancels out.

Seven Calls, On The Record

1. The Powers of Pain repeat — and this time they earn it in the regular season. They won the 2025 title from a 7–7 regular season and a second-place finish in Spectre, which is the least convincing championship run in recent league memory. They now hold the most valuable roster in the league and the softest division in it. Nine wins is the projection. We will go a half-game higher.

2. Wookie Leaks starts 0–3 or thereabouts. His first five opponents average 414, and his division is Big Blue, the Pork Chop Express and the Beaver Eaters, twice each. Big Blue in week 7 and again in week 13, a series he trails 6–19. If the season is going to get away from him it will have done so by Halloween.

3. The New World Order makes the playoffs on the seventh-best roster in the league. Softest card in the league at 291, and they sit right in the middle of the curve where a soft schedule actually pays. This is the club the structure genuinely helps.

4. The Beaver Eaters are a trap in November. Their first five opponents average 304 and their last four average 468 — the most brutal back-load in the league, finishing with the Pork Chop Express in 13 and the champions in 14. Whatever their record is at week 10, subtract from it before you believe it.

5. Heavy Hitters do not repeat 2–12, and it will not matter. They drew eight games against last season's top six, more than anybody in the league. Their roster is better than their record was. Their schedule is not going to let them prove it.

6. The Four Horsemen produce a division winner at 8–6 who thinks he is good. The New World Order, Heavy Hitters, the Hairy Gumbas and Horse Collars are worth 1,072 between them — barely more than Big Blue and the Pork Chop Express are worth as a pair. Six of their fourteen games are against each other. The table will flatter all four of them.

7. The Killer Klowns lose to The Machines twice. They are 1–26 in twenty-seven meetings, they share a division, and the calendar says weeks 7 and 13. We are not going to be the ones to call the upset.

The Short Version

Nobody drew this schedule out of a hat. It was built out of four divisions and last year's finish inside them, by hand, and it checks out to the game — all 112 of them, four rules, no exceptions.

The design does something the chat has not given it credit for. Your division, your paired division and your placement games each look badly unbalanced on their own. Stacked together they cancel: the spread in strength of schedule falls by 40 per cent over the last four games alone, and the finished card is more even than any slice of it.

What is left over is worth about a third of a win to the club it helps most. There is one systematic quirk — winning your division buys you a harder finish than coming second in it — and one thing the structure cannot fix, which is that Black and Blue is worth half again what The Four Horsemen are worth.

You are going to blame it anyway. Everybody does. But this year the receipts are public, the rules are on the record, and every fixture on the card traces back to something you did on the field. Week one is the Bosom Bowl rematch. After that nobody gets to blame anything.

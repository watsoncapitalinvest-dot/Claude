Fourteen Weeks, Four Pods, And One Very Lucky Champion

The 2026 card went up on Sleeper this week, and the reaction in the chat was the reaction it always is: sixteen men scrolling to week one, finding their own name, and deciding within four seconds whether they have been robbed.

So this desk did the boring thing. We pulled the whole card down from Sleeper — 112 games, fourteen weeks, sixteen clubs — laid it against twenty-one seasons of league history and against what every roster in the league is currently worth, and went looking for the shape of it.

There is a shape. It is not the one anybody assumed.

Nobody drew this schedule. Nobody sat down and decided the Wookie should get Big Blue, the Pork Chop Express and the Beaver Eaters twice each. Sleeper did not run a lottery. It ran a template — a fixed pattern keyed to the order the sixteen of you were added to the league, and that order is the only random thing about the entire season.

What follows is the card, the machine that produced it, the eleven things buried in it that are worth knowing, and — because somebody always asks — what we think it all adds up to in January.

How It Was Actually Built

Start with the thing that gives the whole game away. Week 8 is week 2 again. Not similar to week 2 — identical. All eight fixtures, the same eight pairs of clubs, replayed six weeks later.

Week 10 is week 4 again. Week 13 is week 7 again. Three mirrored blocks, six weeks apart each time, twenty-four of the season's fixtures played exactly twice and the other sixty-four played exactly once.

Follow that thread and the machine falls open. Every club in this league plays three opponents twice, eight opponents once, and four opponents not at all. That is true of all sixteen, without exception. And the three you play twice are not three random clubs. They are the same three for everybody in your group, because Sleeper has quietly sorted the league into four pods of four and given each pod a home-and-away round robin.

Here is the part that should annoy you. The pods are not seeded, not balanced, and not based on last season. They are your roster slot, modulo four — slots 1, 5, 9 and 13 in one pod; 2, 6, 10 and 14 in the next; and so on. Roster slots are join order. They are the order the sixteen of you happened to get added to the Sleeper league.

Which means the six games that shape a third of your season were decided by an administrative accident that predates the 2026 season entirely.

That is where strength of schedule in this league actually comes from. Not from the eight one-off games — those roughly average out for everybody — but from which room you were standing in when the music stopped.

The Wookie has the hardest schedule in the league, at 385, and it is almost entirely the pod: his three repeat opponents average 481, the highest figure on the board by seventy points. He plays Big Blue twice, the Pork Chop Express twice and the Beaver Eaters twice. That is six games against three of the league's four most valuable rosters, and he owns the least valuable roster in the league.

At the other end, the New World Order drew the softest card in the league at 291, and Heavy Hitters the second softest at 302 — both of them in Pod C, the weak room.

Pods account for 36 per cent of the entire spread in strength of schedule across the sixteen. Nothing else in the card comes close.

The One Thing Worth Arguing About

Now the accusation everybody wants to make, tested properly.

The reigning champion is the Powers of Pain. They also own the most valuable roster in the league, at 610 — sixty-three clear of Big Blue and a hundred and fifteen clear of anybody else. And their pod — Still The Cream, the Guido Haters, the Smoke Dragons — averages 239. That is the softest set of repeat opponents any club in this league has been handed.

The New World Order is a whisker behind at 240, so the two easiest rooms in the league are effectively tied. The difference is what is standing in them. The New World Order owns the seventh-most valuable roster in the league. The Powers of Pain own the first.

239
The champion’s pod-mate average — lowest in the league

The best roster in the league got the weakest room. So we ran it. Fifty thousand times we reshuffled which club sits in which roster slot, kept Sleeper's template exactly as it is, and asked how often the strongest club in the league draws a pod that soft.

8.3 per cent. And the strongest club holding the outright softest pod in the league — which is what has happened here — comes up 11.1 per cent of the time. Call it one draw in nine.

SCFL NewsRoom · The Schedule · August 2026

So: unusual, yes. Rigged, no. And critically, the rest of the schedule shows no tilt at all. The overall spread in strength of schedule — 94 points from the Wookie down to the New World Order — is wider in 43 per cent of those fifty thousand redraws. The tendency for stronger clubs to draw weaker schedules, which looks damning at first glance, is matched or beaten by 52 per cent of redraws, because it is mostly just arithmetic: you never get to play yourself, so the best roster in the league is the only one that never faces the best roster in the league.

One club got a lucky room. The schedule did not get rigged. Those are different complaints, and only the first one is true.

If you want somebody to be angry at, be angry at whoever added the Powers of Pain to the Sleeper league eighth.

Eleven Things Buried In The Card
  1. Week 8 is week 2 again. All eight fixtures, the same eight pairs, replayed six weeks later. Week 10 repeats week 4 and week 13 repeats week 7. Nobody announced this.
  2. The most-played fixture in league history is on the card twice. The Guido Haters and the Powers of Pain have met 45 times since 2008 — more than any other pairing in the SCFL — and Sleeper handed them weeks 4 and 10. The champions lead the series 24–20.
  3. The third-most-played fixture in league history does not happen at all. The New World Order and the Beaver Eaters have met 37 times and stand at 19–18 — the longest series in the SCFL still separated by a single game. They are one of the 32 pairings that never occur in 2026.
  4. The Killer Klowns play The Machines twice. Weeks 7 and 13. The Klowns are 1–26 against them in twenty-seven meetings, the most lopsided long-running series in the league, and the machine that built this card does not know that.
  5. Two clubs got a reprieve they will never acknowledge. Still The Cream, 4–30 against The Machines in 34 meetings, do not face them. Neither does Big Blue, who are 12–16 against them across 29.
  6. Week 6 revives a fixture that has been dead for five years. The New World Order and Still The Cream have not played since 2021 — the longest-dormant pairing the 2026 card brings back.
  7. The biggest game on paper is a one-off. Week 12, the Powers of Pain against Big Blue: 1,157 points of combined roster value, the two most valuable rosters in the league, and they meet exactly once all season.
  8. The smallest game on paper is in the same week. Week 12 also gives you the Smoke Dragons against Wookie Leaks — 388 combined, the two least valuable rosters in the league, ten points of roster value apart.
  9. The closest matchup of the entire season lands in week 14. The New World Order (352) against Master Jet-I (355). Three points between them, in the last week before the playoffs.
  10. The steepest schedule in the league belongs to the Guido Haters, and it runs downhill. Their first five opponents average 417; their last four average 291. The Beaver Eaters do the exact opposite: 304 through five, then 468 across the closing four.
  11. Heavy Hitters, who went 2–12, drew eight games against last season's top six. That is more than anybody in the league. Big Blue, the Guido Haters and Still The Cream drew three apiece.

We checked whether that survives changing the model. It does. Crank the curve steep enough that the projected table runs from 10.6 wins down to 4.5, and the biggest schedule effect in the league still only reaches plus 0.64. Flatten it and the effect shrinks toward nothing. Across every version, the top three are the same three clubs, in the same order.

Your schedule will be worth less to you this season than one healthy quarterback, one good waiver claim, or one Sunday where your bench outscores your starters.

Which is the real finding, and it is a boring one. The Powers of Pain are the favourite because they have the best roster in the league by a distance, not because they got a soft pod. The soft pod is worth them four one-hundredths of a win. The roster is worth them two.

Seven Calls, On The Record

1. The Powers of Pain repeat — and this time they earn it in the regular season. They won the 2025 title from a 7–7 regular season, which is the least convincing championship run in recent league memory. They now hold the most valuable roster in the league and the third-softest card. Nine wins is the projection. We will go a half-game higher.

2. Wookie Leaks starts 0–3 or thereabouts. His first five opponents average 414. Big Blue in week 7 and again in week 13, a series he trails 6–19. If the season is going to get away from him it will have done so by Halloween.

3. The New World Order makes the playoffs on the seventh-best roster in the league. Softest schedule in the league, and they sit right in the middle of the curve where a soft schedule actually pays. This is the club the card genuinely helps.

SCFL NewsRoom · The Schedule · August 2026

4. The Beaver Eaters are a trap in November. Their first five opponents average 304 and their last four average 468 — the most brutal back-load in the league, finishing with the Pork Chop Express in 13 and the champions in 14. Whatever their record is at week 10, subtract from it before you believe it.

5. Heavy Hitters do not repeat 2–12, and it will not matter. They drew eight games against last year's top six, more than anybody in the league. Their roster is better than their record was. Their schedule is not going to let them prove it.

6. Somebody wins Pod C at 8–6 and thinks they are good. The New World Order, Heavy Hitters, the Hairy Gumbas and Horse Collars are worth 1,072 between them — barely more than Big Blue and the Pork Chop Express are worth as a pair. Six of their fourteen games are against each other. The table will flatter all four of them.

7. The Killer Klowns lose to The Machines twice. They are 1–26 against them in twenty-seven meetings across the history of this league. Sleeper looked at that and scheduled it in week 7 and again in week 13. We are not going to be the ones to call the upset.

The Short Version

Sleeper did not draw your schedule. It applied a template to a list, and the list is the order you joined. Six of your fourteen games were decided by that; the other eight were decided by a rotation that treats all sixteen of you identically.

The champion drew the softest room in the building, which happens about one time in nine and is the only genuinely unlikely thing on the whole card. Everything else about this schedule is exactly as ordinary as a schedule can be.

And when it is all run through the numbers, the whole apparatus — pods, mirrors, strength of schedule, the lot — is worth about a third of a win to the club it helps most.

You are going to blame it anyway. Everybody does. Week one is the Powers of Pain against the Pork Chop Express — last year's champion against last year's best record — and after that nobody gets to blame anything.

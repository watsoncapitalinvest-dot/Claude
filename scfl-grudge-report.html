The Grudge Report

This league has always described itself in grudges. Ask any manager who his rival is and you will get an answer in under a second, delivered with total confidence and no evidence whatsoever.

So the Newsroom went and counted. Every matchup in the league’s records — eighteen seasons, twenty-one franchises, 2,344 games — cross-checked against the standings until the two agreed exactly. Then we counted something else: who actually talks to whom, and how often it stops being friendly.

The results do not flatter everybody. Several of the rivalries this league believes in are real. Several are one man losing repeatedly to another man and calling it a rivalry.

The Method

A rivalry needs three things, and this desk scored all three. You have to play each other a lot. The series has to be close enough to be worth arguing about. And you have to actually engage — which we measured as back-and-forth in the chat, normalised so that the quiet managers aren’t punished for being quiet and the loud ones don’t win by volume alone.

Note what is deliberately absent. We counted how often exchanges turn sharp, because that says something about a pairing. We are not printing what was said. This league has been friends for thirty years and the worst thing anyone said at eleven o’clock on a Sunday night is not evidence of anything except a bad week.

The Tormentor

Start with the finding that reorganises everything else. The Machines does not have a rivalry. He has victims.

Against the Killer Klowns he is 26-1. Against Still The Creamiest, 30-4. Against the Smoke Dragons, 18-3. Against the Hairy Gumbas, 17-3. Against Wookie Leaks, 17-3. Against the Beaver Eaters, 10-2.

That is six franchises and 134 games at a combined 118-16, with a points margin north of four thousand. There is no other word for it. The loudest arguments in this league are not between equals — they are the sound of six men losing to the same person for two decades.

The Flaw

And then the record book turns on him, because for all of that, The Machines has been to eleven title games and lost seven of them.

Eleven finals in eighteen seasons is a rate nobody else approaches; it means he has played for the championship in more than half the years this league has existed. He has four rings to show for it. He has lost the final to the Powers of Pain twice, to the Guido Haters, to Horse Collars, to the New World Order, to a franchise that no longer exists, and — most recently and most expensively — to Big Blue.

The most dominant regular-season franchise in SCFL history has spent eighteen years being beaten in the one game that gets remembered.

The One Man Who Holds

Which brings us to the only genuine rivalry The Machines has, and the best one in the league.

Big Blue is 13-16 against him. That is not a winning record, but it is the only competitive one anybody owns, and it comes with the highest stakes attached: seven playoff meetings, more than any other pairing involving either man, and a title game.

In 2023 Big Blue beat The Machines in the final, 125.5 to 100. It was the middle jewel of a three-peat — 2021, 2022, 2023 — which makes Big Blue three-for-three in championship games, the only perfect record in the building. He has also won the last five meetings between them outright.

One man is 118-16 against six franchises and cannot beat the one who matters. That is a rivalry.

The Purest

If you want the pairing that behaves most like a rivalry is supposed to, it is the Pork Chop Express and the Smoke Dragons.

The series is 9-7 across sixteen games, a margin of 154 points over nine seasons — close enough that either man can claim it depending on the day. What separates it is the engagement: 21.6% of all the back-and-forth these two have in the chat is with each other.

Only one pairing in the league engages more, and it proves the point rather than spoiling it. The Machines and the Smoke Dragons run at 23.4% — the highest mutual figure in the SCFL — and that series is 18-3. Men talk most at the person beating them. Take away the pairings where somebody is simply losing, and the Express and the Dragons are the most engaged evenly-matched teams in the building, by a distance.

Two evenly matched teams who cannot stop talking to one another. That is the textbook definition, and they are the only pair who satisfy it completely.

The Old Marriages

The longest series in league history belongs to two men nobody thinks of as rivals at all: the Powers of Pain and the Guido Haters have played **forty-five times**, splitting them 24-21, with seven playoff meetings. Forty-five games and almost nothing said about it.

Close behind: the New World Order and the Beaver Eaters, 37 games and a series standing at 19-18 — one game apart after seventeen seasons together. And Horse Collars and the Beaver Eaters, 35 games across seventeen seasons, 19-16 to the Beavers, with the two of them among the most constant presences in each other’s chat.

These are the old marriages of the SCFL. Enormous shared history, almost no heat, and a scoreline that refuses to separate.

The Haunting

Now the cruellest thing in the file, and it was invisible until this audit, because the man on the winning end of it barely speaks.

Still The Creamiest and the Heavy Hitters have played twenty-seven times. The regular-season series is a coin flip: 14-13 to the Hitters. But they have met in the playoffs **ten times — more than any other pairing in league history** — and the Hitters have won eight of them.

Eight postseason eliminations at the hands of the same quiet franchise. It is the most one-sided postseason relationship the records contain, and it has been happening in near silence for two decades.

The Food Chain

It does not stop there. The Hairy Gumbas have knocked the Heavy Hitters out seven times. The Hitters have knocked the Creamiest out eight times.

There is a pecking order buried in the postseason records of this league, and it has been remarkably stable: the Gumbas eat the Hitters, the Hitters eat the Creamiest, and The Machines eats almost everybody until the final, where somebody else eats him.

The Quiet Ones

Two pairings deserve mention precisely because nobody argues about them.

The Jet-I and the Smoke Dragons have played twenty-nine times and the series sits at 15-14, with five playoff meetings. Dead level over three decades’ worth of games, and it has never once become a talking point.

And the tightest series in the entire dataset, by the only measure that cannot be argued with: Lamb Chops versus The Machines, nine games, and a cumulative points difference of **four**. Not four a game. Four in total. The newest manager in the league and the most decorated, separated by a rounding error, and neither of them has ever mentioned it.

The Half-Point Games

Three playoff games in league history have been decided by half a point. The Heavy Hitters over the Powers of Pain in 2021. The Killer Klowns over the Jet-I in 2024. Big Blue over the Pork Chop Express in 2018. Somewhere in each of those weeks a man set a lineup on a Sunday morning and never learned how close it came.

The Verdict

The league’s own account of itself turns out to be about half right.

The rivalries people talk about loudest are mostly real, and the Express and the Dragons are as advertised. But the two most remarkable relationships in the record book — the Hitters’ twenty-year haunting of the Creamiest, and forty-five games between the Powers of Pain and the Guido Haters — belong to men who have never made a noise about either.

And the loudest grievance in the SCFL, the one that has generated more words than any other subject in five years of chat, is not a rivalry at all. It is six franchises, 118-16, and one man who still cannot win a final.

Is SCFL Really the Oldest Dynasty League? We Went Looking For Proof — and Found Competition

“Twenty-two years, four separate record files that all agree on 2005 — and, while checking, five other leagues that say they're older, one of them our exact size.”
By The Record Room · Investigated with the league's own archives, plus a full open web and Reddit sweep, and a size comparison

The claim has been repeated around the league for years without ever really being tested: SCFL, now in its 22nd season and 16 teams strong, is the oldest continuously running dynasty fantasy football league anywhere. This desk decided to actually check it — pull the internal record files, go looking for anyone who could say otherwise, and specifically ask whether SCFL's size changes the picture, since a 16-team league is a harder thing to keep alive than a 10-team one. The internal case turned out to be real and well-documented. The web turned up something the league hasn't had to reckon with: five other leagues, all still running, all claiming to be older — and the strongest one runs the exact same 16 teams SCFL does.

The Case For SCFL

Four separate files in the league's own archive — built at different times, for different purposes — independently land on the same founding year. records.json shows a continuous span of 21 recorded seasons, 2005 to 2025, sourced from League Legacy's archival platform. The commissioner's own note in league-titles.json confirms championships were won before the digitized history even starts. dossier.json names four individual franchises with their own 2005 start dates on record — the Quahog Clams, the Dicks, the Beekman Marauders, and Caves ofda hood. And most convincingly, scfl-franchises.json shows 16 of the league's 23 all-time franchises independently carrying a 2005 start date in their own individual records — not one league-wide number, sixteen separate ones. The league's own migration trail, from ESPN to League Legacy to Sleeper, adds a real, harder-to-fake detail on top: it survived an actual platform change, not just an uninterrupted count on one system.

“Dynasty's own organized community — the forum where dynasty owners have argued about this exact question for two decades — is younger than SCFL. The format didn't have a name yet when this league started keeping score.”

The Competition We Found

There is no registry of the oldest fantasy leagues — no Guinness record, no central list anyone can check a claim against. So this desk went looking the way anyone would: forums, Reddit, and open search, then went back for more — specifically checking whether SCFL's size changes anything, since SCFL runs 16 teams and two of the first rivals found only run 10. It doesn't. Five real, self-reported dynasty leagues turned up, all older than SCFL, and the oldest reasonably-documented one is also 16 teams.

Five Leagues, Five Files

Mega Football League — Kingman, Kansas
Founded 1987 · team count unconfirmed

The oldest claim found, and the most serious one: turned up in a survey of long-running fantasy leagues that independently calls it "a Dynasty League" — the one spot in this whole search where that word came from the source, not this desk's inference. A separate reference puts it on its "34th season" as of 2020, which lines up with a 1987 start. Repeated, differently-worded searches never turned up its team count or roster rules — the single biggest information gap in this report.

The 16-team league — footballguys forum
Founded 1990 · same size as SCFL

The size-and-age match. A forum post dated August 27, 2023 describes a 16-team dynasty league — SCFL's exact footprint — with all 16 original owners still in it, scattered across the country. The poster names players from the original startup draft: Warren Moon, Herschel Walker, Andre Rison, Ozzie Newsome, Fuad Reveiz — real NFL names whose careers line up with a 1990 startup draft, a detail that at least holds together even though it couldn't be checked directly.

The "oldest running dynasty league" — DLF forum
~30th year · team count and post date both unconfirmed

Posted to a Dynasty League Football forum thread literally titled that. The account has real texture: the original championship roster included Marcus Allen and Jerry Rice, the league ran on pen-and-paper record-keeping before moving to spreadsheets about seven years before the post, and ten owners have stayed stable for the past 17 years. It's also the one rival in this report whose post date this desk could not pin down at all, despite a dedicated search.

Two more, same footballguys thread
Both dated to the Aug 27, 2023 post

A 14-team dynasty league with 11 of its original 14 owners still in it — closer to SCFL's size than the 10-team version below it. And the 10-team league itself: 5 of the original 10 owners, one who joined in year 4, one who is the son of an original owner.

None of these five claims is independently verified — they're self-reported, exactly like SCFL's own claim, on forums this desk could not directly access to confirm founding years or continuity of format. But none of them can be ruled out, either. The 16-team league is the one that matters most for the size question: it's the exact same footprint as SCFL, 15 years older, and — per the poster — hasn't lost a single original owner.

Also found, but not counted as rivals

The same footballguys thread surfaced a league in its "38th year" (no further detail available), and a league with its "40th annual draft" behind it — started 1984, 5 original owners still in — but neither had its format confirmed as dynasty. One thread reply is explicitly ruled out rather than just unconfirmed: a 34th-year league the poster describes as straight redraft, not dynasty — useful proof that not everything old on that thread is a real rival. And for scale: fantasy football itself dates to 1962 (the GOPPPL, an 8-team AFL-only redraft league) — 43 years older than SCFL, but the wrong format entirely to count here.

One catch on all five: they're old data, not live status

The footballguys thread behind three of these claims is dated August 27, 2023 — so "31st year" was true then, not now; if those leagues are still going, they're really closer to their 34th season. The Mega Football League figure traces to a 2020 mention. The DLF forum league's post couldn't even be dated. None of the five has been confirmed still running in 2026 — leagues fold, and a real post from 2020 or 2023 only proves a league was alive then. SCFL's evidence, by contrast, is live league data as of today. That's a real point in SCFL's favor this report hadn't said plainly before now.

Does Being 16 Teams Matter?

Yes, and it's a fair point to raise — a 16-team dynasty league is a genuinely harder thing to keep alive for two decades than a 10-team one. Twice as many owners who need to stay engaged, get replaced, or the league frays; more trades and more rookie picks to track every year; more coordination, full stop. That's real, and it's part of why SCFL's run is impressive. It just isn't a reason SCFL's run is the oldest — the strongest rival on the list above is also 16 teams, and older.

The Verdict

What SCFL can honestly claim

Not "the oldest, full stop" — five real leagues once claimed to be older, two of them SCFL's own size, and none can be ruled out. But not "definitely beaten," either: not one of those five has been confirmed still running in 2026, while SCFL's evidence is live, current data as of today. What holds up, and what's genuinely rare: SCFL is one of a small number of dynasty leagues anywhere with a documented, multiply-sourced, continuous run past 20 years — and, as far as this research could tell, still standing while some of its oldest-claimed rivals may not be. That's the real story, and it's a good one.

How the League Could Actually Settle This

  1. Find one dateable artifact from 2005. The physical league trophy mentioned in the archive is the best lead — if it's engraved, photograph it. One real object beats any amount of self-reported history.
  2. Engage the rival leagues directly, and lead with "are you still running?" Start with the 16-team league since 1990, then the Mega Football League, then the rest. None has been confirmed active in 2026 — a "we folded" answer from any of them takes that rival off the board immediately, no further digging needed.
  3. Search the Wayback Machine for the league's original ESPN page. A timestamped snapshot needs no one's word.
  4. Ask the founders. Old emails, draft photos, a 2005 group text — primary testimony from people who were actually there.

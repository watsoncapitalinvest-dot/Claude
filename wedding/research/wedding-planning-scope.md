# Wedding Planning Research Brief (US, 2025–2026)
### Research to inform design of a comprehensive single-couple wedding-planning app

Key baseline stats (The Knot 2025/2026 Real Weddings Study, ~10,500 US couples): **average wedding cost $34,200**, average spend **$292/guest**, average of **13 vendors hired**, typical engagement **12–18 months**. These numbers anchor everything below.

---

## 1. The Complete Planning Timeline / Checklist

The canonical structure (The Knot, Zola, Loverly, Here Comes the Guide checklists all converge on this). An app should treat these as a task engine with dates auto-computed backward from the wedding date, adjustable for shorter engagements (Zola's guidance: with 6 months, compress all 12–6 month tasks into month one).

### 12+ months out (right after engagement)
- Celebrate; tell families; insure the ring
- Set the total budget and identify who's contributing (the single most load-bearing early task)
- Draft the guest list (rough count — drives venue size and budget)
- Choose 2–3 candidate dates and season; decide region (hometown vs. destination)
- Define style/formality/vision (mood boards; Pinterest is the dominant tool)
- Hire a wedding planner (if using one — do this right after date + guest list per Zola)
- Research and tour venues; **book the venue** (this locks the date); popular venues book 12–24 months out
- Choose the wedding party
- Start a wedding website; begin dress/attire browsing
- Consider engagement party; take engagement photos (often bundled with photographer contract)

### 9–12 months
- Book the "one-per-day" vendors: **photographer, videographer, band/DJ, planner** (photographers frequently book 12+ months ahead — Shutter & Sound, The Knot)
- Book caterer (if not in-house at venue) and officiant
- Start dress shopping seriously — **order the dress 8–10 months out** (gowns take 4–6+ months to arrive, then alterations)
- Book florist (popular florists book 12 months out for spring/fall — Zola/Shutter & Sound)
- Reserve **hotel room blocks** for out-of-town guests
- Send **save-the-dates: 6–12 months out** (12 months for destination weddings — Bliss & Bone, Joy)
- Buy wedding insurance; set up registry (needed before showers)
- Book hair/makeup artists; book rehearsal-dinner venue

### 6–9 months
- Book rentals (tables, chairs, linens, tent, dance floor), lighting, transportation, photo booth, ceremony musicians
- Order invitations and stationery; hire calligrapher if using
- Choose wedding-party attire; shop for suit/tux
- Plan honeymoon (passports! book flights/hotels)
- Cake tastings; book baker
- Finalize guest list; collect mailing addresses (an app should make address collection collaborative)
- Plan ceremony details with officiant; register for premarital counseling if required by faith
- Schedule dress fittings; buy shoes/accessories (needed for first fitting)

### 3–6 months
- Menu tasting; finalize catering menu and bar package
- Order favors, welcome bags, signage, guest book
- Book rehearsal dinner details; plan welcome party / after-party if having them
- Purchase wedding bands
- Send invitations for destination weddings (12–14 weeks out — Bliss & Bone)
- Arrange guest transportation/shuttles (book ~4 months out — WeddingWire)
- Write vows (start); choose readings and music (processional, recessional, first dance, parent dances, do-not-play list)
- Hair/makeup trial; first dress fitting
- Finalize florals proposal; decide on day-of details (escort cards vs. seating chart display)

### 1–3 months
- **Mail invitations 8–12 weeks before** the wedding; set **RSVP deadline 3–4 weeks before** (5–6 weeks for destination) (Bliss & Bone, Zola)
- Apply for **marriage license** (state rules vary wildly: in-person requirements, waiting periods up to 3 days, validity windows 30–90 days — an app should surface state-specific rules)
- Final dress fitting; break in shoes
- Write/finalize vows and toasts; confirm speakers
- Buy gifts for wedding party, parents, each other
- Create day-of timeline draft; share with vendors
- Confirm honeymoon logistics; chase RSVP stragglers after deadline
- Note: many hotels **release room blocks 4–6 weeks out** — RSVP deadline must land before block release (Bliss & Bone)

### Final month
- Send **final headcount to caterer** (usually due 1–2 weeks out per contract)
- **Finalize seating chart** once RSVPs are locked
- Confirm every vendor **in writing**: arrival times, addresses, load-in logistics, balances (Here Comes the Guide, HHH1908)
- Pay final vendor balances (most contracts: due 14–30 days before or day-of)
- Prepare **tip envelopes** with cash, labeled per vendor; assign a distributor (planner or trusted person)
- Print programs, menus, place cards; assemble welcome bags
- Pack for honeymoon; final beauty appointments (hair color, etc.)
- Delegate day-of tasks to specific people; give timeline + vendor contact sheet to wedding party and family

### Week of
- Rehearsal + rehearsal dinner (typically night before)
- Deliver welcome bags to hotels
- Steam attire; pull together emergency kit (sewing kit, stain remover, meds, snacks)
- Confirm final weather plan / rain contingency
- Hand off all décor, signage, favors to coordinator/venue

### Day of
- Hour-by-hour execution. The timeline itself is a deliverable an app should generate.

**Sample day-of timeline, 4pm ceremony with first look** (The Knot, Bliss & Bone, Loverly): 7:00am hair/makeup begins (5 people + 2 stylists ≈ 5.5 hrs) → 10:00 photographer arrives for details (dress, rings, invitation suite) → ~1:00 first look + couple portraits → wedding party + family portraits → 3:30 guests arrive/prelude → 4:00 ceremony (15–30 min secular; longer religious) → 4:30 cocktail hour → 5:30 grand entrance → dinner (~45 min) → toasts → first dance/parent dances → open dancing → cake cutting → bouquet/garter (optional) → last dance + send-off ~10–11pm (venue-dependent) → after-party. A first look moves all formal portraits before the ceremony, freeing the couple for cocktail hour.

**App implication:** the timeline is the spine. Every task should link to a budget line, a vendor, and/or a guest-list state, with dependencies (e.g., "seating chart" blocked until "RSVP deadline passed").

## 2. Budget Management

### Category percentages (Joy, Paperlust, WeddingBudgetCalc, MyWeddingKit — 2025/2026 consensus)

| Category | % of total | On $34,200 |
|---|---|---|
| Venue + catering (combined) | 40–50% | $13,700–17,100 |
| — Catering alone | 20–25% | |
| — Venue/rentals alone | 18–22% | |
| Photography + videography | 10–12% | $3,400–4,100 |
| Flowers & décor | 8–10% | $2,700–3,400 |
| Music/entertainment | 5–10% | $1,700–3,400 |
| Attire & beauty | 5–8% | $1,700–2,700 |
| Wedding planner | 5–6% | |
| Stationery & postage | 2–3% | |
| Wedding rings | 2–3% | |
| Favors & gifts | 2–3% | |
| Cake | 2% | |
| Transportation | 2% | |
| Ceremony (officiant, license, décor) | 2% | |
| **Contingency buffer** | **5–10%** | **$1,700–3,400** |

### Hidden costs couples forget (The Knot "24 Hidden Costs," Here Comes the Guide "21 Hidden Costs," 24/7 Wall St.)
- **Gratuities** — set aside 10–20% of each vendor fee; adds up across 13 vendors
- **Service charges** — venues add **15–25% on food & beverage**; this is *not* the staff tip, it covers the venue's labor costs. Couples routinely double-count or miss it
- **Sales tax** — on nearly everything; combined with tips, a common rule of thumb is to pad total costs by up to a third
- **Alterations** — a few hundred to $1,000+ beyond the dress price
- **Vendor meals** — contracts commonly require hot meals for photographers, videographers, DJ, coordinator, sometimes officiant (caterers offer reduced-price vendor meals)
- **Postage** — invitations with inserts or square envelopes need extra postage, ×2 (invite + RSVP return envelope)
- **Welcome bags**, day-of stationery reprints, marriage license fee, dress preservation/cleaning
- Overtime fees (venue, photographer, band), setup/breakdown fees, cake-cutting and corkage fees, delivery fees, trial hair/makeup sessions, insurance riders required by venue
- **App implication:** budget tool should auto-add tax + service-charge + gratuity lines per vendor, and ship with a "hidden cost" checklist pre-seeded.

### Deposit & payment norms (That's The One deposit-data guide, Chicago Style Weddings)
- Standard: **25–50% deposit at booking → optional interim payment 60–90 days out → balance due 14–30 days before or day-of**
- Never pay 100% up front; pay by **credit card** (disputable), never cash/wire/Venmo for deposits
- **App implication:** per-vendor payment schedule with due-date reminders is a killer feature — couples juggle ~13 vendors × 2–3 payments each.

## 3. Guest Management

- **Guest list building:** start with rough count pre-venue (drives everything); typical split of invite "allowances" between couple and both families; maintain A-list/B-list tiers. Collect addresses collaboratively.
- **Save-the-dates:** 6–12 months out (12 for destination); include names, date, city, website URL, "invitation to follow." **Rule: everyone who gets a save-the-date must get an invitation** (Bliss & Bone — the most-violated etiquette rule).
- **Invitations:** 8–12 weeks out (12–14 for destination). Contain full details: ceremony time/address, reception, dress code, RSVP deadline + method.
- **RSVP tracking:** deadline **3–4 weeks before** (4 if many travelers; 5–6 destination). Expect ~10–20% declines; plan a follow-up call list for non-responders. Digital RSVPs now dominant — 61% of 2026 couples use exclusively digital invitations (Digital Invitation/The Knot data), and QR-code RSVPs on printed invites are mainstream.
- **Meal choices & dietary needs:** for plated dinners the caterer needs per-person entrée selections in advance — track name, party/group, table, meal choice, dietary restrictions, plus-one status in one sortable master record (SeatPlan/Paperlust guidance). This master sheet is the deliverable sent to caterer and venue.
- **Plus-ones:** standard etiquette — married/engaged/cohabiting partners always invited by name; long-term relationships strongly encouraged; blanket plus-ones for wedding party is customary; be consistent within guest groups. Leave **2–3 buffer seats per 100 guests** for surprises (VowLaunch).
- **Seating charts:** escort cards (table assignment) vs. place cards (exact seat — needed for plated meals with entrée markers). Build only after final headcount. Walk the room: sight lines, speaker proximity, accessibility for elderly guests (SeatPlan).
- **Hotel blocks:** reserve 9–12 months out; courtesy blocks (no financial commitment, usually 10 rooms) vs. contracted blocks (attrition clauses — read carefully). Blocks **release 4–6 weeks before** the event; the RSVP deadline and website messaging must precede release.
- **App implication:** the guest is the central data object — one record flowing through: address → save-the-date sent → invited → RSVP'd → meal → hotel → table → thank-you-note sent. Every existing app fragments this; unifying it is the win.

## 4. Vendor Management

### Full vendor category list (The Knot / WeddingWire vendor checklists)
Venue (ceremony + reception), wedding planner/coordinator (full-service vs. day-of), caterer, bartending service, cake baker, photographer, videographer, florist, DJ, live band, ceremony musicians, officiant, hair stylist, makeup artist, stationer, calligrapher, rentals (tables/chairs/linens/tent/dance floor), lighting/production, transportation (couple + guest shuttles), photo booth, attire (dress salon, suit/tux, alterations specialist), jeweler (rings), decor/design, emcee, accommodations/hotel blocks, travel-honeymoon advisor, welcome-amenity supplier, plus extras (dessert bar, late-night food, childcare, security, valet). ~86% of couples book a venue; 90% hire a photographer (WeddingWire).

### Booking order & lead times (Zola, Shutter & Sound, The Knot, WeddingWire)
1. **Venue** — 12–18+ months (locks the date; peak venues 2 years)
2. **Planner** — 12+ months (before venue if full-service)
3. **Photographer** — 9–12+ months (one wedding/day; first to sell out)
4. **Videographer, band/DJ** — 8–10 months (bands need longer than DJs)
5. **Caterer** — 9–12 months (if not venue-provided)
6. **Florist** — 9–10 months (12 for spring/fall)
7. **Officiant, hair/makeup** — 8–9 months
8. **Dress ordered** — 8–10 months (production + alterations)
9. **Stationer** — 6–8 months; **cake** — 5–6 months
10. **Rentals/lighting** — 6–8 months; **transportation** — 4+ months; **photo booth** — 4–6 months

### Contract red flags (HelloPrenup, Plan A Wedding, MyWeddingKit, Chicago Style Weddings)
- Deposit over 50% with no flexibility, or demand for 100% upfront (cash-flow-strapped or scam vendor)
- **No deposit at all** (legitimate vendors always take a retainer)
- Cash-only payment
- Vague deliverables ("wedding photography" without hours, second-shooter, image count, delivery timeline)
- "Additional charges may apply" with no trigger conditions; missing tax/delivery/overtime/setup fees in the quote
- No cancellation/postponement/force-majeure terms; no substitute policy if the vendor is ill
- Slow or evasive pre-contract communication
- Protection: everything in writing, pay deposits by credit card, verify reviews across platforms

### Tipping guide (The Knot cheat sheet, Zola, Here Comes the Guide, Grand Wedding 2025)
| Vendor | Tip |
|---|---|
| Catering/waitstaff | 15–20% of F&B (check contract — often already included as "staff gratuity") |
| Photographer/videographer | $50–200 each (optional if business owner) |
| DJ | 10–15% or $50–150 |
| Band | $25–50/musician |
| Officiant | $50–100 (religious: $100–500 donation to house of worship; judges often can't accept) |
| Hair/makeup | 15–25%, like a salon |
| Delivery/setup crews (florist, cake, rentals) | $5–20/person |
| Transportation | 15–20% (check for included gratuity) |
| Planner | Not expected; ~$100–500 or gift for exceptional service |
| Venue coordinator/banquet manager | $250–500 or 1–2% of F&B |

Anti-double-tipping rule: scan contracts for "gratuity included" language. Withdraw cash and stuff labeled envelopes ~2 weeks out (Here Comes the Guide).

**App implication:** per-vendor record = contact, contract PDF, deliverables list, payment schedule, gratuity plan, day-of arrival time, meal requirement, plus a red-flag checklist at booking time.

## 5. Existing Apps — Features, Strengths, Gaps

| App | Core features | Users love | Complaints / gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Knot** | Checklist keyed to date, budgeter, guest list, free website, registry aggregation, 300k+ vendor marketplace, real-wedding content | Largest vendor directory; authoritative checklist content | **Pay-to-play vendor rankings** (top listings = biggest advertisers, not best vendors); heavy ads/upsells; vendor spam once you inquire; 2.5% cash-fund fee; dated UX (TopConsumerReviews, Nathan Tailors, InviteCount/Reddit) |
| **Zola** | Truly integrated registry + website + planning tools; invitations/paper; vendor directory | Cleanest all-in-one integration; better customer service; strong registry | Smaller vendor directory (weak outside metros); 2.5% fee on cash gifts ($125 on a $5k honeymoon fund); design "sameness"; guest-list export paywalled behaviors (Nathan Tailors, InviteCount) |
| **Joy (WithJoy)** | Free website, guest list, digital invites/RSVP, registry, planning tools | Genuinely free, no paid tier, less data-monetization feel | Only ~60 website templates vs. Zola's 1,000+; limited aesthetic range; lighter planning depth (Nathan Tailors) |
| **WeddingWire** | Vendor directory + reviews, checklists, guest/budget tools (same parent co. as The Knot) | Review volume | Same pay-to-play and ad-heavy model as The Knot |
| **Aisle Planner** | **Pro-planner B2B tool**: layered checklists, day-of timelines, drag-drop seating charts, design boards, vendor management, client portals | The gold standard for *structured planning depth* (timelines, seating, design boards) | Built for professionals, subscription-priced, overkill UX for one couple (Planning Pod, That's The One) |
| **HoneyBook** | **Vendor-side CRM**: leads, proposals, contracts, e-signature, invoicing, payments (~$32.50/mo) | Contract + payment workflow | Not a couple-facing planning tool at all (HoneyBook, EC Marketing comparisons) |

**Synthesis — what Reddit/r/weddingplanning actually says (InviteCount roundup):** couples aren't missing features, they want **fewer strings** — no vendor-marketing emails, no ads, no biased directories, no paywalled exports. The most common real-world stack is a big-platform website/RSVP **plus a hand-built spreadsheet** for budget, guest master list, and payment tracking, because no consumer app unifies those well.

**Opportunity space for a custom single-couple app:**
1. Unified guest object (address → RSVP → meal → hotel → seat → thank-you) instead of siloed tools
2. Vendor hub with contract storage, payment/deposit due-date reminders, and tip calculator — consumer apps barely touch this (it lives in HoneyBook, on the vendor's side)
3. Budget with auto tax/service-charge/gratuity lines and hidden-cost seeding
4. Dependency-aware timeline (tasks unlock/block based on real state, dates computed from wedding date)
5. Day-of timeline generator shareable with vendors
6. Zero ads, zero vendor spam, full data export

## 6. Key Early Decisions (the "first 10")

Ordered roughly by dependency (Zola "First 10 Things," The Knot, Janelle & Co):
1. **Budget + who pays** — determine firm number and contributions before touching vendors; drives everything
2. **Guest count (rough)** — second-biggest driver; at ~$292/guest, list size ≈ budget
3. **Date/season** — usually chosen as a range, finalized by venue availability; affects pricing (peak May–Oct Sat vs. off-peak/Fri/Sun discounts)
4. **Location: hometown vs. destination** — destination trades guest count for experience; requires 12-month save-the-dates, travel logistics, multi-day format
5. **Venue type/style** — determines date, capacity, catering rules, rain plan, and which vendors are allowed (preferred-vendor lists)
6. **Formality & style vision** — black-tie vs. casual; drives attire, invitations, décor, and vendor selection
7. **Wedding party size** — cost implications (gifts, bouquets, transport, hair/makeup coverage) and logistics
8. **Religious/cultural elements** — officiant eligibility, venue constraints, premarital counseling requirements, multi-ceremony formats (affects timeline and budget significantly)
9. **Planner or DIY** — full-service vs. month-of coordinator vs. self-managed
10. **Priorities exercise** — each partner picks top 2–3 categories to splurge on; everything else gets economized (this should be a first-run app flow)

## 7. 2025–2026 Trends

- **Formats:** the **wedding weekend** is mainstream — welcome party Friday, wedding Saturday, farewell brunch Sunday; destination weddings now run 3–4 days with group activities; "satellite events" (welcome parties, after-parties, morning-after brunches) treated as first-class events needing their own guest lists, invites, and timelines (TheWed 35 Trends, Here Comes the Guide, Cater-Event). Micro-weddings/elopements-plus-party remain a strong parallel track (Paperlust).
- **Colors/style:** Pantone-influenced warm earthy tones (mocha/taupe/terracotta/caramel) alongside bold jewel tones (plum, forest, sapphire, oxblood) and expressive citrus/pastel palettes; textured, "painterly," personality-driven design over minimalist neutrals (Paperlust, The Knot, Pinterest 2026 Wedding Report, Reverie Social). "Micro-luxuries": specialty welcome drinks, lounge seating, custom touches (Kennedy Blue).
- **Technology:** wedding websites are universal; **61% of 2026 couples use exclusively digital invitations**; QR codes on printed invites for RSVP, seating lookup, and guest photo-sharing; digital/audio guestbooks; live streams for remote guests; **AI planning assistants** (budget generators, vow/speech drafting, seating optimization, vendor-email drafting) called out in The Knot's 2025 Real Weddings Study as a fast-growing category (Junebug Weddings, Digital Invitation, TheWed).
- **Other notable currents:** private vows, non-traditional entrances, food-as-experience (stations, late-night snacks), sustainability (rentals, local florals), unplugged ceremonies coexisting with content-creator vendors ("wedding content creator" is now a hireable category).

**App implication:** model the wedding as **multiple events** (welcome party, rehearsal dinner, ceremony, reception, after-party, brunch) with per-event guest sublists, timelines, and budget lines — this is the structural gap in most consumer tools, which assume one event on one day.

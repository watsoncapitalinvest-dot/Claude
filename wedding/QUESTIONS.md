# 20 Questions Before We Build Your Wedding App

Answer these together — every answer becomes seed data for the app (budget model, timeline engine, venue shortlist, guest list structure). Research context is noted under each question so you can see why it matters.

## Where & When

**1. Which location is the wedding — Westchester, West Palm Beach, or still an open question?**
*Context: They're near-opposite markets. Westchester peaks Sept–Oct (foliage) at ~$300–$700/guest; Palm Beach peaks Jan–Mar (dry season) at ~$378–$458/guest. If it's genuinely undecided, the app should run a side-by-side comparison mode.*

**2. What season or specific date range are you dreaming of — and how flexible are you?**
*Context: Same venue, off-peak or Friday/Sunday, saves 15–40%. In WPB, June–Nov is hurricane season (Sept–Oct worst). In Westchester, Oct Saturdays are the hardest dates in the county.*

**3. How long do you want the engagement — when do you want to be married by?**
*Context: Marquee venues in both markets book 12–24 months out for peak Saturdays; off-peak can be done in 6–9 months.*

**4. Roughly how many guests?** (Take a first pass: must-invites vs. nice-to-invites.)
*Context: This is the single biggest budget lever — at your two markets' rates, every 10 guests is roughly $3,000–$4,600. Westchester weddings average 166–176 guests; a destination WPB wedding naturally trims the list 15–25%.*

**5. What's the all-in budget range you're comfortable with, and who's contributing?**
*Context: Realistic bands — Westchester: $60K–$110K for 125–150 guests at a named venue ($40–55K value plays exist). WPB: $40K–$65K non-resort; $150K+ at Breakers/Four Seasons tier. Pad whatever number you pick by ~25–30% for tax, service charges, and tips.*

## Money & Priorities

**6. Each of you: name your top 2–3 "splurge-worthy" categories and 2–3 you genuinely don't care about.** (Food? Band? Photography? Flowers? Dress?)
*Context: Planners call this the priorities exercise — it's the #1 tool for keeping a budget honest, and the app will use it to weight your budget allocations.*

**7. How do you feel about the trade-off between guest count and per-guest experience?** (Big party with simpler everything vs. smaller list with top-shelf everything?)

## The Venue & The Vibe

**8. What venue style makes your hearts beat faster?** Historic mansion/estate, castle, ballroom on the water, oceanfront resort, garden/museum, farm-to-table, country club — or a rooftop/loft?
*Context: Westchester's signatures: Tappan Hill Mansion, Whitby Castle, Glen Island Harbour Club, Lyndhurst, Blue Hill at Stone Barns. WPB's: The Breakers, Eau Palm Beach, Flagler Museum, Ann Norton Sculpture Gardens, Pelican Club.*

**9. Indoor ballroom or outdoor ceremony?** How much weather risk can you stomach (and does a tent/rain plan stress you out)?

**10. How formal? Black-tie, formal/cocktail, semi-formal, or relaxed?**
*Context: This decision cascades into attire, invitations, venue fit, and even ceremony time — black-tie + sunset ceremony is the current destination-glam formula in Palm Beach.*

**11. One day or a wedding weekend?** (Welcome party, rehearsal dinner, after-party, farewell brunch?)
*Context: ~31% of couples now host a welcome party. If WPB wins, guests flying in makes a multi-day format almost expected; the app would then track multiple events, each with its own guest sublist and timeline.*

## The Ceremony & The People

**12. What does the ceremony itself look like?** Religious, cultural, or secular? Officiant in mind (clergy, friend, judge)? Any traditions from either family that must be honored?
*Context: This affects venue eligibility, timeline length, and paperwork — e.g., Florida knocks $25 off the license and waives the 3-day resident wait if you take a 4-hour premarital course.*

**13. Wedding party: how big, or none at all?**
*Context: Each attendant adds real cost (hair/makeup coverage, bouquets/boutonnieres, gifts, transport) and coordination load.*

**14. Do you want a professional planner, a month-of coordinator only, or full DIY with this app as your planner?**
*Context: Month-of runs $1,500–$3,500; full-service $5K–$15K+. Note: some luxury venues (The Breakers) effectively require a professional planner.*

## The Party

**15. Food and drink: what's the vision?** Plated dinner, stations, family-style? How important is the bar (top-shelf, signature cocktails, espresso martini cart, raw bar)? Any major dietary needs in your families?
*Context: Venue+catering is 40–50% of every wedding budget — this is the heart of the spend.*

**16. Music: live band, DJ, or both — and is this a dance-all-night crowd?**
*Context: DJs run $1,500–$3,000 in both markets; bands $4,500–$15,000+. Bands are the norm at Palm Beach resorts and Westchester country clubs.*

**17. Photography & video: what style do you love?** Editorial/posed, documentary/candid, or "paparazzi-style"? Do you want videography? A content creator for same-day social clips?
*Context: Photo+video typically eats 10–12% of budget; top shooters in both markets book 12+ months out and sell out first after venues.*

## The Feeling

**18. Where do your guests live, and how much travel are you willing to ask of them?**
*Context: This is the real Westchester-vs-WPB question. WPB in peak winter means flights + $200–$1,500/night hotels for northern guests (though PBI has ~63 weekly nonstops to NYC alone); Westchester means Metro-North access and 10–20 min shuttles. The app will build hotel blocks and travel info around this.*

**19. Close your eyes: it's the morning after the wedding. What's the one thing you hope people are still talking about — and what weddings have you attended that you loved or that taught you what NOT to do?**

**20. What are you each most worried about?** (Cost creep, family dynamics, weather, decision fatigue, the seating chart…) And what would "this app made planning feel easy" actually look like for you day-to-day — shared checklist? budget alarms? one place for every contract and payment due date?
*Context: Reddit's verdict on The Knot/Zola: couples don't want more features, they want no ads, no vendor spam, and one unified place — that's exactly what a custom app can be.*

The Feature
The Week The Treatment Room Won
Week six was the lowest-scoring week of the season by nineteen points a team, and it was not because anybody got worse. It was because of who was not playing.

The sixteen rosters averaged 85.81 points in week six. In week five they averaged 105.29. That is a drop of 19.48 points a team in seven days, and it is the lowest weekly average of the season by a distance — the next lowest, week two, was 92.40.

Two of the three lowest-scoring games in the entire season were played in this one week. Gumbas and Wookie Leaks combined for 103.90, the worst of the year. The Machines and the Smoke Dragons combined for 130.74, the third worst. The game of the week was won with a score that would have lost in each of the five weeks before it.

The reason was in the chat all weekend, and almost none of it was about fantasy football. Jalen Hurts had a blood clot found in his leg. Terry McLaurin was done for the year. Mike Evans went down and nobody watching could tell how badly. Cam Skattebo was benched for fumbling. "Harbaugh basically shits on Skattebo for zero points," wrote BIG BLUE, "and 2 QBs on byes same week my only other QB has blood clots. I’m in fantasy hell." BIG BLUE lost by twenty-two to a side that had not won a game.

The Feature, continued
Seven Hurt, Two Slots
Three rosters spent the weekend working the wire around the same injuries. One of them is carrying seven injured men and seats for two.

The Horsecollars had seen it coming further ahead than anybody. "When I picked up McKee the clot was in the legs," they wrote on Monday. "Possible 3 week absence which would cover my QB bye week 8. Morphed into a worst case type scenario for Hurts." They had taken Tanner McKee off the wire days earlier on a three-week read, and then watched the three weeks become a season. McKee sat on their bench on 10.30 while Tyler Shough threw four touchdowns in his place.

Not everybody got to the wire in time. "You got semi lucky Baker had a Doubtful designation," Pork Chop Express told them. "I was locked out from picking him up." A man who has not practised in weeks carried a tag that said he might, and that was enough to put him out of reach.

And then there is Gumbas, who is carrying seven injured players on a roster with two injured reserve slots. Five of the seven are occupying active seats they cannot be moved out of: Will Shipley, Terrance Ferguson, Ty Simpson, Alec Pierce and Tyquan Thornton. He started one of them. Will Shipley, carrying an IR designation, took a running back seat and scored nothing, in a game decided by 4.42 points.

This desk published a study three days ago on whether two injured reserve slots is enough for a league this size. It found that twelve of sixteen rosters had their injured reserve full, that eighteen injured men were sitting on active rosters league-wide, and that both numbers had risen every week of the season. Week six did not change the argument. It simply played it out on the field, in the lowest-scoring Sunday anybody here has had.

The Game of the Week · THE MACHINES v Smoke Dragons
Six And Oh, And Nobody Believes It
The Machines stayed unbeaten with seventy-seven points — a score that would have beaten three teams in a normal week — and they trailed for the first fifty-eight minutes of it.

Four and a half minutes into the first quarter the Smoke Dragons went ahead on a Colston Loveland fourteen-yard touchdown catch, and they stayed ahead for the next two and three-quarter quarters. Chase McLaughlin kicked from forty-three, then forty-seven, then fifty-three. Jameis Winston threw a seventeen-yard touchdown. By the end of the first the Smoke Dragons were ten clear; midway through the second they were sixteen clear and the only unbeaten record in the Spartan division was in genuine trouble.

It ended with a minute and thirty-five seconds left in the third quarter, on a Puka Nacua forty-three-yard catch worth 4.30. That put the Machines in front 54.30 to 52.84 — the only lead change in the game — and the Smoke Dragons scored 0.30 points for the rest of the afternoon. Ryan Flournoy caught a six-yard touchdown in the fourth. Rachaad White caught a nine-yard touchdown with two minutes and thirteen seconds left. The margin flattered it.

Seventy-seven points is the Machines’ lowest of the season by 28.54, and it is worth being precise about how low. In each of the five weeks before this one, between three and five of the sixteen teams scored below 77.60. It is a score that normally beats a quarter of the league and loses to the rest. In week six it beat seven, because week six was the week nobody scored.

Both men saw it coming. "I don’t think I’m breaking 70 this week," the Machines wrote on Sunday evening. "Fortunately for you," came the reply from the Smoke Dragons, "60 would have been more than enough." It was. The Machines are 6-0 and have now won a game while being outscored by thirteen of the other fifteen rosters.

Gumbas v Wookie Leaks
The Lowest Game Anyone Has Played
Wookie Leaks beat Gumbas 54.16 to 49.74 in a game that produced fewer points than any other this season. It turned on a seat, not a play.

The two sides combined for 103.90 points, which is the lowest total in any game this season — 7.50 below the previous worst. Wookie Leaks went ahead on a George Holani three-yard run with 12:54 left in the first quarter, worth three tenths of a point, and that was the only lead change in sixty minutes of football.

Gumbas had five starters finish under three points: Will Shipley on nought, Cody White on nought, Juwan Johnson on nought, Jalen Nailor on 0.90 and Ty Johnson on 2.70. Matthew Stafford threw two second-half touchdowns and George Kittle caught an eleven-yard touchdown with 6:33 left in the fourth to make it close. It finished 4.42 short.

The 12.40 points Caleb Williams scored on the Gumbas bench would have covered that nearly three times over. He is a quarterback and the superflex seat he would have filled was occupied by Juwan Johnson, who scored nothing — a legal swap, available all week, worth more than the margin. The best lineup available to Gumbas scores 62.14 and wins this game by eight.

Gumbas had said on Sunday afternoon that something had gone wrong: "6 guys on IR, 2 bye — was thinking to leave a guy out anyway instead of dropping," followed by "Now I got to shoot a 90 today." Pork Chop Express was unmoved. "No one cares what you shoot. Just fill your lineup." The Machines was briefer: "This public shaming is really all we have available to us in this situation." Gumbas has now played in two of the four closest games of the season and lost both.

Lil’ Chops v StillTheCream
Six Lead Changes Before The First Quarter Ended
StillTheCream and Lil’ Chops traded the lead seven times. Six of them came inside two minutes of the opening quarter, and the seventh decided it.

Nothing in this league has been as busy as the first quarter of this game. With seven minutes left in it, a Ka’imi Fairbairn forty-nine-yard field goal put StillTheCream ahead by four tenths of a point. Thirty-three seconds later a Garrett Wilson fifteen-yard catch took it back. Then an Aaron Rodgers interception, then a Bucky Irving six-yard run, then the same Bucky Irving losing five yards, then a Tyler Loop extra point — six lead changes between 07:00 and 05:00 of the first quarter, the last of them leaving Lil’ Chops ahead by two tenths.

Then Rodgers threw two touchdowns and Lil’ Chops built a lead of nearly twenty-seven, and the game looked finished before half-time.

It was not. Tre Tucker caught an eleven-yard touchdown with four and a half minutes left in the third and a forty-four-yard touchdown with a minute and a half left in it, and those two plays alone were worth 17.50. That brought StillTheCream to within 2.88. Fifty seconds into the fourth quarter Jordan Love threw a nine-yard touchdown and the seventh lead change of the afternoon arrived: 63.16 to 58.04. It was the last one.

StillTheCream have now won three in a row having lost their first three, which is the sharpest turn anyone has managed this season. Lil’ Chops left 18.20 points on their bench, and their best legal lineup — the same men, better seated — scores 93.92 and wins this comfortably.

Beaver Eaters v Horsecollars
Called It On Thursday
The Beaver Eaters said on Thursday that a thirty-two point lead would hold. It held, the Horsecollars never led, and the league’s other unbeaten record is still intact.

"I’ll take a 32 pt lead after Thursday," the Beaver Eaters wrote before the weekend started. "At least with these decimal points, collars won’t catch a lucky W with his bench points." They were ahead 15.60 to nothing before twelve minutes of the first quarter had gone, and the Horsecollars did not lead at any stage of the afternoon.

That is not to say the Horsecollars were not scoring. Tyler Shough threw four touchdowns and finished on 30.10, the second-highest individual score of the week. Jonathan Taylor added 19.10. For a stretch of the second quarter the Horsecollars outscored the Beavers heavily — from 71.06 to 42.58 up to 83.62 to 69.68 — and got the margin down to under fourteen.

Drake Maye kept answering. He threw a forty-two-yard touchdown in the first, a nine-yarder and a seven-yarder in the second, and finished on 25.10. Bryce Young added 18.20 and Malik Nabers a forty-six-yard catch with 1:20 left in the half that pushed it back over twenty.

"I love beating the collars," the Beavers wrote afterwards. "Long time nemesis." They are 6-0 and top of the table on points for. The Horsecollars started 4-0 and have now lost two in a row.

Phins Phag v BIG BLUE
The First One
Phins Phag had lost five. They led this from the first scoring play of the game and were seventy-two points clear before half-time.

"I think I’m getting my first W today in a very long time," Phins Phag wrote on Sunday evening, which was underselling it. They had led since a Jason Myers fifty-five-yard field goal with 13:15 left in the first quarter and they never gave the lead back. There were no lead changes in this game at all.

By 5:16 of the second quarter it read 72.22 to 0.00. Omar Cooper had caught a forty-eight-yard touchdown, Hunter Henry a seven-yard one, Chuba Hubbard had run in from the one and Cam Ward had thrown an eight-yard touchdown. BIG BLUE’s first scoring play of any size did not arrive until the fourth quarter.

There is an explanation and it is a grim one. BIG BLUE started Jalen Hurts, who did not play, and Cam Skattebo, who did not play — a blood clot and a benching in the same week, on top of two quarterbacks on bye. Christian McCaffrey’s 21.70 and two one-yard touchdowns in the fourth were a tidy-up operation on a scoreline already decided.

Chuba Hubbard finished on 18.10 and Omar Cooper on 16.30. Phins Phag are 1-5 and off the bottom of nothing — they are still sixteenth — but the number in the left-hand column has changed for the first time this season.

Killer Klowns v Guido Haters
Twenty-Six Points On The Bench, And It Did Not Matter
The Killer Klowns left more on their bench than anybody in the league and won by twenty-three anyway, because the Guido Haters are in the middle of the longest losing run of the season.

The Klowns left 25.76 points on their bench, the most of any roster this week, and still won by 22.80. That is the sort of margin for error only one side in this game had.

It was a real contest for three quarters. The lead changed five times, the last of them with fifty-three seconds left in the third, when Kirk Cousins threw an eleven-yard touchdown to put the Guido Haters ahead 67.56 to 67.24. Cousins added a forty-four-yard touchdown pass twenty-one seconds later and the Haters led by better than seven going into the fourth.

They did not score again. Jayden Daniels threw a one-yard touchdown with 3:30 left and a nine-yard touchdown with 1:48 left, the Klowns closed on 96.24, and the Haters finished exactly where that third-quarter run had left them.

Bhayshul Tuten took 20.70 off two first-quarter touchdown catches, Daniels finished on 19.00 and Chris Boswell kicked 18.00 from fifty-two and fifty-eight. The Guido Haters have now lost five in a row and sit fifteenth.

Skirt Chasers · Week 6 · 2026
The Powers of Pain v Master-Jeti
Nine Lead Changes And A Hundred And Thirty-One
The highest score of the week came out of the week’s wildest game. The Powers of Pain and Master-Jeti swapped the lead nine times and then one of them simply left.

Nine lead changes is the most in any game this season. The first came with 5:24 left in the opening quarter, on a C.J. Stroud six-yard touchdown pass. The ninth came with 14:43 left in the third, on a Kenneth Walker thirteen-yard run that put Master-Jeti ahead 66.52 to 65.84. In between, neither side led by more than fourteen.

Master-Jeti had the better individual afternoon and lost by thirty-one. Trevor Lawrence threw for 29.20, the highest single score by anybody in the league this week. Kenneth Walker ran for 25.90, including a thirty-three-yard touchdown. DeVonta Smith added 13.50. Three men over thirteen points and it was not close by the end.

What settled it was a run between the eighth minute of the third quarter and the thirteenth of the fourth. Master-Jeti led 88.44 to 80.64 at the first of those marks and trailed 111.14 to 97.50 at the second — a swing of better than twenty-one points with Master-Jeti barely moving. Matthew Golden’s nine-yard touchdown catch sat at the end of it, and Brock Purdy’s eleven-yard touchdown pass with 5:50 left made the final number.

C.J. Stroud finished on 22.70, Kyle Pitts on 18.00 with two touchdown catches inside the last eighty seconds of the first half. Both teams are now 3-3, which after a game like that feels like an administrative error.

n.W.o v Pork Chop Express
Fifty-Four Points, Start To Finish
Pork Chop Express took the lead on the first scoring play and won by the biggest margin of the week. Four straight now, and a division decider on Sunday.

This was over early and stayed over. The first listed swing of the game — a D’Andre Swift four-yard touchdown run with 4:19 left in the opening quarter — already had Pork Chop Express ahead 24.32 to 5.70, and n.W.o did not lead at any point.

By the middle of the second quarter it was 89.08 to 41.10. Michael Penix threw three touchdowns inside eight minutes of that quarter, Chris Olave caught an eighteen-yard touchdown and Isaiah Likely a twenty-four-yarder for the losing side. Josh Allen threw two touchdowns in the third and James Cook ran one in with 1:57 left in it, by which point it was 128.28 to 71.48.

Penix finished on 24.60, Cook on 21.30 and Swift on 19.00 — three men over nineteen in the same lineup. The 54.14 margin is the widest of the week and Pork Chop have won four in a row since their week two defeat.

They are 5-1 and second in Black and Blue. The side above them is unbeaten, and the two of them meet on Sunday.

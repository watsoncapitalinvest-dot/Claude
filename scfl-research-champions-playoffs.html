Champions & Playoffs: The Full Dataset Behind the Last Two Ledger Pieces

Compiled by The Record Room · For internal handoff — a writer turns this into the next piece, not us · Sources: history.json (2008–2025 seasons, standings, and matchups)

This is the research, not the article. It backs the two published pieces — "Champions Don't Win It. Losers Get Handed It." We Checked and Once You're In, Do You Win It — Or Do They Just Lose It? — but it's organized here as raw tables and method notes, not a thesis. Everything below is real, sourced, and checkable. The read on what it means is at the bottom, clearly marked as ours and not the last word.

Scope & Method

  • Universe: all 18 SCFL championships on file, 2008–2025, and every playoff game the eventual champion played each of those years (53 games total).
  • Champion identification: history.json's champion field is an owner handle, not a team name — matched to that season's roster of teams by owner. Verified for every year.
  • Regular-season baseline (PPG): each team's points-for divided by games played (wins+losses+ties) that season — the number used for every "delta" figure in Table B.
  • Win-rank / PF-rank (Table A): the champion's rank among all teams that season by regular-season record and by total points scored, computed independently of playoff results.
  • Classification (Table A): JUGGERNAUT = top-2 in the league in both wins and points. BACKDOOR = a losing or .500 record, or bottom-half in either wins or points. MIDDLING = everything between.
  • Title-path games (Table B): every playoff game the eventual champion appears in, in order. Because the champion won the league, every game they're in on the path counts as a win by definition — except one.
  • The 2017 exception: that year's champion played the Machines twice in a three-week bracket, splitting the results (won R1, lost the Machines game, won the Machines rematch). The loss is a real result but doesn't fit the "title-path win" table and is excluded from Table B and all its averages. Flagged, not hidden — worth a call if anyone wants to understand the 2017 bracket structure before publishing.
  • Category tags (Table B): GIVEAWAY = champion scored below their own season average and won anyway. COLLAPSE-DRIVEN = both teams' deltas were negative-leaning or the opponent's drop was larger than the champion's gain. SURGE-DRIVEN = the champion's gain over their own norm was the bigger number. The line between the last two is a simple magnitude comparison, shown so it can be re-checked or re-drawn.

Research Team Notes

Observations, not conclusions. Whoever writes this up should feel free to land somewhere we didn't.

  • The two most recent champions are also the only two "backdoor" champions on file. That could be a real trend, a rules/seeding change worth tracking down, or just two data points — the sample is too small to call it from this side of the desk. Worth asking the league office whether playoff seeding or format changed around 2023-2024.
  • Even the cleanest dynasties have at least one bad-day win in their ledger. Big Blue (every year, 2021-23), the Machines (2012, 2016, 2018) — teams we'd otherwise call unambiguous juggernauts each needed one game where they underperformed their own season average and won purely on the other side collapsing. Whether that undercuts the "juggernaut" label or is just what happens over a 3-round bracket is an editorial call, not a data one.
  • Sample size caution: 53 games, 11 of them giveaways. That's a real pattern, not noise, but it's thin enough that one or two future seasons could move the percentages a lot. Worth a light touch on any claim about where this is "trending."
  • The averages-vs-frequency split (this packet's central tension) is worth leading with or burying, depending on the angle — on strict average magnitude, winner-surge and loser-collapse are statistically identical (+13.75 / −14.08). Only when you count games one at a time does the loser's-collapse side pull ahead (64%). Which framing is "more true" is a legitimate editorial choice, not something the data settles by itself.
  • Not checked yet: whether giveaway/collapse games cluster by round (semifinal vs. final), by matchup type (division rival vs. not), or by any roster/injury signal. All three are answerable from data already in this repo if the next piece wants to go there.
  • Voice note: the two published Ledger pieces already took positions (the first argued a real split with a recent-trend lean; the second leaned toward "usually lost, not won"). This packet doesn't require agreeing with either — it's the same underlying numbers, reusable for a different angle entirely.

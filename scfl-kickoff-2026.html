THE DEFENDING CHAMPION
The Champion Who Lost More Than He Won
The Powers of Pain won the 2025 title from the top seed with a 7-7 record and the fewest points of any team in the field. The format handed him the door. He walked through it.

Here is the 2025 season in one line. The Powers of Pain finished 7-7. He scored 1,183 points, fewer than any other team that made the playoffs and fewer than four teams that missed. He was the number one seed. He is the champion.

None of that is a scandal, and none of it is an accident. It is the format doing exactly what the format was built to do, and it is worth understanding before anybody sets a lineup this year.

How It Happens

Eight teams qualify: the four division winners, plus two wild cards from each conference. Win your division and you are in, and you are seeded above every wild card no matter what the record says. The Powers of Pain won the Spectre Syndicate at 7-7 in a division where the Guido Haters also finished 7-7 and Still The Cream finished 2-12. Somebody had to win it.

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026

The reward for winning it was the top seed in the John McClane Conference and a bracket that never asked him to be the best team in the league — only the best team in three particular weeks.

He was. That is the whole point of a postseason, and anyone who wants to be sour about it should look at the 2024 champion, the Killer Klowns, who came out of the seven hole.

What It Means For 2026

Two things, and they pull in opposite directions.

The first is that a division title is worth more in this league than in almost any comparable format. It is not a tiebreaker or a banner — it is a seed, and the seed is the whole tournament. Four of the sixteen teams in this league will get in on a technicality this year. Do not spend December scoreboard-watching for a wild card if you can simply win four games in your own division.

The second is that the Powers of Pain has to do it again from a harder position. He is no longer the surprise. He has the trophy, he has the target, and he is in a division with the Guido Haters, who matched his record last year, and a Still The Cream side that just drew the best draft grade in the league.

The Record Says

This is a franchise that knows how to be there. Four title-game appearances, three of them won — the best conversion rate of any manager who has been to more than one. The Machines has eleven finals and four rings; the Powers of Pain has four finals and three.

He does not get there often. He wins when he does.

THE NAME CHANGE
Two Wins, Eight Hundred and Twenty-One Points, One New Name
The Heavy Hitters and Still The Cream both finished 2-12. The tiebreaker went to points, and the Hitters lost it by a distance that is difficult to look at.

The oldest rule in this league is also the only punishment in it that outlives the season. Finish last and you are renamed for the following year, and the league votes on what you get called.

The 2025 race for it came down to two teams and did not resolve until the points were counted.

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026
The Tiebreak

The Heavy Hitters finished 2-12. Still The Cream finished 2-12. Level on the only number that usually matters, the question went to points scored, and it was not close: the Hitters put up 821, the Cream 1,028.

That 821 is the number to sit with. The next-worst total in the league was 1,003 — the Smoke Dragons, who at least had five wins to show for it. The Heavy Hitters were 182 points adrift of fifteenth place. Across a fourteen-game season that is thirteen points a week, every week, of daylight between them and the second-worst offence in the SCFL.

So the name change is theirs.

The Cruel Part

The Hitters did not have a bad draft. The Powers of Pain graded their 2026 class a B+, tied for the fifth-best mark on a board of fourteen — level with the Machines, and ahead of the Express, the Gumbas and the Collars.

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026

The cruel part is who they finished level with. Still The Cream also went 2-12 — and drew an A+, the best grade POP handed out all summer. Two teams tied at the bottom, one walks away with the best draft class in the league and his name intact, and the other takes the rename with a perfectly respectable haul.

That is the rule working as designed. It is not meant to be fair. It is meant to be memorable.

What Comes Next

The league votes. History says the vote is not kind and the name is not chosen to flatter — the SCFL has been doing this for the better part of twenty years, and the archive is full of franchises who spent a season answering to something they would not repeat in front of their children.

The consolation, such as it is, arrives in the spring: last place takes the 1.01. As The Machines once put it, getting the number one pick helps a little.

It has helped before. It is not helping the Heavy Hitters get their name back.

THE GROUP OF DEATH
Everything Good Is in One Division
The three highest-scoring teams in the SCFL last season all play in Black and Blue. Two of them went 11-3. They have to keep playing each other.

Sort last season’s sixteen teams by points scored and stop at three. The Pork Chop Express, 1,531. Big Blue, 1,490. The Beaver Eaters, 1,466.

All three are in Black and Blue. They play each other twice a year.

The Math Of It

Six games a season are divisional, which means a Black and Blue team spends nearly half its schedule against the best-scoring group in the league. The Express and Big Blue both finished 11-3, the two best records in the SCFL, and they did it while beating each other up.

The Beaver Eaters are the exhibit. They scored the third-most points in the league — more than the eventual champion by 283 — and finished 7-7. On any other schedule that is a comfortable playoff team. In Black and Blue it was ninth seed and an early exit.

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026
And The Fourth Team

Wookie Leaks also went 7-7 in there, which in the circumstances is a good deal better than it looks, and he has drawn an A for his 2026 draft. So has Big Blue.

Which is the genuinely alarming part for the rest of the league. The division that already had the three best offences just added two of the three best draft classes.

The Stakes

Only one of these four wins the division, and the seed that comes with it. The other three are fighting for two Cobra Kai wild cards against a Four Horsemen field that includes Horse Collars at 10-4 and the New World Order at 9-5.

Somebody very good is going to miss the playoffs out of this division. Possibly two somebodies.

It is also the division with the most recent history of settling things properly. Big Blue and the Beaver Eaters have met six times in the postseason — second only to Big Blue and The Machines — and the Beavers have taken four of the six despite trailing the all-time series 10-15.

That is the shape of Black and Blue. Enormous scoring, no room, and a postseason record of the bigger name losing.

SPECIAL REPORT — THE RIVALRY AUDIT
The Grudge Report
Twenty-one franchises, 2,344 games, eighteen seasons of matchups. Who actually has a rivalry in this league, who has a tormentor, and which of the great grudges turn out to be invented.

This league has always described itself in grudges. Ask any manager who his rival is and you will get an answer in under a second, delivered with total confidence and no evidence whatsoever.

So the Newsroom went and counted. Every matchup in the league’s records — eighteen seasons, twenty-one franchises, 2,344 games — reconciled against the standings until the two agreed exactly. Then we counted something else: who actually talks to whom, and how often it stops being friendly.

The results do not flatter everybody, and one of the things they did was demolish a couple of the stories this desk was ready to print.

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026
The Method

A rivalry needs three things, and this desk scored all three. You have to play each other a lot. The series has to be close enough to be worth arguing about. And you have to actually engage — measured as back-and-forth in the chat, normalised so the quiet managers aren’t punished for being quiet and the loud ones don’t win on volume.

Two facts about how this league is built had to be respected, and ignoring either one produces nonsense. The first is the schedule. Sixteen teams, two conferences, two divisions of four. You play your own division twice — six games — the other division in your conference once, and four more scattered across the other conference to fill the card. Which means the size of a head-to-head sample is mostly a fact about the schedule rather than about the two men involved: share a division for a decade and you will pile up thirty games without either of you choosing it. Shared history here is counted in seasons spent in the same division, not in raw games.

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026

The second is the postseason. Eight teams qualify — the four division winners plus two wild cards from each conference — and then all sixteen keep playing for three more weeks, the bottom half in a consolation ladder that decides nothing. The records flag every one of those as a playoff game. They are not. A playoff meeting in this report means both teams qualified. That is twelve games a season, not twenty-four, and sorting it properly is what killed two of the rivalries this desk was ready to print.

Note also what is deliberately absent. We counted how often exchanges turn sharp, because that says something about a pairing. We are not printing what was said. This league has been friends for thirty years, and the worst thing anyone typed at eleven o’clock on a Sunday night is not evidence of anything except a bad week.

The Tormentor

Start with the finding that reorganises everything else. The Machines does not have a rivalry with most of this league. He has victims.

Against the Killer Klowns he is 26-1. Against Still The Creamiest, 30-4. Against the Smoke Dragons, 18-3. Against the Hairy Gumbas, 17-3. Against Wookie Leaks, 17-3. Against the Beaver Eaters, 10-2.

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026

That is six franchises and 134 games at a combined 118-16, with a points margin north of four thousand. The loudest arguments in this league are not between equals — they are the sound of six men losing to the same person for the better part of two decades.

It holds up in the postseason too, where it counts. He has met the Smoke Dragons five times in the playoffs and won all five.

The Record

And to be clear about what that adds up to, because the standings deserve the credit: The Machines has won four championships. Nobody else has more than three. He has also played in eleven of the eighteen title games on record, which is not a statistic about luck. It means that in more than half the seasons this league has existed, he was the last or second-to-last man standing.

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026

He has lost seven of those eleven. That is the line people reach for, and it is worth being careful with. A man who reaches the final eleven times is going to lose finals; the seven managers who beat him were each having the best season of their lives, and a fantasy final is one week of scoring in which the better team loses all the time. The Machines has more rings than anyone and more finals than anyone by a distance of seven. Those are the facts in the right order.

The Best Rivalry In The League

Which brings us to the one pairing that has everything, and it is not close.

The Machines and Big Blue have met **seven times in the playoffs — more than any other pair of franchises in league history** — and Big Blue has won four of the seven. The regular-season series belongs to The Machines at 16-13 across twenty-nine games, which is exactly the shape a real rivalry should have: one man edges the long haul, the other takes the games that matter.

In 2023 Big Blue beat him in the final, 125.5 to 100. It was the middle jewel of a three-peat — 2021, 2022, 2023 — and it makes Big Blue three-for-three in title games, the only perfect record in the building. He has also won the last five meetings between them outright.

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026

One man is 118-16 against six franchises. Against the seventh he is 3-4 when it counts. That is a rivalry, and it is the only one he has.

The Purest

If you want the pairing that behaves most like a rivalry is supposed to, it is the Pork Chop Express and the Smoke Dragons.

The series is 9-7 across sixteen games, a margin of 154 points over nine seasons — close enough that either man can claim it depending on the day. What separates it is the engagement: 21.6% of all the back-and-forth these two have in the chat is with each other.

Only one pairing engages more, and it proves the point rather than spoiling it. The Machines and the Smoke Dragons run at 23.4% — the highest mutual figure in the SCFL — and that series is 18-3. Men talk most at the person beating them. Set aside the pairings where somebody is simply losing, and the Express and the Dragons are the most engaged evenly-matched teams in the building, by a distance.

The Old Marriages

The longest series in league history belongs to two men nobody thinks of as rivals at all: the Powers of Pain and the Guido Haters have played forty-five times, splitting them 24-21, with four postseason meetings on top. And it is not an accident — they have been divisional rivals in sixteen of their eighteen seasons together, which is the longest continuous division pairing in the league. Two men scheduled against each other twice a year for the better part of two decades, and almost nothing said about any of it.

Close behind: the New World Order and Horse Collars, 41 games and sixteen shared divisional seasons; the New World Order and the Beaver Eaters, 37 games and a series standing at 19-18 — one game apart after seventeen seasons together, five of their meetings in the playoffs. And Horse Collars and the Beaver Eaters, 35 games, thirteen of them divisional years, 19-16 to the Beavers.

These are the old marriages of the SCFL. Enormous shared history, almost no heat, and a scoreline that refuses to separate.

The Postseason Specialists

One franchise has quietly made a habit of ending Big Blue’s Januarys. The Beaver Eaters have met him six times in the playoffs — second only to The Machines–Big Blue file — and have taken four of the six, despite trailing the overall series 10-15.

That is the postseason inverting a regular-season order, which is the rarest thing in these records and the only honest version of a giant-killing in the league.

The Ones That Weren’T There

Two rivalries this desk fully expected to write did not survive the counting, and they are worth reporting as corrections rather than quietly dropping.

The Heavy Hitters and Still The Creamiest look, at first glance, like the great unspoken feud: twenty-seven games and a series sitting at 14-13. Sort the postseason properly and they have met four times, and split them 2-2. The Hairy Gumbas and the Heavy Hitters, who appeared to have a postseason stranglehold, have never met in a playoff game at all. Every one of those meetings was in the consolation bracket, which is to say none of them were playing for anything.

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026

There is a lesson in that for everybody who tells rivalry stories, this desk very much included. Half the grudges in a fantasy league are two teams who kept running into each other in week fifteen while both seasons were already over.

The Quiet Ones

Two pairings deserve mention precisely because nobody argues about them.

The Jet-I and the Smoke Dragons have played twenty-nine times and the series sits at 15-14. Dead level across a decade and a half, and it has never once become a talking point.

And the tightest series in the entire dataset, by the only measure that cannot be argued with: Lamb Chops versus The Machines. Nine games, and a cumulative points difference of four. Not four a game — four in total, with Lamb Chops holding a 6-3 edge. The newest manager in the league and the most decorated, separated by a rounding error, and neither of them has ever mentioned it.

The Half-Point Games

Two playoff games in league history have been decided by half a point: the Killer Klowns over the Jet-I in 2024, and Big Blue over the Pork Chop Express in 2018. Somewhere in each of those weeks a man set a lineup on a Sunday morning and never learned how close it came.

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026
The Verdict

The league’s own account of itself turns out to be about half right.

The rivalries people talk about loudest are mostly real, and the Express and the Dragons are exactly as advertised. The best of them all is The Machines and Big Blue, which has the games, the stakes, the title and the current streak. But the most remarkable series in the book — forty-five games between the Powers of Pain and the Guido Haters — belongs to two men who have never made a noise about it, and two of the feuds this desk believed in turned out to be consolation football.

SPECIAL FEATURE — THE HILL STANDOFF
The Man Who Would Not Sell
Five years of offers for Tyreek Hill, a deal that died at midnight, and the argument the Hairy Gumbas are still winning in their own defence.

On the afternoon of August 9, 2026, the Hairy Gumbas were complaining about their roster in the league chat, which is not unusual, when they said the quiet part out loud. “Lost tons of value last few years due to injury,” the Gumbas wrote. “Chubb Hill Kittle. Guys basically shot dead fantasy wise in one play lol. Drop to 0 value instantly.”

Within two minutes three franchises had converged on the obvious rebuttal, and the SCFL had reopened the longest-running argument in its trade history. “You have to trade people before that happens,” The Machines said. The Pork Chop Express was less clinical: “You should have sold Hill for a truckload. Same with Chubb, Elliot. Tannehill. I know you were trying to win. But when guys get old, you sell to a contender and move on.”

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026
The Purchase

In the summer of 2021 the Gumbas traded the fifth overall pick to the New World Order and got back Tyreek Hill and Sterling Shepard. The Powers of Pain graded it a B and called it right: “With a win now squad that is solid at the QB position Gumbas makes the wise decision to trade out and pick up a top 5 WR in Tyreek Hill.” It was a good trade. It stayed a good trade for two seasons, and nothing in this story changes that.

The Courtship

What followed was the longest sustained sales pitch in league history. The Smoke Dragons opened talks in April 2023, came back in June, came back in July with a package built around Brandon Aiyuk, and came back in August offering two 2024 first-round picks plus Kadarius Toney. By September the Dragons had reduced the argument to a single claim, put directly to the Gumbas: Hill would never again be worth more than he was worth right then. Nick Chubb had just gone from franchise cornerstone to untradeable contract in the length of one tackle, and that was the comparison on offer.

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026
Deadline Day, 2023

On November 29, with the Gumbas at two wins and eliminated, Hill went on the block around ten at night. The market that formed in the next four hours was the strongest he would ever have. Wookie Leaks was up before dawn working on it — “I’m only up this early bc I’m trying to get Tyreek from Tommy” — with an offer that “hovered around a 25 1st, 24 2nd and a wr.” The Machines were in briefly. The Pork Chop Express got close enough that The Machines reported: “I just know rumors are pretty solid deal going to the Gumbas. If people stay awake.” The Powers of Pain was in the market too, with picks two and three years out.

The Dragons’ Bid

The Smoke Dragons made the largest offer and disclosed it publicly the next morning: “I offered him 2024 1, 2026 1, 2024 2, 2024 2, Shultz, Toney — for hill and kittle.” When that stalled, they stripped it down: “I widdled it down to hill alone for all of the pieces minus schultz and toney. But still no response.”

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026

It got closer than any public account has recorded. At one stage the two sides were a single second-round pick apart on a deal sending Hill and George Kittle to the Dragons for A.J. Brown and Dalton Schultz. The Gumbas offered to drop that pick to close it. The Dragons declined, unwilling to absorb the combined age of a 29-year-old receiver and a 30-year-old tight end. Both men were right about the same problem from opposite ends of it, and neither would move four inches.

Midnight

The Dragons restructured once more and sent a final offer built around Najee Harris, Jaylen Warren, Jahan Dotson, Toney and a 2024 second before going to bed. It was never answered. The Gumbas manager had worked a twelve-hour day building a deck, driven eighty minutes home, coached a two-hour basketball practice and put his kids to bed before opening the trade chat at midnight. He fell asleep. “Agreed to a trade, and somebody else fell asleep,” The Machines noted the next day. The Pork Chop Express was less generous: “Tommy found the best deal to be from an owner with narcolepsy.”

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026

The deadline passed with nothing. “Deadline came and went with no big late moves,” the Pork Chop Express observed. “Couple teams could have really taken advantage of the market being without a seller.” The Dragons: “And in the end no trade happened anyway. ha ha.”

The Price That Never Came

The New World Order diagnosed the impasse precisely: “Think the Gumbas were hoping for a top 8 pick in the 2024 draft and nobody was offering it.” The Gumbas’ answer, the day after, was that the market would return: “Ok last post on hill. He will be for sale in the summer 2024. Lol. For the right price.” The New World Order put a date on it: “Hill will get traded 100% on July 4, 2024.”

He was not traded on July 4, 2024. The Dragons asked again that September and got no deal. He was not traded in 2025. By September 2024 the Powers of Pain was already writing the obituary as a punchline — “Is Tyreek Hill even worth anything anymore? He’s on to his third QB” — and in September 2025 the Pork Chop Express filed the four-word version: “Tyreek Hill. Season over.” In February 2026 Wookie Leaks posted the ending: “Dolphins releasing Tyreek.” He remains a Hairy Gumba.

The Machines’ Offer

One more approach belongs in the file, and it frames the argument more cleanly than anything else in it. With Hill and George Kittle both thirty, The Machines offered T.J. Hockenson, then twenty-seven, and George Pickens, then twenty-three, along with a 2024 pick, for the pair of them. The offer was written out in ages rather than in names — two players on the near side of the cliff for two players already over it. It was not taken.

The Reckoning

Which brings us back to August 9, 2026, and the part of this story the Gumbas get to write. Pressed on why the offers were never taken, they gave an answer of two words: “Weak imo trades lol.” The Pork Chop Express: “The gumbas would be dominating.”

The Defence

And here the case against them gets considerably weaker, because the Gumbas did something nobody else in this argument bothered to do. They went and looked up what the picks actually became.

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026

The Powers of Pain’s package for Hill was built on selections two and three years out. Traced forward, the Gumbas found the 2025 fifth became Travis Hunter and the 2024 fifteenth became Trey Benson — a two-way rookie whose fantasy value the chat openly doubts, and a running back buried fourth on the Arizona depth chart. “So trey Benson travis Hunter And a guy who doesn’t play anymore and had 530 yards total for 2023-2024 For number1 wr in the league,” the Gumbas wrote. “Glad I didn’t make that one pop.”

That is not a rationalisation. That is the single strongest piece of evidence in this entire file, and it belongs to the defence. The Gumbas have argued for years that mid-to-late first-round picks are lottery tickets dressed as assets, and on that specific offer, from that specific team, they were demonstrably correct. They also made the point that the picks in question were years out and unknowable: “Was also trading for picks 2-3 years down the road, no way to know what number pics they were gonna be.” “Would you take 2028 picks from good teams for the number one wide receiver now?” they asked. “It’s not a no brainer trade.”

Skirt Chasers · The Kickoff Issue · August 2026
The Pattern

The trouble for the Gumbas is that Hill is not the only exhibit. On the same afternoon it emerged that The Machines had once offered Aaron Rodgers and two first-round picks for Ryan Tannehill, and been turned down. “Guess should have given you Rodgers for Tannehill,” the Gumbas conceded. “But at the time he might have not continue playing.” The Pork Chop Express supplied the epilogue: “That deal was Roger’s and 2 firsts for Tannehill. Tannehill is pumping gas.”

The Machines, who has won more than anyone in this league, drew the structural conclusion: “Tommy’s team probably has the most players that I’ve just been on one single team of anybody I can remember. Very little movement of big players. Just lifelong Goomba’s.” To which the Gumbas replied, with no defensiveness at all: “The way we like it lol.”

That is the actual disagreement, and it is not really about Tyreek Hill. The Machines also volunteered a statistic worth more than any single trade: “There is a direct correlation between the teams that do the most trades and the teams with best records last 5 years.” The Gumbas are a roster of players who arrived and never left, run by a man who likes it that way, in a league where movement correlates with winning.

The Objection

The Gumbas have one more argument, and it is aimed squarely at this article. Told that the Newsroom had traced every pick and every offer, they pushed back on the method itself: “You can’t just post trade offers and say should’ve taken that trade or not taking that trade — a lot more goes into it.” And: “Hindsight goes both ways.” They also noted, correctly, that a machine can be made to say whatever its operator wants.

So let the record state what it can and cannot support. It cannot show that the Gumbas were offered a haul and refused it out of stubbornness; on at least one package, the picks turned to dust and they were right to pass. It cannot show they had any way of knowing Chubb’s knee, Hill’s decline or Kittle’s odometer. What it does show is ten documented approaches from five franchises over three years, a deal that reached a handshake and died over a single second-round pick, a final offer that went unanswered because a working father fell asleep at midnight, and a receiver who has now been released by his NFL team and is still, in August 2026, on the Hairy Gumbas roster.

The Verdict

This was never a story about a bad manager. The Gumbas were right about late first-rounders, right that Kittle had years left, right not to be stampeded by a chat that wanted their best player at a discount, and right that hindsight is cheap. The Powers of Pain, who has watched this franchise for twenty years, put it more kindly than any of the accountants: “The Gumbas are the Cincinnati Bengals to me. Need to get a 🏆 for Caleb.”

But there is no such thing as holding an asset. There is only choosing when to sell it, and if you do not choose, time chooses for you — and time has never once negotiated in good faith. Nine times the league came to the door with a number. Nine times the answer was no, or nothing at all. The market has closed. The wire opens on the 26th, and the Gumbas will be shopping in it like everybody else.

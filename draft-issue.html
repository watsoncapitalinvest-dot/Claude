The Column

The King Is Seated. The Board Is Open.

The board goes live tonight. The Powers of Pain defends five rings, New Wod Order traded up, and the class has a quarterback-sized hole in it. Sixteen teams, one night.

It comes down to tonight. The board goes live on Sleeper, the room is on a Zoom, and sixteen franchises turn a winter of trading into names on a card. The Powers of Pain drafts as the reigning champion — its fifth title, more than any team in SCFL history, sealed by a late-season run nobody else could answer. The other fifteen spent the offseason making sure it does not get to six.

The front of the board belongs to the teams chasing it. Heavy Hitters holds the 1.01 and comes back around at nine. StillTheCream sits on two and four. And Wookie Leaks, a perennial rebuilder, has stacked three of the first eleven picks: five, ten and eleven — a real haul at the top of the board.

New Wod Order has spent the run-up buying the board. It swapped franchise quarterbacks with The Machines — Lamar Jackson and the twentieth pick for Patrick Mahomes and the sixth, a fourteen-spot jump up. It flipped Jaylen Waddle to the Beaver Eaters for the seventh. And on draft night it sent Sam LaPorta to The Powers of Pain to pry loose the sixteenth pick and TJ Hockenson. That is three of the first sixteen picks — six, seven and sixteen — on one roster. Five teams, meanwhile, enter the night without a first-round pick at all: The Machines, Pork Chop Express, Lil’ Chops, the Beaver Eaters and now The Powers of Pain.

Pork Chop Express draws the longest wait in the room — no first-round pick, and nothing on the clock until 28th overall. It is not taking it quietly: “Coming up one game short is unacceptable,” it posted when the season ended.

They are all drafting into a class with a hole in the middle. It is deep at receiver — Carnell Tate and Jordyn Tyson head a long line of them — and nearly empty at quarterback, where Fernando Mendoza is the only rookie worth a first in a Superflex format that cannot function without the position. Jeremiyah Love is the consensus 1.01; after the top three, it is wideout after wideout. Down at twelve sits Big Blue, the franchise that won three straight from 2021 to 2023 and set the bar the rest of the league still measures against. Sixteen teams get one night to close the gap.

Correction & Apology
✒️ The Record on Wookie Leaks, Set Straight

An apology is owed. An earlier version of The Column stated that Wookie Leaks had never reached the playoffs. That was wrong, and it was unfair to a franchise that has earned its place in the field. Per the official league record, Wookie Leaks — one team since 2015, under a handful of names (The Wade Garrets, The 40 Pound Testicles, and, for one penalty season, The Dry Dicks) — has reached the eight-team championship bracket four times: 2015, 2020, 2021 and 2023. The 51–97 regular-season mark is accurate; the claim that this team had never played in January was not. We retract it without reservation, and we regret the error.

Playoff appearances count the eight-team championship bracket only; consolation games are excluded. Record and berths: official league season records, 2015–2025. Current team name per Sleeper; the Dry Dicks penalty season noted per league history.

Some Records Are Made Up

The 1,600-yard lie, the mask, the microfilm, and the apology that never came.

WEST PALM BEACH, FLA. — It began the way most SCFL disasters begin: with a man reminiscing about a football team that was not very good. The subject was Lakeland High, class of 1997. The man runs StillTheCream. And he had a number.

Sixteen hundred yards. Receiving. Six games. Senior year.

There are lies, and there are lies with a paper trail, and StillTheCream — God bless it — volunteered the paper trail. “The stats are in the newspaper at the library,” it said. “North County News, 1997. You don’t live far from the library. Go look it up.”

It was talking to the wrong team. It was talking to The Machines.

The Machines runs the power rankings. It is a four-time champion and the single most relentless voice in a chat that has never once lacked for volume. When The Machines is told that the truth is sitting on a roll of microfilm eleven minutes away, The Machines does not text back. The Machines gets the keys.

There was, to be fair, a math problem. Sixteen hundred receiving yards in a season is not a “good year.” It is a New York State record. It is the kind of number that makes a seventeen-year-old the most recruited receiver in the country. It had never, the chat quickly established, been sniffed by anyone out of Lakeland — a program that spent that fall getting run off the field.

It was August 2021. There was a pandemic on. The library turned The Machines away at the door for not wearing a mask. The Machines found a mask. The Machines went back, and explained to the woman at the desk exactly why a grown man needed the 1997 North County News on microfilm — and, by its own account, “she even had some words for Creams herself.”

Then it started to spin. Reel by reel. Game by game. Photographing every Lakeland football item the paper printed that fall and posting them to the chat in real time, like a hostage video.

“593 actually equals 1600 if you factor in interest since 1997.”— The Machines, doing the math on its own crime scene

The findings were not kind. Lakeland went 1–7. In one game the entire team gained roughly 260 yards — total, all eleven of them. The other schools got write-ups; Lakeland got a blurb, and one week the blurb was faded enough that StillTheCream would later claim its real numbers had been, conveniently, blacked out. The actual senior receiving total, per the North County News: 593 yards. The box-score lines surfaced too — 62 yards one week, “all 29 of them” another.

Five hundred and ninety-three. It had said sixteen hundred.

The chat, sensing blood and microfilm, ascended. “He could have dialed www dot Google dot com,” The Beaver Eaters marveled, “but the man got a mask, went around roadblocks, just to prove Creams wrong — and Creams STILL won’t admit it.” Pork Chop Express was more clinical: “The Machines went to the library and your butthole got super tight.” Forty2V co-signed the accounting: “that’s a pro-rated 1600.”

StillTheCream’s rebuttal was a masterclass in every direction but backward. It wondered aloud whether The Machines had doctored the microfilm. It pivoted to vaccines. It explained that Lakeland was “a bullshit program” next to the real powers — throwing its own school under the bus to account for why a record-setting year went unnoticed. It pointed out — correctly, irrelevantly — that Pork Chop Express also claims a fictional stolen-base record and Heavy Hitters a phantom home-run record, as if a chat full of liars somehow exonerates the biggest one. Then it went dark for days.

The Machines would not allow it. “Day 1,” it posted. “Waiting for the 1600-yard lie to be addressed, or an apology.” Then Day 2. Then Day 3. It got a name — 1600 Gate — and a proposed sentence: a formal vote to rename StillTheCream’s franchise “The 1600s,” in perpetuity, until it confessed.

It never quite did. Five days in, cornered, StillTheCream produced the closest thing to a white flag the SCFL has ever recorded: “I was wrong about 1600 being my senior season total. If that’s what I said. I remember seeing an article with my yards amount, and it said 1600.” The Beaver Eaters offered the kindest available theory: “Maybe it was 1600 points. Of basketball.”

The Machines called off the dogs. It did not, and this is the entire point, forget. Four years later “microfiche” remains the most feared word in the chat — the thing you reach for when you want to call a team’s bluff and mean it. There have been sequels. There have been threats to rename the last-place team “Microfiche” for a season. There has been, from StillTheCream, no correction.

Because that is the thing about a league that keeps its own history — that will take an hour out of a workday to go check the record. Some records are made to be broken. This one was made up. And the North County News, 1997, is still on file.

— The SCFL NewsRoom

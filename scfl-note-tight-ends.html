A Tight End Scores Less Than Your Kicker

In a non-PPR league the tight end slot is the cheapest seat in the lineup. Which is an argument for selling one, and the reason the one worth selling is the one you keep.

The SCFL NewsRoom · The Player Desk

The premise offered to the desk was that tight ends do not score a lot. In this league, in this format, that is not an opinion. It is the lowest number on the board.

The average started tight end in this league is worth two and a half points a week less than the average kicker.

It is not only the average. The ceiling is low too: a top-tenth week from a tight end is 15.5 points, which is less than a top-tenth week from the defence and barely two thirds of what a running back's good week pays. And the gap between a very good tight end and an ordinary one is about ten points a week, against fifteen at running back and twelve at quarterback. The slot compresses everybody into the same small range.

So sell the tight end

That is the obvious conclusion and it is the one the desk reached, and it needs one correction before it is safe to act on. The tight end on this roster worth selling is Colston Loveland, who is 22, whose target share has climbed from 7 to 12 per cent with a 26 per cent week in the middle of it, and whom the market has repriced from $21 to $43 in a year.

Everything in that sentence except the position argues for keeping him. He is the right age for a roster rebuilding toward next season, he is the only asset in the group whose price is going up, and his 110 yards and no touchdowns through five weeks mean his value is entirely in what the market believes rather than in what he has produced — which is the definition of an asset you sell high or hold, not one you dump for convenience.

Where that lands

The position being cheap is an argument for not paying for tight ends. It is not an argument for giving one away at a discount, and a mid-second (ten dollars) against a $43 asset is a discount.

If Loveland goes, he goes for something that moves the rebuild forward — a real pick or a young receiver with a share — and the second tight end is the one that leaves for a sweetener.

How this was worked out. Every starter's points for weeks one to four of 2026, taken from the committed week files rather than from a live fetch, and grouped by the slot the manager actually started him in. Week five is left out because its file was pulled while the week was still being played.

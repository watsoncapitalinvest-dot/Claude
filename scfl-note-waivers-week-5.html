Three Names On The Wire, And One Of Them Is Real

Every roster place in this league is taken but eight. Of everyone left, exactly one has a share that has gone up every week he has played.

The first thing to say about the waiver wire in a 16-team league with 21 places a roster is that there is almost nothing on it. Three hundred and twenty-eight of the three hundred and thirty-six places are filled. Across five weeks, twenty unrostered players at any skill position have had as many as twelve touches between them, and most of those twenty are backup tight ends and third receivers whose usage is going nowhere.

So the question is not who is good. It is who is being used more this week than last, and whether he is young enough to matter next year.

Kimani Vidal is the claim

He is the only unrostered player in the league whose share has risen every week he has been used: nothing, nothing, 6 per cent, 12, 17. Twenty-two touches, 112 receiving yards and two touchdowns out of a role that did not exist in September. At 25 he is not a long-term asset, but he is the one name on this list where the offence is visibly handing him more.

The catch, said plainly

Omarion Hampton is not hurt. He took fourteen carries in week five, the same week Vidal took ten touches, and he has seventy-nine for the season. Vidal is the third man in a three-man backfield who happens to be the one gaining.

That is a bench stash, not a starter. It is still the best thing available.

Kevin Coleman Jr. is the one to buy before the price

Twenty-three, a rookie, and his targets have gone 2, none, 4, 4, 7. Eleven per cent of Miami's throws in each of the last two weeks, up from four. And he has 36 yards on five catches, which is to say nothing has actually happened yet.

Nothing has happened yet. That is the entire argument for picking him up.

That is the point. The desk's own rule is that a rising share with no production is the buy and a flat share with touchdowns on top is the trap, and this is the first half of it. For a roster rebuilding toward next season he is free, he is 23, and the only thing he costs is a bench place.

Dameon Pierce is not a keeper. He is a trade.

Pierce had no touches at all in the first four weeks and sixteen in week five, 28 per cent of everything Philadelphia ran or threw. Saquon Barkley and Tank Bigsby had none between them in that game. Whatever the reason, the job was his for an afternoon.

He is 26, which puts him outside every reason this roster is holding anything. But a free running back with a starter's workload has real value to a team trying to win this season, and the way to use him is to claim him and sell him while the workload lasts — not to hold him and find out.

And the ones to leave
  • Darnell Washington has the best raw line here — 11 catches, 131 yards, a touchdown — and plays the position this desk measured at 6.8 points a start, below the kicker. In non-PPR that line is about 19 points across five games, under four a week, and his share went down in week five.
  • Corey Kiner went 9, 4, 12, nothing, 11 per cent. That is not a trend, it is a weather pattern.
  • Bryce Lance sits at 7 per cent behind a receiver this desk already flagged as New Orleans' most-used man. There is no room in front of him.
  • Darren Waller is rising and is 34, which is somebody else's problem.
One caveat with a date on it

This was built with thirteen of week five's sixteen games counted. Atlanta, Baltimore, Buffalo, Carolina, Kansas City and the Rams had not played. Anybody who breaks out tonight or tomorrow is not on this page, and the board is rebuilt from the same two files the moment they have.

How this was worked out. Who is owned comes from this morning's roster pull — all sixteen teams, starters, bench and reserve — matched on the NFL's player id first and on the name second, because the pool contains two K.Colemans and only the id separates them. Usage is counted out of nflverse play-by-play: a player's carries plus targets against his own team's carries plus pass attempts, week by week. Ages are floored from birth dates, not rounded; rounding made three of these men a year older than they are.

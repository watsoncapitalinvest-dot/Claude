The Man With the Cold Face

Every league has superstitions. Most of them are noise — a lucky jersey, a bad seat, a manager who will not say a player’s name out loud in a good week. The SCFL has exactly one piece of folklore that has survived contact with the evidence, and it belongs to the worst franchise in the building.

🥶

It is an emoji. 🥶 — the cold face. In this league it is not a temperature. It is a mark, and when the Wookie posts it at a player, the chat goes quiet for a second and then starts writing the obituary.

The Mechanism

Nobody voted on it and nobody wrote it down. The closest thing to a founding document is a throwaway line from August 1, 2021, when a separate argument about whose fantasy defense had been called the voodoo defense pulled the Wookie into a thread he had slept through. “I think I used those for the curse,” he wrote. “Only time I think I really even mentioned voodoo in here was for the 🥶.”

That is the whole liturgy. There is no ritual, no doll, no incantation — just a man, an emoji, and an eleven-year record of things going wrong immediately afterward.

The Founding Cases

Ask the league for the canon and you get two names, and you get them fast. Pressed in February 2022 on whether the thing was even still real, the Wookie answered like a man reciting scripture: “Romo was still the greatest instance of the 🥶 ever.” Tony Romo was traded, and, in the Wookie’s own words, “traded and less than a week later broken back.”

The Machines, who keeps the league’s institutional memory the way other men keep receipts, immediately supplied the second: “That and the Luck trades the biggest 🤯 ever.” Andrew Luck changed hands and then retired out of the sport entirely. Two transactions, two franchise quarterbacks, two men who never played another meaningful down for the team that bought them.

The Doctrine

What makes this more than a running joke is that the league treats it as a property of the player, not the moment. Contact is permanent. In the same February 2022 thread, The Machines waved off an injury worry with a sentence that reads like case law: “He was never a wookie, so at least the SCFL boogie man hasn’t touched him.”

Once a Wookie, always marked. And the mark travels through paper as well as flesh. When Horse Collars pointed out that a pick he had acquired from the Wookie had also turned to dust — “that occurred with my other pick I got from you. Moss” — the Wookie’s response was not denial. It was an apology, and a slightly alarmed one: “Shit. My bad, Doc. Lol. Didn’t realize it extended to picks of mine too.”

He was never a wookie, so at least the SCFL boogie man hasn’t touched him.

Horse Collars, who has never once in five years let a premise pass unexamined, asked the only question that mattered: “Why would it not? Has this been studied before for validity?”

This desk has now studied it.

The Roll Call

Leonard Fournette drew the 🥶 live on Thursday night football in September 2021 — “🥶 Fournette….” — and later arrived on the Pork Chop Express in the Kirk Cousins deal. Kareem Hunt came out of the same roster in the Derek Carr trade. The Pork Chop Express, who bought both and has never fully recovered from the experience, filed the definitive skeptic’s confession in October 2022: “Wookie had Fournette and Hunt. Lenny came to me in the Cousins trade I believe. Hunt in the Carr trade. I said the curse didn’t exist. Hunt kicked a woman on camera.”

Jameson Williams was the Wookie’s centrepiece pick at eleven in the 2022 draft, and the Powers of Pain’s grade sheet flagged the problem in the act of praising it: “the explosive Alabama receiver is recovering from an ACL tear and won’t be available till late in the season.” He was reinstated from an NFL suspension in September 2023. He made news in Detroit that had nothing to do with football in October 2024, which the chat logged in three words: “Rough week for Wookie.”

SCFL NewsRoom · Investigations · August 2026

Treylon Burks did not make it through a single evening. On August 19, 2023, hours after arriving, The Machines called it before the injury report did: “Burks felt the Wookie curse fairly quick.” And then, with the clinical delight of a man watching a hypothesis confirm itself: “Tweak something within minutes of walking into the Wookie’s facility.”

Cooper Kupp is the expensive one. The Wookie paid a package the Smoke Dragons summarised as renting “a hurt Kupp for 1.5 firsts,” and by September 2023 The Machines was reduced to asking the room the same question on a loop — how many games had the man actually played since the trade. “Cooper Kupp is going to be 31. On IR. Injured every year. Has played one full season ever.”

There are more. The Machines wondered aloud in December 2022 whether Jimmy Garoppolo had ever been on the roster — “Did Wookie ever own Jimmy G? Wondering if the curse is still active” — and in October 2022 extended the theory to an entire position group, floating “a strange Wookie-style curse on potential Jets QBs they will face.” Horse Collars, watching an old grudge detonate in real time, gave the phenomenon its formal name: “Voodoo for trades. Wookie Curse. He has the curse.”

Weaponised

The Wookie has never once denied it. He has monetised it.

SCFL NewsRoom · Investigations · August 2026

In December 2021 he announced a procurement: “Hopefully my Hurts voodoo doll gets here soon 🤞🏻,” then talked himself out of his own scheme two lines later — “But I never had Hurts, so the curse won’t be in play.” Even the owner respects the eligibility rules.

In September 2022, after trading Matt Ryan away and taking chat abuse for it, he aimed the thing at a man who had just tried to embarrass him in public: “It’s all good, Dave. I hope Ryan breaks both clavicles. See what Foles has left in the tank.” Then, a beat later, the slogan he has been running on ever since: “Make the Curse Great Again.” Then, immediately: “Should’ve hashtagged that.”

And in December 2025 he made the leap from cursing players to cursing people, claiming a rival’s elimination as a personal act — “already got Schechter the fuck outta the playoffs, let’s see if the old 🥶 is back, just in another form.”

The Problem

Here is the part the Wookie does not put in the brochure.

SCFL NewsRoom · Investigations · August 2026

Pull the ledger and the curse has a home address, and it is his own. In eleven seasons under the Wookie Leaks banner, from 2015 through 2025, the franchise has gone 51-97. That is a .345 win percentage — the lowest of any franchise in this league with more than three seasons on the books. Its all-play mark is 875-1324-21. Its ELO sits at 650, third from the bottom of a twenty-three-franchise table.

It has made the playoffs zero times. Not once in eleven years.

The franchise’s first incarnation, running from 2005 through 2012, went 38-65-1 and made the postseason three times. Add the two eras together and you get nineteen recorded seasons, 89-162-1, three playoff appearances, and zero championships. Nineteen years. No trophy. No banner. No final.

The Theory

So which is it: a man who curses other people’s players, or a man standing in the middle of the blast radius?

The evidence supports the crueller reading. Every documented case runs in both directions. The players he sells break for the buyer. The players he buys break for him. Fournette and Hunt went out and detonated on the Pork Chop Express; Burks and Williams and Kupp detonated on arrival. The Machines, back in October 2021, described the market’s read on the man in a sentence he did not intend as folklore: “He was offering out horrible trades that would’ve hurt his team but people took pity on him or something.”

By August 2024 that had hardened into open pricing. “I feel like this is a Wookie offer that he throws out there,” the Pork Chop Express wrote of an unrelated proposal, “in case one of us just had a massive head injury.” When your name becomes the unit of measurement for a trade nobody sane would accept, the curse has stopped being about the players.

The Verdict

The Wookie Curse is real. Nineteen seasons of standings say so, and they say it about him.

SCFL NewsRoom · Investigations · August 2026

He has spent five years pointing a cold blue face at other people’s rosters and watching, with genuine and undisguised joy, as ligaments and careers and playoff hopes came apart on schedule. He is very good at it. It is the single most reliable thing his franchise does. And in all that time — through the Romo trade, through Luck’s retirement, through Fournette and Hunt and Burks and Williams and Kupp, through the Hurts doll and Make the Curse Great Again and the freeze aimed at a man’s playoff seeding — the trophy case has stayed empty.

Horse Collars, watching the thing reactivate in October 2022, put it better than any of the accountants. Somebody, he noted, had once “laughed off the curse 🥶.”

“And it came alive with vengeance.”

The wire opens on the 26th. Somewhere in Mos Eisley a man is scrolling the injury report with his thumb over an emoji, and eighteen managers are quietly hoping he is not looking at anybody they own.

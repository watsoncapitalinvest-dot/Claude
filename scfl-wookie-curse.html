The Roll Call

Every league has superstitions. Most of them are noise — a lucky jersey, a bad seat, a manager who will not say a player’s name out loud in a good week. The SCFL has exactly one that keeps surviving contact with the evidence, and it can be stated in six words.

Players the Wookie trades away get hurt.

Not while he owns them. That is the part people get wrong when they first arrive. On his roster they are fine — bored, maybe, occasionally underused, but structurally intact. It is the leaving that does it. The moment a player crosses out of the Wookie’s building and into somebody else’s, the clock starts. And the clock has a very good record.

🥶

It has a symbol, too. 🥶 — the cold face. In this league it is not a temperature. It is a mark, and when the Wookie posts it, the chat goes quiet for a second and then starts writing the obituary. Horse Collars gave the whole thing its formal name in October 2022, watching it work in real time: “Voodoo for trades. Wookie Curse. He has the curse.”

SCFL NewsRoom · Investigations · August 2026

Nobody voted it into existence and nobody wrote it down. The closest thing to a founding document is a throwaway line from August 1, 2021, when an argument about something else entirely pulled the Wookie into a thread he had slept through. “I think I used those for the curse,” he wrote. “Only time I think I really even mentioned voodoo in here was for the 🥶.”

That is the whole liturgy. No ritual, no doll, no incantation. A man, an emoji, and a transaction log that keeps ending in an injury report.

What follows is the roll call. It is not complete. It is only the ones the chat caught.

Tony Romo

Patient zero, and the case the Wookie himself still nominates. Pressed in February 2022 on whether the thing was even still live, he answered like a man reciting scripture: “Romo was still the greatest instance of the 🥶 ever.” Then, because he is not a monster, an apology to the manager who had been holding the receipt: “Sorry @Mike.”

The mechanism, in his own words: “Traded and less than a week later broken back.” The Machines, who keeps this league’s institutional memory the way other men keep receipts, offered no rebuttal at all. He offered corroboration: “It was next level.”

Leonard Fournette

The only name on this list who was marked out loud, in public, before anything happened to him. On a Thursday night in September 2021, watching from across the league, the Wookie posted four characters and a name: “🥶 Fournette….”

Fournette came onto the Wookie’s roster in the enormous August 2022 quarterback chain and was moved along again inside the year. By November 13 the Wookie was noting the aftermath in the chat himself, in four words: “Lenny hip injury too.”

Kareem Hunt

This one is not our finding. It belongs to the Pork Chop Express, who spent his rookie year in this league acquiring players out of the Wookie’s building and has never fully recovered from the experience. His confession, filed in October 2022, is quoted in full because it cannot be improved on:

“Wookie had Fournette and Hunt. Lenny came to me in the Cousins trade I believe. Hunt in the Carr trade. I said the curse didn’t exist. Hunt kicked a women on camera.”
Zack Moss

The case that established the curse travels through paper as well as flesh. Horse Collars, sifting the wreckage of assets acquired from the Wookie, noted that “that occurred with my other pick I got from you. Moss.” The reply was not a denial. It was an apology, and a faintly alarmed one: “Shit. My bad, Doc. Lol. Didn’t realize it extended to picks of mine too.”

Horse Collars asked the only question that mattered — why would it not, and had anybody ever studied this thing for validity?

Kyler Murray

The loudest one, and the one that suggests you do not even have to stay long. In the August 2022 blockbuster Murray came into the Wookie’s building from The Machines and went straight back out the other side to the Smoke Dragons. He barely touched the carpet.

On the night of December 12, on a play nobody touched him on either, he did not get up. The chat has all of it in real time. “Murray. Oh no,” from the Pork Chop Express. “Non contact.” Then: “He’s done.” Then: “He went down like a sack of potatoes.”

And from the man who had taken him four months earlier, the line that belongs on the plaque: “I had the week locked up. Should have kept him on the bench and avoided injury.” Two minutes later, all of it: “Very upsetting.”

Cooper Kupp

Left for The Machines on September 13, 2023, in perfect working order, for a future first and a receiver. He lasted about six weeks. On November 19 Horse Collars filed the two-word update the league had been waiting on without admitting it was waiting: “Kupp hurt.” Eleven days after that, The Machines was explaining his own receiver to the room: “Stafford was out and now he’s dealing with an ankle. Injuries always a concern with Kupp.”

Dk Metcalf

As late as July 24, 2024, The Machines was citing Metcalf as evidence that the Wookie’s roster was young and healthy: “Wookie has Swift, Richardson, Metcalf, Williams — not like his team is old.” Metcalf moved to The Machines inside that same season. On October 20 he was carted off. The buyer’s account, live: “Metcalf grabbed bunch of points first thankfully. But carted out.” And then the sentence every trade partner of the Wookie eventually types: “Just can’t keep this f***ing team healthy for one week.”

Jordyn Tyson

The newest name, and the one that came within a day of the record.

On August 6 the Wookie sent Tyson to the New World Order. The chat spent that evening doing what this chat does, which is arguing about it at length. On August 13, in a joint practice against Jacksonville, Tyson pulled up holding a hamstring. The reporting has him out something like two months, with a live chance it eats into the regular season. One outlet used the word bleak.

Seven days. Romo went in less than a week.

His write-up flagged the same worry twice in a single paragraph: “If Tyson can stay healthy he has the talent to create a 1A/1B situation in New Orleans with Chris Olave.” And: he “drew Stefan Diggs comparisons, which is exciting if he can just remain healthy.”

The Doctrine

What elevates all this above a running joke is that the league treats the mark as permanent, and as a property of the player rather than the moment. In February 2022 The Machines waved off an injury worry about somebody else with a sentence that reads like case law: “He was never a wookie, so at least the SCFL boogie man hasn’t touched him.”

Once a Wookie, always eligible. Nobody has ever been de-listed.

The Owner

He has never denied any of it. He has leaned all the way in.

In December 2021 he announced a procurement — “hopefully my Hurts voodoo doll gets here soon 🤞🏻” — then talked himself out of his own scheme two lines later: “But I never had Hurts, so the curse won’t be in play.” Even the owner respects the eligibility rules.

In September 2022 he traded Matt Ryan away, took some chat abuse for it, and pointed the thing at a manager who had just tried to embarrass him in public: “It’s all good, Dave. I hope Ryan breaks both clavicles. See what Foles has left in the tank.” A beat later came the slogan he has been running on ever since — “Make the Curse Great Again” — and a beat after that: “Should’ve hashtagged that.”

By December 2025 he had stopped bothering with players altogether and gone straight at a rival’s season: “Already got Schechter the f*** outta the playoffs, let’s see if the old 🥶 is back, just in another form.”

He was joking. He is always joking. Nobody in this league has ever been entirely sure how much — which is the whole appeal, and also the reason The Machines has twice gone looking for a connection to explain an unrelated bad month. In December 2022: “Did wookie ever own Jimmy g!” And, immediately after: “Wondering if the curse is still active.” In October of that year, extending the doctrine to an entire position group, “a strange Wookie-style curse on potential Jets QBs they will face.”

Nobody laughs it off. In this league, laughing it off is documented as the thing that makes it worse. Horse Collars watched somebody try it once and reported the result in two lines: they “laughed off the curse 🥶,” and then “it came alive with vengeance.”

The Wire Opens On The 26Th

The Wookie will pick up the phone. He is one of the most active traders in the league, he answers everybody, and he is generally reasonable on price. Somewhere in the middle of that conversation you will realise you are about to acquire a perfectly healthy football player from a man whose last several perfectly healthy football players are currently listed as questionable, doubtful, out, or retired.

Take the trade. Everyone does. Just don’t say you weren’t warned, don’t say it out loud afterwards, and whatever else you do — do not laugh it off.

MOS EISLEY · AUGUST 18, 2026

Seven Days

On August 6, the Wookie traded Jordyn Tyson to the New World Order for Xavier Worthy, Tony Pollard and the Smoke Dragons’ 2027 second.

On August 13, in a joint practice against Jacksonville, Tyson pulled up with a hamstring. The reporting has him out something like two months, with a live chance it bleeds into the regular season. One outlet used the word bleak.

Seven days.

The Rule

Every league has superstitions. Most of them are noise — a lucky jersey, a bad seat, a manager who will not say a player’s name out loud in a good week. The SCFL has exactly one that keeps surviving contact with the evidence, and it can be stated in six words.

Players the Wookie trades away get hurt.

SCFL NewsRoom · Investigations · August 2026

Not while he owns them. That is the part people get wrong when they first arrive. On his roster they are fine — bored, maybe, occasionally underused, but structurally intact. It is the leaving that does it. The moment a player crosses out of the Wookie’s building and into somebody else’s, the clock starts, and the clock has a very good record.

🥶

It has a symbol, too. 🥶 — the cold face. In this league it is not a temperature. It is a mark, and when the Wookie posts it, the chat goes quiet for a second and then starts writing the obituary. Horse Collars gave the whole thing its formal name in October 2022, watching an old grudge detonate in real time: “Voodoo for trades. Wookie Curse. He has the curse.”

The Tyson File

What makes this one different is that the entire league is on the record about it, in writing, with timestamps, eight days before the hamstring.

SCFL NewsRoom · Investigations · August 2026

Start with the Powers of Pain, who graded the Wookie’s 2026 rookie class an A and singled Tyson out as the prize: a top-ten talent who fell into his lap when the Creams went running back. POP then said the quiet part twice in the same paragraph. “If Tyson can stay healthy he has the talent to create a 1A/1B situation in New Orleans with Chris Olave.” And: he “drew Stefan Diggs comparisons, which is exciting if he can just remain healthy.”

Then, on the evening of August 6, Lil Chops posted the transaction. “🚨Official Trade🚨 Wookies send Jordyn Tyson to NWO for Xavier Worthy, Tony Pollard, Dragon’s 2027 2nd.” His verdict took four more words: “NWO with a nice trade to catapult the rebuild.”

The pile-on was immediate and close to unanimous. The Beaver Eaters: “Questionable trade for sure.” The Machines, running the dynasty numbers: “Worthy is ranked in the 50s dynasty wise, definitely fallen off value. Tyson is top 20.” And then the structural objection, which is the one this desk would have signed at the time: “Shipping away a possible blue chipper even before he’s played a down will always be questioned in this league.”

SCFL NewsRoom · Investigations · August 2026

The Machines summed the deal as “NWO just got himself a top five player in the draft for Pollard and a mid-50s dynasty wide receiver.” Lil Chops was blunter — “to draft one and sell it for 2 depreciating assets is pretty silly” — and then, at 6:09 p.m., wrote the sentence that is going to follow him around for a while:

“I hope NWO flips Tyson for his real value to show Wookie how it’s done.”

That was August 6. On August 13 the New World Order’s new blue-chip receiver grabbed the back of his leg in Jacksonville, and the Wookie was suddenly holding Xavier Worthy, Tony Pollard and a second-round pick, and nobody in the chat wanted to relitigate anything.

The Origin

Nobody voted the curse into existence and nobody wrote it down. The closest thing to a founding document is a throwaway line from August 1, 2021, when an unrelated argument about whose fantasy defense had been called the voodoo defense pulled the Wookie into a thread he had slept through. “I think I used those for the curse,” he wrote. “Only time I think I really even mentioned voodoo in here was for the 🥶.”

That is the whole liturgy. No ritual, no doll, no incantation. A man, an emoji, and a transaction log that keeps ending in an injury report.

The Canon

Ask the league for the founding cases and you get two names, fast. Pressed in February 2022 on whether the thing was even still live, the Wookie answered like a man reciting scripture: “Romo was still the greatest instance of the 🥶 ever.” Tony Romo left the roster and, in the Wookie’s own words, was “traded and less than a week later broken back.”

Tyson went in seven days. The record still stands, but not by much.

The Machines, who keeps this league’s institutional memory the way other men keep receipts, supplied the second case without being asked: “That and the Luck trades the biggest 🤯 ever.” Andrew Luck changed hands and then retired out of the sport entirely. Two trades, two franchise quarterbacks, neither one playing another meaningful down for the team that bought him.

The Doctrine

What elevates this above a running joke is that the league treats the mark as permanent, and treats it as a property of the asset rather than the moment. In February 2022 The Machines waved off an injury worry with a sentence that reads like case law: “He was never a wookie, so at least the SCFL boogie man hasn’t touched him.”

And it travels through paper as well as flesh. When Horse Collars noted that a pick he had acquired from the Wookie had also turned to dust — “that occurred with my other pick I got from you. Moss” — the response was not a denial. It was an apology, and a faintly alarmed one: “Shit. My bad, Doc. Lol. Didn’t realize it extended to picks of mine too.”

Horse Collars asked the only question that mattered: “Why would it not? Has this been studied before for validity?”

It has now.

Exhibit · Kyler Murray

In August 2022 the Wookie moved Kyler Murray out of his building as the centrepiece of the blockbuster that brought back Justin Herbert. Murray landed on the Smoke Dragons. On the night of December 12, on a play nobody touched him on, he did not get up. “Murray. Oh no,” from the Pork Chop Express. “Non contact.” Then: “He’s done.” Then: “He went down like a sack of potatoes.”

And from the man who had bought him four months earlier, the line that belongs on the plaque: “I had the week locked up. Should have kept him on the bench and avoided injury.” Two minutes later, all of it: “Very upsetting.”

Exhibit · Cooper Kupp

On September 13, 2023, the Wookie sold Cooper Kupp to The Machines for a future first and change, and took two days of chat abuse for it. Kupp played six healthy weeks in his new uniform. On November 19 Horse Collars filed the two-word update the league had been waiting on without admitting it was waiting: “Kupp hurt.” Eleven days later The Machines was explaining his own acquisition to the room: “Stafford was out and now he’s dealing with an ankle. Injuries always a concern with Kupp.”

Exhibit · Dk Metcalf

As late as July 24, 2024, The Machines was listing the Wookie’s roster as proof it was not an old team: “Wookie has Swift, Richardson, Metcalf, Williams — not like his team is old.” Metcalf was moved to The Machines inside that same season. On October 20 he was carted off. The buyer’s account, live: “Metcalf grabbed bunch of points first thankfully. But carted out.” And then the sentence every trade partner of the Wookie eventually types: “Just can’t keep this f***ing team healthy for one week.”

The Pork Chop File

No franchise has funded more of this research than the Pork Chop Express, who bought Leonard Fournette out of the Wookie’s roster in the Kirk Cousins deal and Kareem Hunt in the Derek Carr deal, and who filed the definitive skeptic’s confession in October 2022: “Wookie had Fournette and Hunt. Lenny came to me in the Cousins trade I believe. Hunt in the Carr trade. I said the curse didn’t exist. Hunt kicked a woman on camera.”

Fournette had already been marked out loud, incidentally. On a Thursday night in September 2021, watching him from across the league, the Wookie posted four characters and a name: “🥶 Fournette….”

Calling The Shot

He has never once denied any of it. He has monetised it. In September 2022 he traded Matt Ryan away, took chat abuse for it, and aimed the thing at a manager who had just tried to embarrass him in public: “It’s all good, Dave. I hope Ryan breaks both clavicles. See what Foles has left in the tank.” A beat later came the slogan he has been running on ever since — “Make the Curse Great Again” — and a beat after that: “Should’ve hashtagged that.”

He was joking. He is always joking. Nobody in this league has ever been entirely sure how much.

The Standing

So here is where the SCFL’s only piece of working folklore sits going into the 2026 wire, which opens on the 26th.

The Wookie will pick up the phone. He is one of the most active traders in the league, he answers everybody, and he is generally reasonable on price. Somewhere in the middle of that conversation you will realise you are about to acquire a perfectly healthy football player from a man whose last several perfectly healthy football players are currently listed as questionable, doubtful, out, or retired.

SCFL NewsRoom · Investigations · August 2026

The New World Order took that trade twelve days ago, and the whole league told him he had robbed the Wookie blind, and for seven days he had.

Take the trade. Everyone does. Just don’t say you weren’t warned, don’t say it out loud afterwards, and whatever else you do — do not laugh it off.

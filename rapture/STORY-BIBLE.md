# UNTITLED RAPTURE PROJECT — Story Bible

*Working document. Plot development in progress — nothing here is locked.*

---

## PREMISE

The Rapture happens. Billions die in a single instant. The people left behind
come to understand that they were judged and found wanting — that there is no
heaven waiting for them, and that being left behind *is* hell.

Some conclude that if the score no longer counts, nothing is forbidden. Some
choose to be good anyway, for no reward and no reason. The good ones build a
settlement in a forest that becomes a new Eden. The world's new order comes to
take it. At the moment the defenders are finished, the sky opens and angels
come down and win the battle.

## THEME

**Is goodness worth anything if there is no reward?**

The ending must not answer "yes, virtue pays, you just have to wait." The
rescue is **grace** — unearned, arriving for people who had already made peace
with getting nothing. Eden does not fall because the defenders failed a test;
it falls because a friend betrayed them. They are saved anyway.

## WORLD RULES

- **The taken leave bodies.** No vanishing, no piles of clothes. Corpses, mass
  graves, disease, a world that must be physically dug out of.
- **No nameable cause of death.** Science reads that as "we haven't found it
  yet." Faith reads it as "there was nothing to find." Both sides get real
  evidence. Nobody wins the argument, so it gets settled with violence.
- **The pattern of who was taken is messy.** Not all children. Not all
  Christians. Devout people left standing beside atheists who were taken. Whole
  churches emptied, the church next door untouched. Legible enough to feel like
  judgment, inconsistent enough to be deniable.
- **God is silent** from the first morning until the final battle. The only
  visible miracles in the film belong to the enemy.
- **Religious vs. secular dispute** is a live, ongoing conflict throughout.

## CHARACTERS

### EMMA — female lead, early 20s
Sorority sister, in the back seat of the car when it happens. Survives the
opening crash. *(Arc and act-one path still to be developed.)*

### JOHN — male lead
At church that morning **searching for faith** — a seeker, not a believer.

When the congregation realizes the priest was not taken, John is the one who
says out loud that the priest may have been judged and left for a reason. He
argues **against** killing him. A parishioner then testifies that the priest
molested him as a child, and the congregation beats the priest to death.

John is not wrong and he does not swing. But he said the true thing, and the
true thing got a man killed. **His damage: a man who has learned that speaking
costs lives.** For most of the film he will not lead, will not argue, will not
say the thing everyone needs him to say. The moment he finally does is his turn.

Eden is partly his attempt to pay off the founding sin of the movie.

### RUTHIE — Emma's friend, the other crash survivor
Sweet, likable, **weak and easily manipulated**. Not scheming, not selfish by
design — she is a scared kid who bends toward whoever is kind to her.

She is worked at a checkpoint fence by a gentle officer who feeds her, listens
to her, and asks easy friendly questions: how far did you walk, is anyone sick,
are there children with you, how many of you are there. He hands her the thing
she wants most — **absolution.** He tells her she isn't damned, that none of it
is her fault, that no one is keeping score. She gives him the seed tins because
he says they'll be safer there. She never knows she gave him anything else.

**One conversation, two consequences:**
- *Known:* she gave away the seed stock — the sealed tins Eden has been
  starving around rather than eating, because they are next year. She is caught.
  Her only defense: *"He was nice to me."*
- *Unknown until act three:* she gave away Eden's location. She is the reason
  the army comes.

**She dies at the midpoint** — suddenly, unpredictably, sacrificing herself for
people who had already written her off. Not atoning for her crime. Something
else entirely.

They bury her in the forest because they cannot carry her further. That is why
they stop there. That is why they stay. **Eden grows out of her grave** —
things growing out of season, too green, too fast, and nobody says it out loud.

The marker reads **RUTH**. The only time her whole name is used.

In act three the survivors must hold both facts at once: *she died for us, and
she killed us.* Whether they leave her name on the garden is the same choice
God makes about them twenty minutes later.

### THE PRESIDENT — the Antichrist figure
Was **low in the presidential line of succession** — a cabinet secretary
(Agriculture, Housing, Education). Everyone above him died in one morning and
the chair was simply there. The Rapture made him king. He can say *"I was
chosen"* and may not be lying.

**Crucially: he was already well-liked before the Rapture.** The decent one,
the competent one, the man people joked should be running things. Humanity got
what it asked for.

His early acts are **genuinely good** — he restores the grid, gets food moving.
People are not fools for loving him; they are right, for a while. The turn is
slow and every step is reasonable, and by the time anyone can name what he has
become, he is the only thing between the world and starvation.

**His pitch is absolution.** While everyone else screams that the survivors
were judged and damned, he goes on television and says: *there was no judgment,
you are not guilty, no one is keeping score.* That is mercy to a planet
drowning in guilt. It is also permission. He empties the word "sin" and hands
people the freedom to do anything, and they love him for it, because he is the
only man who told them they were innocent.

**He does signs.** Real ones. A hand on a dying woman, then a stadium. The
enemy performs miracles on live television while God says nothing for years.

### SIMON WARD — the personal antagonist
The parishioner the priest molested. **Unnamed and forgettable in the church
scene** — just a shaking young man telling the room what was done to him when
he was nine. His testimony is what pushes the congregation over the edge.

He got his revenge in the first ten minutes and it fixed nothing.

He rises under the President as a regional commander — **Deputy Administrator
Ward.** He is the man at Ruthie's fence. He does not shout and does not
threaten. He is **the kindest man in the movie and the worst thing in it**: he
was groomed by a gentle man, he learned exactly how it is done — the patience,
the small kindnesses, the listening, the sense of being the only person in the
world who sees you — and that is now his instrument. Everything he does to
Ruthie was done to him first.

The monster at Eden's gate is one the church built. John's words started the
killing that made him.

*Surname note: a* ward *is a child in someone's care — what he was. It is also
the wall of a fortress — the thing he comes to break.*

### VILLAIN STRUCTURE — two tiers
- **The President is the ideology.** Global, televised, orderly. The argument.
- **Ward is the blade.** Personal, local, standing at Eden's gate. The cost of
  the argument.

The big villain sends the small one. The man John handed a knife to in act one
is the man who comes to kill him in act three.

## SEQUENCES ESTABLISHED

### OPENING — the car
Four sorority sisters driving to Vegas. **"Highway to Hell"** playing; they're
singing along. Girl 1 driving, Girl 2 in the passenger seat, Girl 3 and **Emma**
in the back.

The driver is going to Vegas to help her sick mother. The others are going to
party. The dialogue carries the exposition and plants the first clue that
something is happening.

Mid-song, the driver and one other girl simply die. The car crashes. **Emma and
Ruthie survive.** As they're talking about it, a plane comes down in the near
distance. Billions are dead in that same instant.

> *Licensing note: "Highway to Hell" is a real six-figure cost and the
> rights-holders can refuse. Write it in — it's the right joke and it sells the
> scene — but keep a fallback track in the back pocket.*

### THE CHURCH — introducing John
Congregation realizes people are dying everywhere and that it may be the
Rapture. Then they realize **the priest is not dead.** John says aloud that the
priest may have been judged. Ward testifies to his abuse. The congregation
kills the priest. John argues against it and fails.

The people who read this as the Rapture conclude they were judged, and that
**being left behind is hell.**

*This is the whole movie in one room: same event, same five minutes, and the
people in it walk out in two directions. Some build a garden. Some decide that
if this is hell, there are no rules in it.*

- What Ruthie dies doing.
- Ending: who survives, what happens to the world, does Emma or John die?

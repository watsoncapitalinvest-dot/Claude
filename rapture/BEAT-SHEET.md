# REMNANT
### Full beat sheet — first pass

*Working title. Alternates: THE FIRST WAVE, THOSE WHO REMAIN, STAND DOWN, EDEN.*

**Approx. 125 pages.** Page markers are targets, not law.

---

## PART ONE — THE FIRST DAYS
*pp. 1–34*

**1. INT. CAR — MORNING (p.1)** "Highway to Hell." Four sorority sisters, windows down, screaming along. KAYLA driving — she's going to Vegas to help her sick mother, and she's the only one with a reason. TESS riding shotgun. RUTHIE and EMMA in the back. The dialogue does the work: who they are, why they're going, and — buried in a radio segment nobody listens to — a report out of Jakarta about a stadium where several thousand people collapsed at once. Tess changes the station. Emma is the only one who heard it.

Emma promises to call her mother when they stop.

**2. (p.4)** Mid-lyric, Kayla stops singing. Tess stops. Neither is breathing. The car leaves the road.

**3. (p.6)** Emma and Ruthie crawl out. Nothing wrong with them. Nothing wrong with Kayla and Tess either — no wound, no blood, no reason. Emma keeps checking for a reason.

**4. (p.8)** A passenger jet comes down over the ridge. Then the sound of the world goes out — no engines, no planes, no horns. Just birds.

**5. INT. CHURCH — SAME MORNING (p.9)** Mid-service. JOHN, 37, at the back, not singing. He is not here for comfort — he is here for an accounting. He has a contracting company he has spent his adult life trying to make into something. Twice it has come within reach and twice a big loss has put him back at the bottom. The second time, he'd already decided God had helped him get there. He is three weeks into that second loss, and he has come here to ask what he did.

Half the congregation goes down where they stand.

**6. (p.12)** Screaming, phones, nothing working. Somebody says the word: *rapture.* And then somebody notices the priest is standing up.

**7. (p.14)** John says the true thing. *If this is a judgment — and he's still here — what does that make him?* He means it as a question. It doesn't land as one.

**8. (p.15)** A YOUNG MAN in the third row starts talking. Quietly, badly, about what the priest did to him when he was nine. He is not named. He will not be named for another hour of screen time.

**9. (p.17)** The room turns. John gets between them and fails. It takes a long time and it is not exciting. When it's over, the priest is on the floor and forty people are looking at their hands.

**10. (p.20)** John walks out into a street of stopped cars. He goes back in alone, later, and buries the priest. **His first grave.** He doesn't know why he does it.

**11. (p.22)** Emma and Ruthie make it to Emma's parents' house. Her mother, father, and fifteen-year-old brother are in three different rooms. No wounds. Emma — who was the only one in that house who ever went to church — digs three graves in the backyard, alone, over two days, while Ruthie watches from the step, unable to help.

*This is the second grave-digger in the movie and it is why she will recognize the first one.*

**12. (p.26) — MONTAGE, SHORT AND UGLY.** The first two weeks. Fires. Roads that don't work. A supermarket. Two men arguing about whether it was God or a weapon, and one of them killing the other over it. Neither of them right, and nobody able to prove anything, because there is no cause of death to find.

**13. (p.28)** Emma and Ruthie start walking. They're going somewhere Ruthie's uncle had a place. Ruthie calls it **Beaumont.**

**14. (p.29)** Cut to John, digging. No landmarks. No context. Nothing that tells you where in the world he is. There are eleven graves behind him and he is working on the twelfth.

**15. (p.30)** Emma and Ruthie pass a row of graves in an empty field. Mounded, squared, some with names on scrap. Nobody around. Ruthie is unnerved. Emma isn't — she knows exactly what she's looking at, and she stands there a while.

*Two threads, no map. The audience holds both halves for twenty pages and never joins them.*

**16. (p.32)** John's group — RAY WHELAN, 60s, from the church, and four others — are heading for water. They call it **the reservoir.**

**17. (p.33)** Emma comes over a rise. There's a man in a hole.

Neither of them says much. She picks up the second shovel.

## PART TWO — THE FIRST WINTER
*pp. 34–62*

**18. (p.35)** The treeline. Eleven people, then nineteen. It is never decided. Nobody votes. They simply fail to leave. John runs it without once agreeing to.

**19. (p.37)** They loot a farm co-op and come back with sealed tins of seed. Nobody plants any of it. **Nobody believes in next year.** DELIA, the nurse, says it out loud: we eat it or we die with it.

**20. (p.39)** **The charter gets written** — not written, said. Four things, after an argument about whether to let in a man who'd done something in the city. HAZEL is the one who says them, and after that she says them at the gate to everyone who comes:
> *Nobody's asked what they did. Nobody lies about what they do here. What you break, you answer for. And the gate is never locked.*

**21. (p.41)** Winter. Hunger. Emma and John orbit each other and do not touch it. CALEB, 15 at this point, wants a gate that shuts.

**22. (p.43)** Ruthie starts drifting toward the road. Not dramatically. She's cold and she's twenty-two and nobody in the camp is kind to her, because she isn't useful and everyone's hungry.

**23. (p.45) — THE FENCE.** A government checkpoint. Soup, blankets, a generator. And a volunteer with a clipboard who is kind to her — **not a soldier, not an officer, nobody**, which is what makes it terrible. The kindness is his own. He asks easy questions. How far did you walk. Is anyone sick. Are there children with you. How many of you are there.

And then he gives her the thing she actually wants: *none of this is your fault. Nobody's keeping score. You're a good girl.*

She gives him the seed tins because he says they'll be safer here.

**24. (p.49)** She's caught. She doesn't lie — the second law, and anyway she can't. Her whole defense is **"He was nice to me."**

**25. (p.51) — THE MEETING.** The charter tears itself apart in one room. Delia wants her out, on arithmetic. Hazel won't have it. John stands up to say she answers for the seed and is not condemned for it — **and he is going to win.**

**26. (p.53)** He never gets to. She's already packed. Somebody says *the gate isn't a punishment, Ruthie.* She says *it is for me.* Emma follows her to the treeline and can't stop her.

**27. (p.55) — THE SECOND ASK.** Ruthie goes back to the fence, because it's the only other place she knows and because he was nice to her. And the man with the clipboard needs one more thing. What she gave him was vague — a direction, a river, how many days. He's been working it ever since. He wants her to **walk them in.** She'd be admitted. The gate is never locked.

He offers her everything again, in the same words.

**28. (p.58)** She says no.

**29. (p.59)** She runs. They shoot her in the back within sight of the treeline. Eden hears it and comes out and finds her, and that's how she comes home.

**30. (p.60) — THE BURIAL.** John digs. Emma carves **RUTH** — the only time in the film anyone uses her whole name.

**31. (p.61)** And then Ada, thirteen and silent, opens a tin of seed and starts putting it in the ground over the grave. Nobody stops her. Then everybody helps.

**32. (p.62)** Somebody calls the place Eden. It's a joke when they say it. It doesn't stay one.

## THE CUT — MONTAGE
*pp. 62–70 — four years, scored, carried on the President's voice*

**33.** THE PRESIDENT — sixth in the line of succession, the decent one, the one everybody joked should be running things — takes the oath in a room with eleven people in it.

**34.** The grid comes back on. Food moves. Bodies get cleared. People weep at the sight of a working streetlight. **He is good at this and everything he does is real.**

**35.** The doctrine, in pieces, across a hundred broadcasts: *There was no judgment. You are not guilty. There is no one keeping score.* To a planet drowning in guilt, this is mercy. It is also permission.

**36.** A sign. Small — his hand on a dying woman in a corridor, and she gets up. Then a stadium.

**37.** And underneath it: the man with the clipboard becomes a man with a uniform becomes **DEPUTY ADMINISTRATOR SIMON WARD.** Pins in a map. Rivers crossed off, one at a time, for four years.

## PART THREE — EDEN
*pp. 70–96 — interleaved throughout*

**38. (p.70)** Eden, four years on. Green, walled, working. Forty-one people. Bees. A school of six children. Toby, six, who has never seen a city and thinks the Rapture is a story.

**39. (p.72)** Emma at twenty-five: unrecognizable, competent, running half the place. Ada at seventeen, in the garden, the only one who really knows what grows. John, still digging when he doesn't know what to do with himself.

**40. (p.74)** Emma and John in a doorway, not saying it. **And somebody says it for them** — one line, disapproving, from someone who loves them both. It is not resolved and nobody brings it up again.

**41. (p.76) — OUTSIDE.** HELEN MARCH, chief of staff, watches a broadcast go out and notices, for the first time, that she can't remember when he last slept.

**42. (p.78) — OUTSIDE.** Ward briefs GREER and sends him in. **We see this.** There is no mystery for the audience, only for Eden.

**43. (p.80)** Greer arrives at the gate, hungry and grateful. Hazel says the four things. **Nobody asks him a single question.** Ray gives him a bunk.

**44. (p.82)** Eden at its best — a wedding, a harvest, an argument about goats. Twenty minutes where the audience falls in love with a place they already know is going to be attacked.

**45. (p.84) — OUTSIDE. THE EMPTY ROOM.** Helen walks in on the President alone. The warmth is off. Not menace — *nothing.* He sits and idles. Then the door sounds and it comes back on, mid-sentence, perfect. She says nothing, then or ever. **She stays.**

**46. (p.87)** Greer, gently, over weeks: *how do you think they'd find this place? Somebody would have to have told them, wouldn't they.* The idea takes.

**47. (p.90)** Caleb finds a boot print two miles out. John tells him it's nothing. It isn't nothing and they both know it.

**48. (p.93) — OUTSIDE.** Ward's map. One pin left.

**49. (p.95)** Ada plants a second field. Emma helps her. It's the last quiet scene in the film.

## PART FOUR — THE SIEGE
*pp. 96–125*

**50. (p.96)** They come out of the trees at first light. Not a raid — an *occupation.* Hundreds. Vehicles. Tents. They don't attack. They set up.

**51. (p.98)** Ward walks to the wall alone, unarmed, and is warm. He offers terms, and the terms are the same ones he offered a girl at a fence: *you were never judged, you're not guilty, no one is keeping score. Come out and eat.* He will wait. He has time.

**52. (p.100)** Inside: it works. Not on everyone. On enough.

**53. (p.102)** The first law dies. Somebody has to have told them. Who came here and never said what they did? And Eden — a settlement founded on never asking — starts asking.

**54. (p.105)** **They land on RAY.** He was in that city. He came with John. And he will not account for the morning of the Rapture, because accounting for it means saying where he was and what he watched.

**55. (p.107) — THE TURN.** John stops asking the room what it thinks. He tells it. In front of everyone, he says exactly what he said in that church and exactly what happened after — and then he says what he wants done about Ray, and he doesn't put it to a vote.

*Four years of never giving an order, and the first one he gives is to protect a man who would rather be exiled than break the law that protects John.*

**56. (p.110)** It costs him. Half of Eden looks at him differently for the rest of the film. Delia is the one who defends him, which surprises everybody including her.

**57. (p.112) — WARD'S WEAPON.** At the wall, mildly, he tells them about Ruthie. That she gave them up. That he's known where they were, roughly, for four years. He means it to finish them.

**58. (p.114)** And then, because he cannot help himself, he tells them the rest: that he asked her a second time, and she wouldn't. That it cost her.

**He hands them their friend back and doesn't understand what he's done.**

**59. (p.116)** Hazel is the one who says they leave the name on the garden. Nobody argues.

**60. (p.117) — THE LAST NIGHT. THE GATE.** John opens it. He has to; it's the fourth law and if he locks it Eden was never real.

The children go first — Toby, and five others, and two adults to walk them out. **That is what the gate was always for.** PAUL DIETZ goes, for his kids. John doesn't say a word to him and doesn't need to. Caleb is asked to go and refuses. Ada is asked and refuses. And a few others just... go.

Nineteen people are standing there at dawn, and every one of them chose it twice with the door wide open.

**61. (p.120)** Ward's last offer, at first light. Emma answers it. Not with faith — she hasn't got any, and neither has anyone on that wall. She answers with the only thing they have, which is that they aren't leaving each other.

**62. (p.121)** Ward gives the order. **He has just lost and he doesn't know it.**

**63. (p.122)** The wall. It goes badly and it goes fast. Hazel dies in the first minutes — the only person there who would have understood what happens next, missing it by an hour. Ray. Delia. Caleb takes a round and stays up.

**64. (p.123)** Emma and John, out of ammunition, back to back. One of them starts a sentence.

**65.** The sky opens.

**66.** They come down like a Renaissance ceiling tearing loose — gold light with no source, anatomy that doesn't work, too many wings, faces that will not resolve. And they are **fading at the edges, half somewhere else.**

**And they take the line beside the defenders. Not in front. Beside.**

*Nobody in the film ever remarks on this. It is the entire revelation.*

**67.** The enemy sorts itself. The men who are only men break and run — **and are allowed to.** Nobody stops them. What's left standing doesn't run, and for the only time in the film, the enemy's true shape shows.

**68.** Hazel stands up out of the mud, fading at the edges, and she is **laughing.**

**69.** Then Ray. Then Delia. The dead get up and pick their rifles back up, and **the enemy's only weapon stops working.**

**70.** Emma sees a girl in the host she buried four years ago. Ruthie has been there the whole time. She is not sorry and she is not ashamed and she is *waiting.*

**71.** MICHAEL takes the President. No dialogue. The thing that was never a man comes apart, and as it goes it takes Ward with it — and the last thing Ward learns is that he was never a partner. **He was equipment.**

**72.** Ward reaches. And John — the man who could not stop one killing in a church four years ago — reaches back.

**It isn't enough.** Ward knew, and chose, and the rule does not bend.

*The church scene closes. John tried this time. He was always clean.*

**73.** Emma goes down. She gets up, fading. She has a few seconds, and she spends all of them **looking at Ada.**

No dialogue. The whole inheritance in one look.

**74.** John, beside her, not carrying anything for the first time in the film.

**75.** And they go. All of them. The field empties.

## EPILOGUE — THE GARDEN
*pp. 124–125*

**76.** Caleb and Ada, in ivy, in a garden four dead people built for them. Neither of them earned it. That's the point.

**77.** The camera pulls back and keeps pulling.

**78.** Something moves in the grass behind her.

*Choice is back. That's not a curse — it's the only thing that ever made any of this mean anything.*

**CUT TO BLACK.**

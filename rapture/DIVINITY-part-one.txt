DIVINITY
Part One — The First Days
First draft pages


FADE IN:

INT. KAYLA'S CAR (MOVING) - MORNING

Sunlight strobing through roadside trees. Windows down. "HIGHWAY TO HELL" up loud, and all four of them are screaming along.

KAYLA (22) drives with one wrist over the top of the wheel. TESS (21) rides shotgun with her arm out flat against the wind.

In the back: RUTHIE (22), knees up against the seat in front of her. And EMMA (21), phone face-up in her lap.

The chorus lands. All four of them nail it. Nobody can sing.

TESS
(over the music)
Say it again.

KAYLA
I'm not saying it again.

TESS
Say it.

KAYLA
One night. One. And you're all in on the gas or I swear to God I will leave every one of you in a Wawa.

RUTHIE
And then Philly.

KAYLA
And then Philly.

TESS
How long's she been in?

KAYLA
Nine days.

A beat inside the noise. Tess reaches over and turns the music down two clicks, which is as much sympathy as anybody in this car knows how to give.

KAYLA (CONT'D)
Put it back up.

Tess puts it back up.

Emma's thumb hovers over her phone. On screen: MOM.

She doesn't press it. She turns the phone face-down on her thigh.

RUTHIE
(to Emma)
You alright?

EMMA
I'll call her when we stop.

Ruthie goes back to the window.

It is an ordinary Tuesday. There is nothing wrong with any of this.

Kayla sings. Tess sings. Emma sings.

Kayla stops singing in the middle of a line.

Two seconds later, so does Tess.

Emma looks up.

EMMA (CONT'D)
Kayla.

The car drifts. Kayla's hand slides off the top of the wheel.

EMMA (CONT'D)
KAYLA —

EXT. THRUWAY - CONTINUOUS

The car leaves the road at seventy-one miles an hour.

It goes over four times.

Then it is very still, and the music is still playing.

EXT. THRUWAY - EMBANKMENT - MOMENTS LATER

Emma hangs upside down in her belt. Glass in her hair. Blood at her hairline.

Beside her Ruthie is screaming and cannot stop screaming.

Emma gets her hand to the buckle. Drops. Crawls out through where the window was.

She gets Ruthie out. They lie in wet grass, breathing.

Then Emma goes back.

She reaches through to Kayla. Two fingers to the throat. Holds them there a long time.

RUTHIE
Is she —

Emma doesn't answer. She goes around to Tess. Same. Two fingers. Holds.

Emma sits back on her heels in the grass.

There is not a mark on either of them.

EMMA
There's nothing wrong with them.

RUTHIE
What?

EMMA
There's nothing wrong with them, Ruthie. Look at them.

Ruthie looks. Two young women in a crushed car, unmarked, eyes closed, not breathing.

Emma is going through it again — throat, wrist, chest, the seatbelt, the airbags — looking for the reason. Hands starting to shake.

EMMA (CONT'D)
There's no reason.

A sound above them. Both of them look up.

EXT. SKY - CONTINUOUS

A passenger aircraft is coming down beyond the treeline, wings level, in complete silence.

No engine note. Nothing.

EXT. THRUWAY - CONTINUOUS

It goes in a mile out. The concussion arrives four seconds later and moves the grass.

Emma and Ruthie do not move.

And then the world goes quiet.

No engines on the eight lanes behind them. No aircraft. No horns.

Birds.

Ruthie is crying. Emma is listening.

CUT TO:

INT. ST. COLUMBA'S - SCHENECTADY - SAME MORNING

A plain Catholic church, three-quarters full. FATHER DUNNE (50s) at the altar. The congregation on its feet, mid-hymn.

At the very back, standing, not singing: JOHN (37). Work coat. Hands that don't belong in here. He has the look of a man attending something out of obligation to himself.

He is not praying. He is waiting for something.

The hymn goes on.

A WOMAN in the fourth row sits down.

Not faints. Sits, the way you'd sit at the end of a long day.

Then the man beside her.

Then eleven more, all across the room, at the same time, with no sound at all.

The organ keeps going for four more bars before the organist notices.

Silence.

Then it starts.

INT. ST. COLUMBA'S - CONTINUOUS

Chaos, but the wrong kind — quiet and stupid and slow. People saying names. Somebody shaking a shoulder.

A MAN drags his wife into the aisle and starts compressions on a woman who has no injury and no heartbeat and will not have either again.

MAN
Call somebody. CALL SOMEBODY —

A dozen phones. None of them work.

John hasn't moved from the back wall. He's counting. His eyes go along the pews, row by row.

Around him: sixty people standing. Forty-odd who aren't.

A YOUNG MOTHER holds a child who is fine. Beside her, her own mother is not.

Somebody at the front says it, not loudly:

PARISHIONER (O.S.)
It's the Rapture.

It moves through the room like water finding a crack. Somebody laughs. Somebody starts praying out loud. Somebody says *don't say that*.

And then a WOMAN near the front turns around and looks at Father Dunne, who is standing at the altar with a hand on the cloth, breathing.

WOMAN
Father.

Dunne looks at her.

WOMAN (CONT'D)
You're still here.

The room turns. All of it. Every face.

Dunne says nothing.

John, at the back, has understood where this is going about four seconds before anybody else.

And he says it anyway.

JOHN
If that's what this is —

Heads turn to him. He stops. He can hear himself.

JOHN (CONT'D)
— and he's still standing here —

He stops for good. Too late.

A hundred faces have already turned back to the altar.

Silence.

Then a chair goes over, three rows in.

A YOUNG MAN (early 30s — unremarkable, slight, the kind of face you would not describe afterward) is on his feet. He is shaking so badly he can barely stand.

He is looking at Father Dunne.

YOUNG MAN
He came to my house.

Nobody moves.

YOUNG MAN (CONT'D)
When I was nine. He came to my house and my mother made him a plate.

Dunne says nothing. He does not deny it. That is the worst thing that happens in the entire scene.

YOUNG MAN (CONT'D)
I told Monsignor Hayes. In ninety-nine. I told him and he moved him to Rome and then he moved him back.

His voice goes. He gets it back.

YOUNG MAN (CONT'D)
He's still standing there.

INT. ST. COLUMBA'S - CONTINUOUS

A MAN in the second row moves first. Not fast. He just walks up and takes hold of Dunne's arm.

John comes off the back wall.

JOHN
Hey. HEY —

He starts down the aisle.

Two more men move in. Then a woman. Dunne goes down behind the rail and out of sight.

John gets a hand on somebody's shoulder and is put on the floor for it.

What follows is not a fight and it is not exciting.

It is forty ordinary people who do not know how to do this, in a confined space, taking far too long. There is shoving. There is a lot of noise and most of it is people telling each other to stop. Somebody is sobbing. Somebody is praying. Two men pull others off and are pulled off themselves.

Some of them stop.

Some of them don't.

John gets up twice and gets between them twice and is put down twice.

We hold on the YOUNG MAN, who has not moved from the third row.

He does not swing. He does not go forward.

He watches, with his hands at his sides, while forty people take it out of his hands.

Something goes out of his face while he watches. We won't understand what it was for another hour.

INT. ST. COLUMBA'S - LATER

It is over.

Forty people are standing in a church looking at their own hands. Somebody is being sick in the vestibule. Nobody is talking.

Father Dunne is on the floor in front of the rail.

John gets up off the tile. His mouth is bleeding. Nobody looks at him.

He goes out.

The third row is empty.

EXT. STATE STREET - SCHENECTADY - DAY

John comes out into a street where every car has stopped where it was.

Some are still running. One has its door open and its indicator going.

A hundred yards up, a bus is across both lanes.

John stands on the step and looks at it for a while.

Then he walks.

EXT. ST. COLUMBA'S - CHURCHYARD - DUSK

A strip of grass between the church and the fence.

John is waist-deep in it, digging, with a spade he found. Coat off. Shirt through.

Behind him, wrapped in the altar cloth, is Father Dunne.

John digs. He does not stop. He does not appear to be thinking about anything at all.

INT. ST. COLUMBA'S - SACRISTY WINDOW - CONTINUOUS

Dark in here.

The YOUNG MAN from the third row is sitting on the floor with his back against the wall and his hands between his knees.

He has been here for hours.

He gets up. Goes to the window.

Through it, in the last of the light: a stranger, alone, digging a hole in the ground for a man that forty people killed on his account.

The young man watches.

He does not go out. He does not say anything.

EXT. CHURCHYARD - LATER

Full dark. John tamps the last of it flat with the back of the spade.

He stands there a while.

Then he picks up his coat and goes.

He never sees the sacristy door open on the far side of the building, or the young man walk out of it and away up the street.

FADE TO:

EXT. ROUTE 5 DEVELOPMENT - DAY (DAY 3)

A forty-one unit build, framed and sheathed, three storeys, plastic snapping on the scaffold.

A hand-painted board at the entrance: HALLORAN BUILDING CO.

John's truck comes in over the gravel and stops.

He gets out.

An excavator sits with its door open. There is a man in the cab. He has been there three days.

John looks at him for a long moment.

JOHN
(quietly)
Hey, Manny.

He does not go closer.

EXT. ROUTE 5 DEVELOPMENT - CONTINUOUS

John walks it. Slowly. All of it.

Trailer — two more men inside, and he closes that door and leaves it closed.

Foundations. Drainage runs. Stacked block. A pallet of windows.

Eleven years of learning how to do this, standing in front of him, finished except for the part that finishes it.

He stops in the middle of the site.

He is not crying. He is doing arithmetic that does not come out.

JOHN (V.O.)
(flat, to nobody)
Nobody's going to foreclose on it.

INT. UNIT 12 - CONTINUOUS

Bare studs. Sawdust. Light through unglazed openings.

Doors came in on Thursday. Still under plastic against the wall.

John pulls the plastic off.

INT. UNIT 12 - LATER

He hangs a door.

Shims it. Checks it. Levels off the hinge side. Sets the strike plate. Drives the screws.

Swings it once. Swings it twice.

It is perfect.

He stands looking at it — a door that works, in a building nobody is ever going to live in.

Then he sits down on the subfloor with his back against the stud wall.

He stays there until it's dark.

EXT. ST. COLUMBA'S - DAY (DAY 5)

John's truck. He gets out with the spade.

INT. ST. COLUMBA'S - CONTINUOUS

He stops just inside the door.

Forty-one of them are still in the pews. Five days now. Some have slid down where they fell.

Somebody has put a coat over a woman in the third row and gone away and not come back for her.

John looks at it a long time.

Then he goes back out for the spade.

EXT. CHURCHYARD - FENCE LINE - DAY

A trench. John, alone, working.

EXT. CHURCHYARD - NEXT MORNING

John is already in the trench when RAY WHELAN (66 — tall, stooped, white beard, carpenter's pencil behind his ear) comes around the corner of the building.

Ray stops.

They look at each other.

Ray doesn't say why he came back. John doesn't ask.

Ray goes to the truck, takes the other spade, comes back, and gets in the trench.

They dig.

RAY
Ground's better down your end.

JOHN
It's not.

RAY
Looks better.

JOHN
It's not.

They dig.

EXT. CHURCHYARD - DAY (SERIES OF SHOTS)

Six days.

-- The trench lengthening. The two of them at opposite ends of it.

-- Carrying. This is the worst part and the film does not look away from how hard it is, or how long it takes two men to carry one person.

-- John on his knees with a marker pen and a fence picket. He writes: MARGARET T. Then, on the next: GREY CARDIGAN, GLASSES.

RAY
You don't know her?

JOHN
No.

RAY
Put something.

JOHN
I did.

-- Ray, digging, mid-anecdote:

RAY (CONT'D)
— and this is a dog that could not find a bowl. Not could not find *food*. Could not find the *bowl*. Same bowl. Same corner. Nine years.

JOHN
Duke.

RAY
Duke. Stupidest animal ever born in Schenectady County and I will say that in front of anybody.

They dig.

Neither of them says one word about the man in the first grave, or the morning, or the church, or what either of them did in it.

They are never going to.

INT. PARISH HALL - NIGHT

Camp beds and mattresses among stacked folding tables. Nine people. One oil lamp.

John comes in filthy, sits on the end of a table, eats out of a tin.

They are all looking at him.

WOMAN (BEV)
Is anybody coming?

JOHN
I don't know.

MAN (DENNIS)
Radio's got nothing since Thursday. Nothing on any of it.

JOHN
Alright.

BEV
Is the city worse?

JOHN
Probably.

DENNIS
So do we go north, or —

JOHN
What do you all reckon?

Silence. They look at each other. This is the fourth time today.

BEV
We're asking you.

JOHN
(not unkindly)
Somebody else should call it.

He goes back to his tin.

They keep looking at him.

He is not being humble. There is a thing he said out loud in a building forty feet from here on Tuesday morning, and he watched what it did, and he is not doing it again.

They follow him anyway. That's the problem.

EXT. PARISH HALL - NIGHT (THIRD WEEK)

Rain. Dennis and a young man, CODY (19), stand at a rain barrel and taste the water and spit it out.

INT. PARISH HALL - NIGHT

Fifteen of them now. The argument has been going an hour.

DENNIS
Water's gone. That's the third barrel.

BEV
There were men on the county road again last night. Ricky saw torches.

CODY
Two nights ago it was torches. Last night it was a truck.

DENNIS
So we move.

BEV
Where?

DENNIS
North. Away from Albany.

BEV
With what? We've got one truck with a quarter tank.

Everybody turns to John, who has been at the back of the room not saying anything.

DENNIS
John.

JOHN
I hear you.

DENNIS
And?

JOHN
What do you want to do?

DENNIS
I want you to say it.

JOHN
Then say it yourselves and I'll carry whatever you pick.

Dennis stares at him.

DENNIS
That's not the same thing.

JOHN
No. It isn't.

Nobody decides anything. It gets late. They go to bed.

They will do this again tomorrow, and the day after that.

EXT. PARISH HALL - NIGHT (THIRD NIGHT)

Four men come up the path with a light.

It is over quickly and it is not dramatic. They take the fuel and the food and most of the tools.

Cody gets in the way of it.

INT. PARISH HALL - LATER

Cody on a mattress. Ray on the floor beside him, holding his hand.

Ray is telling him about the dog.

RAY
— same bowl, same corner. Nine years. You'd have loved him. Everybody loved him. Christ knows why.

Cody's eyes are open. He isn't tracking.

John stands in the doorway and does not come in.

INT. PARISH HALL - DAY (TWO DAYS LATER)

Cody is under a blanket. Bev is folding the other one.

BEV
(to John, gently)
It wasn't your fault, love.

JOHN
No.

BEV
Nobody thinks that.

JOHN
No.

She touches his arm on the way past.

That is worse than anything anybody could have said to him.

EXT. PARISH HALL - LATER

They load the truck. Somebody else says when.

They go north on the Friday.

EXT. BEHIND THE PARISH HALL - NIGHT

John, alone, out past the light.

He gets down on one knee. Not gracefully. Like a man doing a thing he has seen other people do.

JOHN
Alright.

Nothing.

JOHN (CONT'D)
I'd like to know what I did.

Nothing.

JOHN (CONT'D)
Because I put eleven years in. And I lost it, and I got back up, and I put nine more in, and I had it — I had it in April, I had the loan and the paper and the crew, and I told people. I stood in a room and told people You did that. I said that out loud.

Nothing.

JOHN (CONT'D)
And then in June a bank in Delaware went under, and I don't know anybody at that bank, and I've never been to Delaware —

He stops.

He hears it. What is coming out of him is not a prayer. It's an invoice.

He stays down there a moment longer.

Then he gets up and goes inside and never tries again, and never tells one living soul that he tried.

CUT TO:

EXT. RURAL ROAD - UPSTATE NEW YORK - DAY

Emma and Ruthie walking. Packs. Ruthie's is bigger and Emma is carrying it.

EXT. EMMA'S PARENTS' HOUSE - DAY

A driveway in a small town. A basketball hoop over the garage.

Emma stands at the end of the drive and does not go in.

Ruthie waits behind her.

Emma goes in.

INT. EMMA'S PARENTS' HOUSE - CONTINUOUS

Kitchen. Her MOTHER is on the floor by the dishwasher.

Hall. Her FATHER, half out of the den.

And upstairs, at the end, a bedroom door with a hand-lettered sign on it that says exactly what a fifteen-year-old's door sign says.

Emma stands with her hand flat on the door for a long moment.

Then she opens it.

DANNY (15) is on the bed with his headphones still on.

EXT. EMMA'S BACKYARD - DAY

Emma is digging.

A garden spade. Clay soil. It is not the right tool and it is going to take days.

Ruthie sits on the back step.

RUTHIE
I can —

EMMA
It's alright.

RUTHIE
I want to help.

EMMA
It's alright, Ruthie.

Ruthie sits back down.

EXT. EMMA'S BACKYARD - LATER (SERIES)

Two days.

Three graves.

Emma's hands, at the end of it.

Ruthie on the step, saying she's sorry, over and over, in a small voice, until it stops meaning anything at all.

Emma does not tell her it's alright. She has stopped saying that.

EXT. EMMA'S BACKYARD - DUSK

Emma stands over three mounds of turned clay.

She was the only person in that house who ever went to church. She is the only person in that house who is still here.

She does not say anything. She does not know anything to say.

EXT. ROAD NORTH - DAY (SERIES OF SHOTS)

The first two weeks.

-- A supermarket, half stripped. Two MEN arguing over a shelf that has gone over.

MAN A
It's a weapon, it's a weapon, it came off a satellite —

MAN B
Then where's the burns? Where's ONE MARK on ONE of them?

MAN A
You don't know what you're —

MAN B
There's no mark because there's nothing to find, and there's nothing to find because it wasn't a THING —

It stops being an argument. Emma pulls Ruthie out of the aisle and they are already outside when the noise changes.

Neither of those men was right. Neither of them could ever be proved wrong.

-- A crossroads. Somebody has spray-painted a wall: WHERE ARE THEY. Underneath, in different paint: WITH GOD. Underneath that, in a third hand: THEN WHY NOT ME.

-- Smoke on three horizons.

-- Ruthie asleep sitting up. Emma awake.

EXT. FIELD - DAY

Emma and Ruthie come over a stile and stop.

In the field: eleven graves.

Mounded. Squared. Fence pickets driven in at the head of each. A few of them written on.

Nobody anywhere. No house, no road, no smoke, for as far as either of them can see.

RUTHIE
(low)
Emma.

Emma is not frightened.

She walks down the line of them, reading the pickets.

EMMA
(reading)
"Blue coat. Wedding ring."

RUTHIE
Who does this?

Emma doesn't answer.

She crouches at the end of the row and puts her hand flat on the last one, feeling how well it's been squared.

She knows exactly what she is looking at. She knows how long it takes. She knows what your hands feel like afterward.

EMMA
Somebody who knows how.

She stands up and looks north, the way the field goes.

EXT. WOODLAND TRACK - DAY (FOUR DAYS LATER)

Emma and Ruthie walking. More graves passed and not remarked on now — two here, one there, at the edges of things.

Ruthie has stopped asking about them.

EXT. RIDGE - CONTINUOUS

Emma comes up over a rise.

Below her, forty yards down, in a clearing at the edge of the trees:

A man is standing chest-deep in a hole in the ground.

He straightens up.

He is filthy. He has a spade. There are two finished graves behind him and a third started.

He looks up at her without any particular expectation. He does not reach for anything. He does not ask her anything.

Emma comes down the slope.

Ruthie stays at the top.

Emma stops at the edge of the hole and looks down into it, and then at the two finished ones, and then at him.

Neither of them says anything.

There is a second spade lying in the grass.

Emma picks it up.

FADE OUT.

END OF PART ONE

Why it's cut this way

  • The villain narrates. Every word of voice-over in the middle third belongs to Ward, and every word of it is comforting. The audience spends forty seconds being reassured by the worst thing in the film before they know what he is.
  • Three seconds of true silence at 0:29. In a room full of trailers, silence is the loudest available choice. It also does the film's actual work: the sound of the world going out.
  • The angels are one frame. Gold light and faces looking up, and no explanation of what they're looking at. Anyone who's seen the film knows. Nobody else can guess, and it's the reason they buy a ticket.
  • The President's resurrection is the trailer's biggest reveal and it costs nothing to give away — it happens in act two of the film and it makes the last third make sense.
  • The button is the whole marketing strategy. Two and a half minutes of dread, then two old men and a dead dog. It tells an audience that these are people worth spending two hours with, and it's the line they repeat in the car park.
  • No narrator, no "in a world." Nothing in this trailer explains the premise. The cards say two things and then stop.

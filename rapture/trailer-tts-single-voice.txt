DIVINITY. Trailer.


Black.

A radio clicks on. Highway to Hell.

Four girls in a car with the windows down, screaming along, out of tune, delighted. A bare foot on the dash. A hand out the window surfing the wind.

Nothing in the frame is wrong.

The girl driving stops singing.

Two seconds later, so does the one beside her.

The song keeps going. Nobody has turned it off.

The car leaves the road.


Silence.


Then birdsong. Just birdsong.

Emma, upside down in a seatbelt, glass in her hair, breathing.

An interstate. Eight lanes. Every car stopped where it was. Nothing in the frame is moving.

Beyond the treeline, something enormous is burning.

Billions died in a single morning.

A church. People on the floor between the pews. A hand still resting on an open hymnal.

Every one of them left a body behind.

There is no wound. There is no cause. There is nothing to find.

Because there was never anything to find.

Two men in a supermarket, screaming at each other over a fallen shelf.

A shovel going into the ground. A man waist deep in it, filthy, working.

Forty graves in a churchyard, squared and mounded, names written on fence pickets.

A girl comes over a rise. There is a man standing in a hole.

She picks up the second shovel. Neither of them says anything.


Four years.

Green. A wall of stacked timber. Bees. Children running a goat down a path. An old woman lighting a lamp she cannot afford to light.

There was no judgment.

A checkpoint at night. A young woman with a blanket around her shoulders and a bowl in her hands, and a man crouched in front of her, being kind.

You are not guilty of anything.

Her face. She believes him. Anyone would.

There is nobody keeping score.

A stadium. Eighty thousand people on their feet. A man in a suit with blood dried down his front, standing up.


The treeline at first light. Vehicles. Then more of them. Then the counting stops being funny.

Come out. There is food.

Open the gate.

You are not serious.

The gate swings wide, and six children walk out through it into the dark, and a nineteen year old boy holds it open for them with both hands.

We were told what we are supposed to do.

Rifles laid out on a table. Bandages rolled. A lamp still burning.

Nobody ever came up with anything better.

And who do you imagine is watching?


Nobody.


We stayed anyway.


The wall breaking. A boy taking a round and staying up. Fire going over timber. A treeline full of men running the wrong way.

One frame. Gold light with no source in it, and nineteen filthy, exhausted faces turned up into it.


DIVINITY.


Black.

A shovel goes into dirt. Twice.

Stupidest animal ever born in Schenectady County.

You have told me.

I will tell you again.

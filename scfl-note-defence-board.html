Every Defence In The League, Priced

Thirteen defences are sitting free and not one of them is better than the two already on this roster. The upgrade is not a defence. It is the roster place one of them is standing on.

A defence is the one position where the honest number is the median and not the average. They are streamed, their weeks are lumpy, and one return touchdown against a bad quarterback will carry a mean for a month. The question is not what a defence has scored. It is what it pays in a normal week.

A normal week in this league pays 8.0. That is the median of all 148 defence-weeks this season under these settings, and it is the bar everything below has to clear.

The two already here

Houston is a top-six defence in this league by any of the three measures and has gone 0, 12, 16, 7, 19 — the 19 being its best week of the year, last week. Cleveland has gone 1, 10, 10, 15, 14, which is the opposite shape: no ceiling, no floor either, and climbing. Neither of them is the problem.

And the thirteen on the wire
Carolina averages 10.2 and typically pays 5. One afternoon is the entire case for them.

Carolina is the trap on this board and it is worth naming. The mean says 10.2, better than Cleveland. The median says 5.0. They scored 29 in week two — three interceptions, two fumble recoveries, three sacks, a touchdown and three points conceded — and 2, 7 and 3 in their other three games. Claiming Carolina off that average is the defensive version of buying a receiver for his touchdowns.

New England is the same shape, gentler: a 25 in week two and 8, 0, 7, 10 around it. Take the one big week out of either and both sit well below the two defences already on this roster.

Detroit is the only real argument

Detroit has twenty sacks, more than any defence in football, and a median of 13 that beats Houston's. It also concedes 29.6 points a game, fourth-worst in the league, and this scoring charges a point for conceding 28 and four points for conceding 35. That is why a defence with the best pass rush in the NFL has a mean of 9.2.

So Detroit is not an upgrade. It is a different bet: more sacks, more weeks in the red on points allowed, and a zero already on the board. Houston does the same thing with eighteen sacks and seven fewer points conceded a game.

What to actually do
The move

Cut Cleveland, and do not replace it with a defence. Nothing free is better, and with thirteen defences sitting unowned there will still be thirteen sitting unowned the week Houston has its bye.

Cleveland's median is 10 and the best free median is Detroit's 13, so on the one week Houston is off the bench place is worth nothing at all. Spend it on a 23-year-old instead.

One thing this desk could not check: the NFL schedule file is not reachable from here, so Houston's bye week is unknown. If it is the next one, hold Cleveland one more week and stream the bye from the thirteen.

The thing worth saying last is that the question assumed one of the two was bad. Neither is. Houston's median week is fifth-best of all thirty-two defences and Cleveland's clears the league median by two. The roster has a defence problem only in the sense that it has one defence too many.

How this was worked out. There is no way to ask Sleeper what an unrostered defence scored — it only reports players somebody owns — so every defence here is scored from nflverse play-by-play through this league's own settings: 2 a sack, 2 a takeaway, 6 a touchdown, 4 a safety, 2 a blocked kick, and the points-allowed ladder from +10 for a shutout down to −4 for 35 and over. The sum was then checked against what Sleeper actually paid the twenty owned defences in weeks one to four: 60 of 64 weeks match to the penny, and all four misses are out by exactly one two-point event. Treat every figure as good to about two points.

The Game of the Week

Won By A Sixteen-Yard Completion

Gumbas 102.36 · Master-Jeti 101.64
Master-Jeti had led since the middle of the fourth. Matthew Stafford threw three touchdowns to close it, and then a routine throw worth a third of a point with thirty-seven seconds left took the game.

Ten lead changes. Seven of them in the second quarter alone, two teams swapping the front of the scoreboard on 11-yard runs and 28-yard catches, neither able to build anything that lasted more than a couple of minutes of game time. And then, with seven minutes and forty-eight seconds left in the fourth quarter, Master-Jeti stood at 101.64 and the game looked finished. Nick Folk's 48-yarder had just taken the Gumbas to 76.70, and that is where they were: twenty-five adrift with a quarter to play.

Matthew Stafford threw a four-yard touchdown with 4:56 left. He threw a two-yarder with 2:25 left. He threw a five-yarder with 1:42 left. Three touchdown passes inside three and a half minutes of game clock took the Gumbas from twenty-five back to a point and a half back, and Master-Jeti had not scored since the Folk kick and would not score again.

What won it was not a touchdown. With thirty-seven seconds on the clock Stafford completed a sixteen-yard pass — no score, no celebration, a first down — and it was worth 0.32 points. It put the Gumbas ahead 101.82 to 101.64 and that was the game.

Stafford finished on 25.7. The New York Jets defence put up 20.0 behind him and Nick Folk's boot had 12.0. For Master-Jeti, Trevor Lawrence's 19.1, Braelon Allen's 17.6 and Aaron Jones's 15.7 were a better top three than the Gumbas could show and it did not matter, because all of it had been banked before the fourth quarter started.

The Gumbas win their second of the season and go to 2-3. Master-Jeti drop their first in four and sit 3-2. Nought point seven two is the narrowest margin anybody has been beaten by in 2026, and the difference between those two teams in the table is now one game and a decimal point.

The Games

Five Lead Changes, Then A Hundred And Nine

THE MACHINES 156.14 · Horsecollars 46.56
For one quarter the Horsecollars were in it. They were not in it again, and the final margin is the biggest anyone has managed this season.

The first quarter of this game was a genuine fight. The lead changed five times in it — Jaylen Warren's 80-yard touchdown catch, a 14-yard Tyler Shough completion, a Devaughn Vele grab, a Laquon Treadwell catch, the two teams trading the front of the scoreboard every ninety seconds like neither wanted to hold it. At the end of it the Machines led 18-17.

Nothing after the first quarter went the Horsecollars' way at all.

Warren took a 50-yard run to the house nine minutes into the second and the Machines never gave the lead back. The third quarter was the execution: Jared Goff threw touchdowns from 38, from 8 and from 39 yards, then ran one in himself from a yard out, and Trey McBride caught another with twenty-eight seconds left in the period. In fifteen minutes of game time the Machines went from 40 points to 91. The Horsecollars scored two in the same stretch.

Warren finished with 30.8, Goff with 29.5, Tyler Huntley with 24.0 — five men at twenty or better on one roster. The Horsecollars' leading scorer was Geno Smith with 8.3, tied with a tight end. Tyler Shough threw two interceptions inside the first half and finished at minus 2.1.

46.56 is the lowest score anybody has posted since the opening week, and the Horsecollars managed it in the week they met the only other unbeaten side left on their side of the table.

The Machines are 5-0 and top of the Spartan. The Horsecollars drop to 4-1 but keep the Four Horsemen, and a hundred and nine points of damage does nothing to their record and a great deal to their points-against column, which is the tiebreaker nobody thinks about in October and everybody thinks about in December.

Settled By The Interval

Pork Chop Express 126.98 · The Powers of Pain 101.4
Pork Chop Express never trailed, and by half-time the Powers of Pain were chasing a game that had stopped being competitive.

Not one lead change in sixty minutes. Jalon Daniels threw a five-yard touchdown twelve minutes into the first and Pork Chop Express were in front from then until the whistle, which is a tidier way to win a football game than most of this league managed.

The damage was done in the second quarter. D'Andre Swift ran one in from a yard, Justin Jefferson caught a 15-yarder, Matt Gay kicked from 46 and Brock Bowers had already taken a 13-yard touchdown catch at the end of the first. By the time the Powers of Pain got going — C.J. Stroud with a two-yard run and a nine-yard throw, Matthew Golden with a 50-yard catch — they were staring at a 65-54 deficit that had been a good deal worse.

The third quarter was the one that mattered. Sam LaPorta's 38-yard touchdown catch and Jaxon Smith-Njigba's 16-yarder took the Express back out to double figures, and Brock Purdy's interception in between was the Powers of Pain's best chance to turn it, except it went the wrong way.

James Cook's 17-yard touchdown run with four minutes left in the fourth ended the argument, and his lost fumble two minutes later was the kind of thing a team can afford when it is twenty clear.

Pork Chop Express are 4-1 and riding a three-game run, second in Black and Blue behind the only other unbeaten in the division. The Powers of Pain are 2-3 — still top of the Spectre Syndicate, which says more about the Spectre Syndicate than it does about the Powers of Pain.

A Defence, A Kicker And Nobody To Stop Them

Lil’ Chops 136.72 · n.W.o 71.94
Lil' Chops scored 136 without a single lead change, and two of their biggest contributors never touched the ball on offence.

Lil' Chops led from the opening exchange to the final whistle and were never once headed. By the middle of the third they were at 123 and the game had been over for some time.

The remarkable thing is where it came from. Bucky Irving led the way with 29.5, which is ordinary enough, but the San Francisco defence put up 26.0 — a defensive touchdown four minutes from the end of the third among it — and that is a lot of points from a unit that cannot be game-planned for. Aaron Rodgers added 23.3 and Garrett Wilson 16.4.

n.W.o's fourth quarter was the sort that gets played out in an empty stadium. The Atlanta defence returned one for six, Tyler Loop kicked from 51 twice inside a minute of game clock, and Josh Allen threw a touchdown and ran another in during the last four minutes. Allen finished on 28.3, the best single score on the losing side all week, and it moved the needle not at all.

The bench will haunt them. Isaiah Likely sat on 21.4 while Tyson Bagent started and finished on minus 0.6 — a twenty-two point swing available in the same seat, and the largest piece of hindsight in the league this week.

Lil' Chops go to 3-2 and third in the Spartan. n.W.o fall to 2-3 and have now conceded sixty-four or more twice in five weeks.

Davante Adams Decided It In The Fourth

BIG BLUE 105.8 · Guido Haters 80.7
Four lead changes and a two-point conversion kept this alive into the final quarter. Then one receiver caught two touchdowns and that was the end of it.

This was the closest thing to a proper contest in the middle of the card. The lead changed four times — a Washington fumble recovery, a Joe Burrow two-yard touchdown, a 45-yard Ja'Marr Chase catch that put the Guido Haters a point up at 48.24 — and it stayed inside single figures deep into the third.

Mike Evans settled the back-and-forth with, of all things, a two-point conversion catch with two and a half minutes left in the third quarter. It was worth two points, it put BIG BLUE ahead 81.20 to 80.00, and it was the last time the lead moved all game.

Davante Adams did the rest. A four-yard touchdown catch with six minutes left in the fourth and a five-yarder with two minutes to go turned a one-point game into a twenty-six point one, and Adams finished on 17.1 having been nowhere for three quarters.

Joe Burrow's 28.8 was the top score on the board. For the Guido Haters, Chase's 13.7 led a side where five men scored between 9 and 14 and nobody scored more — an even spread is a fine thing in a tight game and no use at all when the other side has somebody who can put thirteen points on in four minutes.

BIG BLUE move to 2-3. The Guido Haters are 1-4 and bottom of the Spectre Syndicate, which, given the state of that division, still leaves them three games off its lead.

A Hundred And Seventy-Eight

Beaver Eaters 178.28 · Smoke Dragons 82.7
The Beaver Eaters led from the first snap to the last and put up the fifth-biggest week this league has recorded. Their quarterback alone outscored seven teams.

Not one lead change. Javonte Williams ran a yard for a touchdown thirteen minutes into the first quarter and the Beaver Eaters were never behind for a second of the sixty.

By the end of the first they had 53. By halftime, 117. Drake Maye threw touchdowns of 35, 11, 34 and 8 yards and ran one in from three; Christian Watson caught two, from 42 and 53; Spencer Shrader kicked from 57, 56 and 40 before the interval. Maye's 40.0 on its own finished within seven points of everything the Horsecollars managed with ten men on the same afternoon.

Jahmyr Gibbs added two short touchdown runs in the third to take it past 176, and the only points the Beaver Eaters surrendered after that were Mark Andrews losing a fumble early in the fourth. Gibbs 32.7, Watson 26.3, Shrader 21.0 from the kicker's spot, Malik Nabers 18.1 — five men over eighteen.

The Smoke Dragons were not disgraced so much as irrelevant. Jameis Winston threw three touchdowns and finished on 25.2, Kyler Murray added 15.7, the Houston defence 12.0 and Bijan Robinson 11.8, and 82.70 would have beaten four other teams on this week's card. It did not come close here.

178.28 is the fifth-highest score in the recorded history of this league and the biggest since 2022. The Beaver Eaters are 5-0, top of the Black and Blue and the first seed. The Smoke Dragons are 2-3, second in the Spectre Syndicate, and have now conceded 586 points in five weeks — more than anybody.

Five Field Goals In A Quarter

Killer Klowns 119.48 · Phins Phag 69.3
Phins Phag's kicker had the best day of anybody on their roster by some distance, and they still lost by fifty.

The Killer Klowns led from first to last and were never seriously inconvenienced. Jayden Daniels put up 28.3, the Green Bay defence 18.0, Pat Freiermuth 16.5 — a spread across the roster that never let Phins Phag get a run at them.

The game's genuine oddity belonged to the losing side. In the third quarter alone Jason Myers kicked field goals from 49, 50, 43, 48 and 40 yards — five of them, inside fifteen minutes of game clock, worth 22 points between them. He finished the week on 23.0, which was the highest score on his roster and nearly a third of everything Phins Phag managed.

A kicker outscoring every other man on a team is a statistic that tells you exactly what sort of afternoon it was. Jacory Croskey-Merritt's 14.2 was the next best. Keenan Allen managed 4.5.

Kyren Williams's one-yard touchdown run with eleven seconds left in the fourth was the final insult, a score nobody needed that pushed the margin past fifty.

The Killer Klowns are 2-3. Phins Phag are 0-5, the only team in the league still without a win, and have the top pick in next year's draft sitting in their lap if the season carries on as it has started.

Built In The First Half, Survived In The Second

StillTheCream 106.34 · Wookie Leaks 98.34
StillTheCream scored 105 of their 106 points before half-time and then watched Wookie Leaks come for them the whole way.

There were four lead changes in this game and all of them came in the first twenty minutes, which gives a misleading impression of how it felt. Parker Washington caught touchdowns from 51 and 27 yards inside the first seven minutes for Wookie Leaks; Rhamondre Stevenson's 12-yard run put them in front at 41.22; Josh Downs and Austin Ekeler traded the lead back and forth either side of the second-quarter whistle.

Then StillTheCream scored 59 points in eight minutes of game time. Ka'imi Fairbairn kicked from 53, Jordan Love threw a 53-yard touchdown, Deshaun Watson found a 29-yarder, and by the time Watson lost a fumble with eighteen seconds left in the half they stood at 105.34 and Wookie Leaks were on 49.10.

StillTheCream scored precisely one more point for the rest of the game.

Wookie Leaks spent thirty minutes of game clock chipping at it — Jacoby Brissett's two-yard touchdown pass, Jameson Williams from 8 and then from 39, a run of scoring that would have won most weeks. They got it back to within eight and ran out of quarters. Brissett threw an interception and George Holani lost a fumble in the same stretch of the third, and either of those is the margin.

StillTheCream take their second in a row and climb to 2-3, third in the Spectre Syndicate. Wookie Leaks are 1-4 and bottom of the Black and Blue — a division containing the league's best team and one of its worst, with very little in between.

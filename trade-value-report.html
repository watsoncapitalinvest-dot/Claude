The Findings — six years of trades, decoded

Every league tells itself a story about how it trades. The SCFL’s story is that it trades constantly, recklessly, and mostly on vibes. The ledger says otherwise. Behind six years of 2 a.m. “🚨 OFFICIAL TRADE 🚨” posts sits a market with rules — prices that hold, seasons that repeat, and one position that quietly costs more than everything else.

We pulled all 235 official trades from 2021 through 2026, put every pick and player on a single dollar scale — the 1.01 = $100 — and let the numbers talk. Here’s what they said.

The market never sleeps — but it has a clock

August is the floor of the trading pit (73 deals); November is the deadline spike (43). And the currency changes with the calendar. In July, four of every five picks that move are this year’s spot picks — everyone jockeying for draft position. By September the draft is done and 93–100% of every pick that trades is a future pick. Spot picks have a two-month shelf life; future picks are the year-round money.

“A merely-startable quarterback is worth more than any pick outside the top five. There is no overpaying for a QB — only paying the tax.”

Quarterbacks are the most expensive thing in the building

Superflex bends the entire price curve around the position. Aging Dak Prescott fetched two future firsts. Jared Goff, in his MVP year, cost picks 9 and 25 plus a first (~$111). Even Jimmy Garoppolo — a mid QB1 — went for Alvin Kamara and a pick. A startable quarterback outprices any pick outside the top five. In this league you don’t overpay for a QB; you pay the tax.

Prices move — anchor to the range, not the box score

The cautionary tale is Cooper Kupp. At his 2022 peak the Dragons paid three first-round picks for him. One year and one hamstring later, the same player fetched a single first — a 60% haircut in twelve months. The cheap end says it too: a fading vet — Keenan Allen, Mike Williams, Waller, Adrian Peterson — is worth exactly one future second, every time. The market has a fixed price for a name on the way down.

The blockbusters — and who runs the room

The richest deal on record is AJ Brown for three firsts and a second. The 2022 “Wookie Blowup” shipped Murray, Herbert, Fournette and Henry through one two-team megadeal. And two desks — Pork Chop Express and the New World Order — have their fingerprints on roughly two of every three trades the league has ever made.

What it means for the Dragons

You hold picks 15 and 16 — two solid late firsts. Don’t kid yourself that they buy the top of the board: in this league the top 3 are a closed shop that only opens for a future top-3 or equivalent, and two mid-firsts don’t come close. What 15 and 16 are is ammunition — enough to slide into the 6–10 range for a target, or to headline a package for the QB you actually need. You’re thin at quarterback in the one league that taxes the position hardest. The play writes itself: pay the QB tax, and pay it early, before the draft drains the shelf of startable arms. The rest of this feature is the receipts.

The Playbook

Ten rules the ledger hands you — how to actually work this market
2Advice & Tips

Everything below falls out of the data on the following pages. If you remember nothing else from this feature, remember these.

🛒 Buying

Pay the QB tax, and pay it early. A startable Superflex QB beats any pick outside the top 5. The shelf of usable arms only thins as the draft nears — buy before the run.
Never pay peak for a player past peak. Kupp went $150 → $55 in a year. Buy the dip on names the league has soured on; don’t chase last year’s box score.
Don’t overpay to move up — and know the ceiling. The top 3 are a closed shop; 15 + 16 won’t buy them. They’re enough to jump into the 6–10 range for a target, or to headline a QB package. Otherwise keep both swings.

💰 Selling

Sell fading vets to November contenders, never to rebuilders — same player, a ~$20 swing depending on who you call.
A fading name = one future 2nd. That’s the league’s universal price (Keenan, M. Williams, Waller, AP all cashed out for exactly that). Don’t take less; don’t pay more.
Move spot picks in July, at a premium, before the draft passes and this-year picks vanish from the market entirely.

⏰ Timing

Line your deals up for August & November. That’s when the market is actually open — 73 and 43 deals. September and December are dead.
Hold future picks as year-round currency. Once the draft passes they’re the only pick that trades — the entire fall runs on them.

⚖ Pricing

Anchor to the range, not the last game. Discount hard for age (RB 27+, WR 30+); the grid is the baseline, the age curve is the swing.
Add both sides in dollars, then land the band. Within 8% is fair, over 18% gets vetoed — then add your own need on top before you call it an overpay.
THE ONE-LINE VERSION

Buy quarterbacks early, sell old names to contenders in November, price everything off the 1.01 = $100 scale, and never confuse a hot month for a permanent value. The market has rules — now you have them too.

Market Laws — how SCFL actually prices assets

The top 3 are a closed shop. Nobody trades out of the top 3 for a bundle of mid-firsts — only for another top pick or a future top-3 / equivalent. Mind the cliff from pick 3 to pick 4.
Players buy picks. The vast majority of pick trades are player-for-pick, not pick-for-pick.
Current mid-1st ≈ a future 1st. A pick in hand this year trades flat for next year’s first (~$45).
A startable QB > any pick outside the top 5. Superflex bends the board around the position.
Late first (13–16) ≈ a solid rookie or a WR2 — real, but nowhere near top-3 money.
Late 2nd / a fading vet ≈ a future 2nd (~$20). The league’s loose change.

THE SUPERFLEX QB TAX — the single most important price in your league

Because you start two QBs, quarterbacks are the most expensive assets in the SCFL — the ledger proves it. Dak (aging) fetched two future firsts (~$110). Goff in his MVP year cost picks 9 + 25 + a future first (~$111). Jimmy G — a mid QB1 — still cost Kamara plus a pick (~$85). A merely-startable QB1 is worth more than any pick outside the top 5. You are thin at QB: expect to pay the tax, and pay it early — the price only rises as the league’s startable QBs dry up.

How to Price a Deal

Add up both sides, land it in a band, then apply the adjustments
7The Method

Price a Trade Idea — add up both sides, then apply four adjustments

Within 8% — dead fair, do it 8–18% — a winner, still gets done Over 18% — a fleecing / veto zone

1 · The Superflex QB premium

A QB is worth +15–25% over grid to a team that needs to fill an SF slot. Paying “too much” in raw dollars for a QB you start every week is not an overpay — it’s the tax. That team is you.

2 · Timeline tilt

Contenders pay +10–20% for win-now vets; rebuilders discount any player 30+ by 20–40% and pay up for youth and picks. Price the deal from the other owner’s window, not just yours.

3 · Consolidation premium

The single best player in a deal carries +10–15% — one elite roster spot beats two good ones. It’s why Kupp cost three firsts, not two, and why packaging picks to move up always costs a little extra.

4 · Age & role haircuts

Off the grid: RB over 27 −20%, WR over 30 −15%, QB over 34 −10% (SF softens it). Committee/backup roles sit at the bottom of their tier; locked-in every-week roles at the top.

Worked example — the real Mahomes/Lamar swap
Machines give: Mahomes ($105) + pick 6 ($56) = $161
NWO give: Lamar ($118) + pick 20 ($25) = $143
Machines paid ~$18 (11%) to slide down the pick board but get younger at the game’s premium position. Inside the fair band — a defensible win-now-vs-age bet, not a fleece. This is the QB tax in action.
Worked example — your picks 15 & 16
Your ammo: pick 15 ($34) + pick 16 ($32) = $66
The closed shop: 1.01 ($100) · 1.02 ($90) · 1.03 ($82)
Reality check: 15 + 16 together do not buy a top-3 pick — nobody in this league moves out of the top 3 for a pile of mid-firsts; that seat only opens for a future top-3 or equivalent. What $66 does buy is a jump into the pick 4–5 range ($66 = one pick 4). Use 15+16 to move up for a specific target, or headline your QB package — not to chase the 1.01.
Worked example — buy the QB you need
Target: a mid QB1 (~$68, per the Jimmy G comp)
Your offer: pick 15 ($34) + pick 16 ($32) = $66
Dead-on fair for a QB that fills your weakest slot — and because it’s a QB for a QB-needy team, even adding a future 2nd to seal it is the tax, not a loss. This is exactly what your two firsts are best spent on. Address the hole before the draft empties the QB shelf.
Worked example — sell an aging vet high
Grid says your 30-yo WR2 = $45
Contender pays +15% → ~$52 · rebuilder pays −30% → ~$32
Same player, a $20 swing depending on who you call. Shop fading vets to November contenders — the ledger shows aging names (Keenan, M. Williams, Waller) all cashing out for a future 2nd. Don’t sell yours to a rebuilder for less.

Cheat Sheet — the conversions to keep in your head

Currency

1.01 = $100 · top-3 = $82–100 (a closed shop) · big cliff after pick 3 · mid-1st ≈ $45 · future 1st ≈ $45 · future 2nd ≈ $20.

Quarterbacks (SF)

Elite QB > the 1.01 · a startable QB1 > any pick outside the top 5 · aging starter ≈ a mid-1st · backup ≈ a future 2nd.

Skill players (non-PPR)

WR1/RB1 ≈ a future 1st · WR2 ≈ a first-to-mid-2nd · any fading vet ≈ a future 2nd · TEs discounted hard — only the top 3 clear $40.

The verdict bands

Within 8% = fair, make it · 8–18% = someone’s winning but it’ll get done · over 18% = a fleece / veto zone. Add the QB tax before you call it an overpay.

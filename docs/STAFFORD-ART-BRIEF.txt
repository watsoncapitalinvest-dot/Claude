A wide, high, far-back view of a night football game, shot from the upper deck
of a half-empty stadium. The lit field sits small in the lower third of the
frame, a pale green rectangle floating in a huge mass of black. Twenty-two
players are spread across it, tiny, none of them close together. The ball is in
the air on a short throw toward the near sideline — a single white speck, barely
larger than a grain, caught about fifteen feet above the turf and moving
sideways rather than downfield. Nobody is reaching for it yet.

Most of the picture is darkness: empty upper-deck seating in the foreground,
blurred and unlit, cutting across the bottom corners. Four banks of floodlights
burn at the top edge, flaring slightly, with fine rain drifting through the
beams. The scoreboard is dark. No crowd detail, no faces, no motion blur on the
players — everything is still and quiet, as if the photograph were taken by
somebody who was not sure the play mattered.

Shot on a long lens from a great distance, slight compression, the field in
focus and the near seating falling off soft. Cold colour: blue-black shadow,
sodium-green turf, white light going slightly amber where it flares. Desaturated
and underexposed, pushed film, visible grain in the shadows. The mood is
anticlimax — a huge empty place, a very small object in the air, and no sense at
all that anything is about to be decided.

No text, no lettering, no numbers, no scoreboard digits, no team logos, no
jersey numbers legible, no watermarks, no signage.

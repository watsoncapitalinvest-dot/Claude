A weathered 38-year-old American football quarterback photographed from the side
at field level, filling the left half of the frame from the knees up, caught a
split second after releasing a short throw. His arm has followed through across
his body, his weight is already settling back onto his heels, and his head is
turning away to the right, downfield, with a flat and slightly tired expression —
he is not watching the ball and he is not celebrating. The ball is visible in the
upper right of the frame, close enough to read clearly, travelling flat and low
toward the sideline, maybe twenty feet from his hand.

No defender is near him. No teammate is in the frame. Behind him the stadium is
dark and far out of focus: a blurred wall of mostly empty navy-blue seats and two
hard floodlight flares, with fine rain drifting down through the beams and
catching the light. The turf is wet and reflecting the lights in long streaks.

Close, heavy detail on him — stubble, mud on the jersey and up one forearm, a
worn grey towel at the waist, breath visible in the cold, plain unmarked uniform.
Shot on an 85mm lens wide open, so he is sharp and everything behind him
dissolves. Low camera, slightly below his shoulder line.

Cold desaturated colour: blue-black shadows, pale green turf, the floodlights
going amber where they flare. Underexposed, pushed film stock, heavy grain in the
shadows, deep blacks. Editorial sports photography, the quiet frame a few seconds
after the play rather than the moment of impact.

No text, no lettering, no numbers, no jersey number, no team logo, no scoreboard,
no signage, no watermark.

STAFFORD FEATURE OPENER — one image
1200x800, 3:2 landscape, JPG. File name: scfl-art-stafford.jpg

Copy everything below this line as the prompt.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You are art-directing the opening image for a feature in a fantasy football
league magazine. The magazine is printed-looking and serious — editorial
photography, not illustration, not a poster, no graphic design. Dark, muted,
grainy, film-like.

Here is the story the image opens.

Seven of the week's eight games were already decided by Monday morning. One was
not. A manager needed a huge day from his 38-year-old quarterback, and everyone
in the league knew it, because somebody worked it out in the group chat at 8:23
that morning and said so.

That night the quarterback threw 40 completions out of 55 for 383 yards and
three touchdowns. It brought his side from 25 points back to a point and a half
short with under two minutes left.

What finally won the game was not a touchdown. With 37 seconds on the clock he
completed a routine 16-yard pass to the sideline — no score, no celebration,
nobody in the stadium would remember it — and it was worth a third of a point.
Final margin: 0.72.

While it happened, two rival managers with nothing at stake sat up past eleven
doing decimal arithmetic on somebody else's game. One of them gave up and wrote:
"that math is past what feel like mathing."

The feeling to capture is anticlimax. The biggest thing that happened all week
looked like absolutely nothing while it was happening. Late, cold, quiet,
unglamorous, slightly absurd. Not heroic, not triumphant, no fist-pumping, no
confetti.

You decide what to photograph. It does not have to be the quarterback, or the
stadium, or even football — if a better image for that feeling is somewhere else
entirely, use it.

Requirements: 3:2 landscape. No text, no lettering, no numbers, no team logos,
no scoreboard, no watermark, no signage of any kind.

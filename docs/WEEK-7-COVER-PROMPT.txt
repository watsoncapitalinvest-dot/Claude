SUBJECT

A tug of war on a ruined floodlit American football field at night. Sixteen mascot characters, eight hauling to the left and eight hauling to the right, on one rope that is not moving.

CANVAS AND BANDS

Portrait, 1024 x 1536 pixels, 2:3. The layout matters more than anything else in this prompt, because a masthead goes above the picture and headline type goes below it, and both are added afterwards.

- y 0 to 300 (the top fifth): sky, smoke, floodlight glare. Nothing else. No heads, no rope, no stadium structure, no bright edge that would fight a line of type.
- y 330 to 800: the characters. Every head sits below y 330. Every foot, hand, tail, shoe and dropped prop sits above y 800. Nothing of any character crosses either line.
- y 610: the rope. It runs dead level across the whole width at this height.
- y 800 to 1536 (the bottom two fifths): ground only. Churned mud, heel ruts, smoke, deep shadow. No characters, no rope, no hands, no feet, no bright highlights, no focal detail. This is the largest area in the picture and it must still look deliberate once it has been darkened and had type laid over it.

THE ROPE

There is exactly one rope. One. It runs straight across the frame from the left edge to the right edge at y 610, perfectly horizontal, thick and muddy and sagging slightly under its own weight. One red cloth rag is tied at its centre and hangs directly over the white centre line painted on the field. No second rope anywhere, no rope in the foreground, no rope behind.

Nobody is winning. Every character is at full strain: heels dug in, bodies leaning back at a hard angle away from the centre, arms locked straight, shoulders down, teeth bared, faces working. The rope has not moved an inch.

THE GROUND

The field is destroyed. Under the rag and running away from it toward the camera, the turf is a churned black mud wallow scored with heel ruts dragged back and forth across the centre line many times over, so it reads immediately that this rope has gone one way and then the other all night and finished exactly where it started. The white centre line survives in broken fragments through the mud. Shallow water lies in the deeper ruts and catches the floodlight.

THE CAST

Sixteen characters, numbered 1 to 16 reading left to right across the frame. Numbers 1 to 8 face right and haul to the left. Numbers 9 to 16 face left and haul to the right. The rag at the centre of the rope sits between number 8 and number 9. Every one of the sixteen is on the rope with both hands.

All sixteen are anthropomorphic mascots: whole bodies standing on two legs, built like costumed sports mascots, with oversized heads, big hands, big feet and a silhouette you could recognise in black. None of them is a crest, a badge, a logo, an emblem or a floating head. They are original characters invented for this picture.

1. A battle-damaged humanoid robot. Hulking, battered black leather jacket, dark glasses, half its rubbery face torn away to bare a polished chrome skull with one glowing red lens underneath. Pulling with no visible effort and no expression whatever.
2. A giant beaver. Oversized head, two huge orange buck teeth bared, thick brown fur with a tan chest, flat paddle tail braced behind it like a third leg.
3. A cocky long-haul trucker. Square jaw, tousled dark hair, white sleeveless tank top, blue jeans, heavy work boots, big belt buckle, grinning like he has already won this.
4. A small round pink pig in bib overalls and a backwards baseball cap, scowling, heels ploughing the mud. Clearly the smallest character in the picture.
5. A carthorse on two legs. Long face, big square teeth, an oversized padded leather harness collar round its neck, a fat cigar clamped in its teeth and smoke curling up past one eye.
6. A lean gothic figure in a tall black top hat and a long black tailcoat worn open over a cream-white suit and cream spats. Pale grinning face under the brim, spindly limbs, the only elegant thing on the rope.
7. A massive blue bull. Royal-to-navy hide, white horns, steam blasting from its nostrils, shoulders lowered, the broadest silhouette on its side.
8. Two face-painted strongmen hauling together, one just behind the other. Bare-chested, studded black leather, spiked shoulder pads, broad bands of black and white war paint across their eyes. These two together are number 8, one of the sixteen, not two of them.
9. A huge deeply tanned showman-wrestler. Bald on top with long bleached-blond hair to his shoulders at the sides, bleached horseshoe moustache, black bandana over his head, dark wraparound sunglasses, enormous arms. Black and white head to foot: black sleeveless shirt with white trim, black and white boots.
10. A shaggy brown-furred ape-like beast, taller than anyone else in the picture, a wide leather strap across its chest, head thrown back roaring, a torn page still crushed in one fist.
11. A green dragon. Big head, scaled hide, smoke curling from its nostrils, wings half-folded for leverage, barbed tail braced flat against the ground.
12. A scowling man in a green, white and red striped football shirt. Clean-shaven, dark cropped hair, no moustache, no chain, no cigar, no hat, both hands wrapped in the rope and a thoroughly disgusted expression.
13. A thick-set hairy-chested wiseguy. Open-collar white shirt, gold chain, thick black moustache, slicked hair, a cigar clamped in the corner of his mouth.
14. A heavyset moustached man in a plain teal and orange sleeveless football jersey, stretched tight over his gut and his chest so his nipples show through the fabric. Big round head, thick black moustache, white football trousers, face scarlet with effort. He is unmissable and he is at the near end of the right-hand team.
15. A leering carnival clown. White face, red nose, orange hair, oversized teeth, white neck ruff, red and white striped sleeves, enormous shoes sunk in the mud.
16. A hooded warrior-monk in rough brown robes, hood up, face in shadow, a plain sword hilt at his belt throwing a faint glow, both hands on the rope.

ARRANGEMENT

Number 1 and number 16 are nearest the camera and the largest, and may be cropped by the left and right edges of the frame. The characters get smaller and more overlapped toward the centre, so each team reads as a line receding into the middle of the picture. No character is fully hidden behind another: every one of the sixteen has its head and at least one hand clearly visible.

No two of them look alike. In particular the two moustached men, number 13 and number 14, must read as plainly different people — different build, different clothing, different colouring — and number 12 has no moustache at all.

LIGHT AND PALETTE

Night. Two banks of stadium floodlights behind and above, burning through drifting olive and amber smoke as hard white flares with long god-rays. Blurred empty stands far back, nearly lost in the haze. Every character rim-lit cold white from behind and filled with warm amber bouncing up off the mud. Palette: near-black, deep red, gold, warm amber, olive-grey smoke, cream.

STYLE

Painted, not photographic and not a 3D render. Opaque acrylic and airbrush, the way action-adventure paperback covers and arcade cabinet panels were painted in the mid-1980s: visible brush and airbrush modelling, deep saturated colour, firm dark linework holding the figures, hard cel-like shadow edges, a faint print halftone texture over the whole surface. Heroic exaggerated proportions. It should look hand-painted in 1985 and printed slightly out of register.

HARD CONSTRAINTS

Count the characters before you finish. Reading left to right there are sixteen numbered characters and seventeen bodies, because number 8 is a pair. Every single one listed above appears. None may be omitted, merged or substituted.

No real sports team crests, no league marks, no club badges, no real-world logos of any kind on any jersey, jacket or piece of equipment.

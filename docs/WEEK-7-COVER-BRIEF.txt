WHAT THE THREE EARLIER RENDERS TAUGHT US

Render 1 drew two ropes — one across the middle and a second across the
foreground with its own red rag — and hung the two nearest characters off the
bottom edge, which is the band the headline sits in. Fixed by saying there is
exactly one rope, in those words, and by giving the bands as pixel rows rather
than as percentages.

Render 2 had all sixteen but sat them low, so "Thirty-Seven Lead Changes" ran
across the trucker's chest and the pig's head. Fixed by naming the exact rows:
heads below y 330, nothing below y 800.

Render 3 was the best picture of the three — masthead on clean smoke, no type
over a face, a genuinely empty mud field — and it had fifteen teams on the
rope. Phins Phag was simply absent, and at thumbnail size that is invisible.
It took cropping both halves of the art and enlarging them to catch it. On a
cover whose whole premise is that all sixteen are on this rope, leaving one
manager off is the only mistake in this job that really matters.


WHAT CHANGED IN THE PROMPT BECAUSE OF THAT

- The cast is numbered 1 to 16 straight across the frame instead of two lists
  of eight with their own numbering. One continuous roster is easier to satisfy
  and, more to the point, easier to verify: count left to right and the last
  one is sixteen.
- The strongman pair is called out as number 8, "one of the sixteen, not two of
  them", so the count has an unambiguous right answer — sixteen numbers,
  seventeen bodies.
- Phins Phag is number 14 and is described as unmissable, because he is the one
  that went missing.
- Every band is given as a pixel row for a 1024x1536 canvas. "The top 20 per
  cent" is a thing a model has to work out; "y 0 to 300" is a thing it can just
  do.
- The style line asks for mid-1980s acrylic and airbrush paperback and arcade
  art rather than naming a toy line. Render 3 came out painted and looked far
  better for it than render 2's clean modern cartoon.
- Nothing in the prompt is a trademark. The first version was refused outright
  for third-party content; a film character and a real wrestler are in there by
  appearance only and must never be named.

FIXES TO PASTE WHEN A RENDER COMES BACK WRONG

Check the full-size file before accepting anything. Crop each half of the
figure band, enlarge it, and count.

A MASCOT IS MISSING —

Everything in this picture stays exactly as it is. One character is missing and
has to be added. Add <the description, copied from the prompt> at the near end
of the <left/right>-hand team, both hands on the rope and feet dug in. That
makes sixteen characters on the rope. Change nothing else: same painting, same
lighting, same mud, same rope, same rag, everyone else in exactly the position
they are in now.

TWO CHARACTERS LOOK LIKE EACH OTHER —

Number 12 and number 13 are different men. Number 13 has a thick black
moustache, a gold chain and a cigar. Number 12 has none of those: he is
clean-shaven, wears a green, white and red striped shirt and nothing else
distinguishing, and he is scowling.

IT DRAWS CRESTS, BADGES OR FLOATING HEADS —

Every one of the sixteen is a whole body standing on two legs with both arms
pulling a rope, drawn the way a costumed sports mascot stands on a sideline.
None of them is a badge, a logo, an emblem or a head.

A SECOND ROPE APPEARS, OR THE ROPE SLOPES —

There is exactly one rope in this picture and it is perfectly horizontal.
Delete any second rope and any second rag. The one rag sits at the centre of
the one rope, directly over the centre line. Neither side has gained ground.

CHARACTERS HANG OFF THE BOTTOM EDGE —

No part of any character — feet, hands, tails, shoes, props — falls below y 800
on the 1536-tall canvas. Nothing is cropped by the bottom edge. Only the two
nearest characters at the far left and far right may be cropped, and only by
the left and right edges.

IT IS REFUSED FOR THIRD-PARTY CONTENT —

Strip these three in order, re-running after each, until it goes through:
1. Number 1 becomes "A hulking humanoid robot in a long dark coat, its head a
   plain polished steel skull with one glowing red lens, no face."
2. Number 16 loses the glow: "a plain sword slung across his back."
3. Number 10 loses the chest strap.


WHEN THE FILE COMES BACK

Save the art somewhere it will survive the container, then:

    python3 scripts/build_week_cover.py --week 7 --art art/week-7-tug-of-war.jpg
    python3 scripts/build_week_issue.py --week 7

Two settings in week-data/2026-w7-written.json are tuned to the art and want
checking against a new one:

- cover.artDrop. Set to 0 for art that already leaves its own top fifth clear,
  which this brief asks for. The 125 the other weeks use exists to push art
  down clear of the masthead, and on art like this it only shoves faces into
  the headline.
- cover.cardFocus. The share card is a 1200x630 crop, about a third of the
  height of the art, taken around this point, and it wants to land on the rope.
  The figure band this brief asks for puts that at roughly 0.38; render 2,
  whose characters sat lower, wanted 0.50. Build it and look at it — at 0.30
  the card came out as empty stadium with sixteen chins along the bottom edge.

The cover and the share card are built from the same file by the same command,
and that is deliberate: the week they disagreed, the link previewed as a chart
and opened on a stadium.

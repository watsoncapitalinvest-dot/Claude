Kyler Is Worth A First

A starting quarterback has cost a first-round pick in this league seventeen times. He is one, on a 5-0 team that traded away its replacement — and this desk originally priced him off a national chart and called him a sell.

The SCFL NewsRoom · The Trade Desk
What this desk had wrong

It priced Kyler Murray at $38 off a national dynasty chart and concluded there was no market, in a league whose own price guide says in plain words: "Below the top-3 picks, quarterbacks are the priciest assets in the league." That line was quoted in another note on this site a few hours earlier and then ignored.

It read the scarcity backwards. Every team carrying three quarterbacks with nothing startable on waivers is not what kills the market for a quarterback. It is why one costs a first here.

And it did not know that Minnesota had traded J.J. McCarthy, which is the fact the whole 2027 case turns on.

What a quarterback actually costs here

Thirty-two trades in this league's recorded history sent a starting-calibre quarterback one way. Seventeen of them — 53 per cent — sent back at least one first-round pick, twenty-seven firsts in all. That is the market rate, and it is not close to a national chart's opinion.

Baker Mayfield cost two firsts. That is the floor this conversation starts from.
He has the job, and the replacement has been traded

Minnesota named him the Week 1 starter out of camp over J.J. McCarthy, the first-round quarterback they drafted in 2024. They then traded McCarthy to the New York Giants for a 2027 fifth-round pick — nflverse's player table now lists him in New York, and he has not thrown a pass anywhere this season. What is behind Kyler on that depth chart is Carson Wentz, who is 33.

And Minnesota are 5-0: 39–22 over Green Bay, 9–3 at Chicago, 23–16 at Tampa, 15–10 over Miami, 23–13 at New Orleans. Nobody takes the ball off a quarterback on a 5-0 team.

Next year is the case, not the problem

He signed a one-year deal worth the minimum in March after Arizona released him, and per reporting it carries a clause barring Minnesota from using the franchise tag. So he is not under contract for 2027 and they have no mechanism to hold him.

Read that the obvious way and it sounds like a reason to sell. It is the opposite. Having traded McCarthy, Minnesota has nobody to replace him with, and at 5-0 they will not draft high enough to find one — so the choice in March is to pay him, reportedly in the region of $40 million a year, or start from nothing. Either he is paid starter money by the team he just won for, or he reaches an open market as a starting quarterback off a winning season. Both of those are a price being set in public, and both are above where he sits today.

What the trade actually costs this roster

Almost nothing, which is the part that makes a first so attractive. He is giving 10.4 a game. The superflex seat behind him would be filled by a bench running back, and a started running back in this league averages 10.3. Jameis Winston slides up to the quarterback slot, where he put 25.2 on the board in week five. The lineup barely notices; the return is a first-round pick.

The desk's position

He is worth a first. Not a second, which is what this desk suggested asking Wookie Leaks for, and which was wrong by an order of magnitude on the league's own trade record.

Do not sell him this week. The floor is a starting quarterback, the ceiling is a 5-0 team with no succession plan and a contract being priced in March, and the asking price has nowhere to go but up while both hold.

He turns 30 next August, so he is still not an asset a rebuilding roster keeps. The windows are after a loud game later this season, or the week a 2027 contract is signed. Wookie Leaks remain the buyer with the least room to say no: Brissett, and then two men who have thrown one pass between them all year.

The thing that breaks all of it is his health. He has been concussed once already this season and has finished a full year twice since 2019.

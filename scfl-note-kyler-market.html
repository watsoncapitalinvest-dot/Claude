There Is No Market For Kyler

All sixteen teams already carry three quarterbacks, and not one startable quarterback in the NFL is unrostered. This desk called him a sell without checking either number.

This desk treated Kyler Murray as something to be sold without once asking who was buying. That was the error, and it is worth setting out properly, because the answer is structural rather than a matter of opinion about the player.

Nobody has a hole

Superflex with nineteen roster places means everybody hoards quarterbacks, and everybody has. Every one of the sixteen teams in this league is carrying at least three; Pork Chop Express carries four. Fifty-four quarterbacks are rostered. And of every passer in the NFL with sixty or more attempts this season, the number nobody in this league owns is zero.

There is not one startable quarterback on the waiver wire. Not one.

That cuts both ways and the second way is the one that matters. A seller needs a buyer with a need, and no team has a need. But it also means that if this roster trades Kyler it cannot replace him — what is left is Jameis Winston at 11.4 a game and Drew Allar, who has not taken an NFL snap. In a format that starts two quarterbacks, selling the second one into an empty wire is not a trade, it is a hole.

And the price is at its floor

The DynastyProcess snapshots run 115, 100, 66, 80, 72 and now 38. He has lost 47 per cent of his value in twelve months and two thirds of it since 2021. He is 29, so there is no youth argument underneath it either. Anybody who looks him up sees an asset in free fall, which is exactly the moment a seller has no leverage.

Except he just got his job back

Three starts, 10.4 a game, and the best of them last week. That is still under the 14.9 a started quarterback averages in this league, and it does not beat what a buyer already owns — Brissett pays 13.7, Geno Smith 12.9, Bo Nix 12.7. But it is a starting job on a real offence, going the right way, in the same month the price print went the wrong way.

The one team that would actually want him

Wookie Leaks are the market. One functional quarterback and then two men who have thrown one pass between them all season, in a format that demands two. That is a need, not a flyer, and a need is the only thing that gets paid in this league.

What the desk would actually do

Not sell at 38 into no demand in the week he reclaimed a starting job and posted his best game. That is selling the bottom twice over.

Open with Wookie Leaks, because they are the one team whose alternative is starting nobody. Ask for the 2027 second, not a player — they have no quarterback to send back.

Otherwise hold. In a format where every room is three deep and the wire is empty, the market for a quarterback does not exist until somebody's starter goes down. There are nine weeks left for that to happen, and it will happen to somebody.

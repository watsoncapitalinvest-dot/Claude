The Warren File

The question was whether Jaylen Warren is worth seven dollars or seventy. Seven. The August snapshot has him at $7 on the scale where the 1.01 is $100, and the five years of snapshots behind it tell the story of why: nothing in 2022, $2 in 2023, a peak of $12 in 2024, back to $3 in 2025, and $7 now. He has been bouncing around the same small number for half a decade.

The case for keeping him

It is not nothing. His share of Pittsburgh's passing work has climbed almost every week of this season — 15 per cent, 10, 12, 17, 19 — and 27 targets through five weeks for 230 yards is a genuine receiving back's usage, not a change-of-pace back's. In a non-PPR league that is worth less than it looks, but it is real, and it is the best his role has been.

The case against

He turns 28 on the first of November. This roster is rebuilding for next year and does not need wins this one, which makes a 27-year-old running back with a $7 price the single least useful kind of asset to be holding: too old to appreciate, too cheap to headline a deal, and good enough right now that somebody else will want him.

What he can actually fetch

The league's own record answers this better than any chart. Twelve trades have sent a back of roughly Warren's description out as the main piece. Eight of them brought back a second-round pick, two brought back a first, and the rest brought back a player and no pick at all. So the honest range is a second, with a first at the top of it if somebody is desperate — and a real chance of nothing but a body.

The standing instruction

Rebuilding for next year. Wins this season are not the point, so age is the first filter on anything coming back and the first argument for anything going out.

How this was worked out. Age is computed from the birth date in nflverse's players.csv, which is the NFL's own player table — not from a name search, which on an earlier pass in this league returned fathers instead of sons. Target share is counted out of play-by-play. What he is worth is read off the league's own 256-trade record rather than inferred from a national value chart, because inferring it that way is how this desk mispriced a quarterback by a full round.

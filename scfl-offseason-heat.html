What It Means

Eight months between championships and the heat rate barely moved: 3.5 per cent of offseason volleys carried an argument word, against 3.67 per cent across the entire five-year chat record. The offseason is not the calm season. It just argues about different things — waivers, schedules, whether the league is old enough to matter — instead of a missed start or a bad beat.

The single hottest week of the whole window was August 17–23, and it is not a coincidence: that is the week this desk published the Heat Index, the Addendum, and the Gumbas file. The league spent a night arguing about being measured, which produced more heat than most weeks produce arguing about football. Being told who argues the most made everybody argue about it.

The week that says more is the second-hottest one, March 23–29 — five months before any of that existed. A sustained, multi-hour exchange, openly called “an altercation” by one of the managers watching it happen, pulled in half the league. The record was going to have a hot week regardless of whether anyone was keeping score.

By raw heated exchanges, no pairing ran hotter than The Machines and Pork Chop Express, with 28 over the window. Meanwhile the reigning, five-time champion managed exactly 4 heated exchanges in eight months — the quietest record of anyone who showed up regularly. Winning, it turns out, is a pretty effective way to stay out of an argument.
